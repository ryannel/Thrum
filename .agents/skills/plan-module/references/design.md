# The design

One design governs each module. It's what the plan, the done-when file and the evaluator check against, so keep it reading as
what is true now: revise sections in place rather than appending amendments, and keep history to git and a brief
note of superseded decisions. Keep it as short as the module allows; detail can live in linked research notes.
[Research and design](blue-sky.md) explains how to arrive at it.

A design for a substantial module usually covers:

- **Purpose and scope:** what it's for, its consumers, what's commissioned now and what's deferred.
- **Cues:** what the player must see or feel, each with its operating range and where it must read (camera, distance,
  speed), and the shipped-game bar it's judged against, stated in words.
- **Techniques and evidence:** the chosen mechanisms, the alternatives considered, what shipped engines and research
  show, and the check that each mechanism makes its cues across the range. Say what would change each decision.
- **The system as it runs:** state and owners, representations, update order, CPU or GPU placement, handoffs and
  contracts with neighbours, the creator journey, and a normal frame walked in order with its cost.
- **Risks:** the most likely ways this fails, the early sign of each, and the plausible wrong substitute for each
  defining cue (what could look right while missing the behaviour).
- **Performance:** the cost account, tripwires and how cost is measured ([performance](performance.md)).
- **Open questions:** what is still undecided, and the experiment or milestone that will settle it.
