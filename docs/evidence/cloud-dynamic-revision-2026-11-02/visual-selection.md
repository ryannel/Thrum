# Cloud visual selection — 2 November 2026

**Decision status: selected visual target and provisional default technique; no Thrum appearance or device-cost proof.** This is an initial selection before reconciling the current cloud implementation or migration plan. I had already encountered the retained cloud studies, so this is not a blind study; the judgment below rests on the linked first-hand reviews and primary technical disclosures. No renderer was run, large source was downloaded, or code imported.

## What the visual evidence selects

Use *Horizon Forbidden West: Burning Shores* as the leading **close-cloud visual comparator**, especially three-dimensional silhouette, fly-through continuity, cloud self-shadow, low-Sun edge light and cloud/terrain integration. This is a defensible target, not a claim of universal “best clouds.” [Digital Foundry's own 27 April 2023 review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YGZUM_s0McU) calls the PS5 presentation a visual masterclass; independent first-hand [Digitec](https://www.digitec.ch/en/page/horizon-forbidden-west-burning-shores-review-the-post-apocalyptic-world-has-never-been-so-beautiful-27434) and [RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review-dlc/horizon-forbidden-west-burning-shores/) reviews specifically credit its new clouds and close flight for the visual improvement. The DF video page's title/description were verified; its full spoken analysis was **not** transcribed in this check. Digitec/RPGFan article text was inspected directly. The reviewers assess the **shipped composite image**, not which individual algorithm caused it or whether its clouds are meteorologically evolving.

[Guerrilla's 2023 Nubis³ disclosure](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-cubed), [technical slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/Nubis%20Cubed%20%28Advances%202023%29.pdf), and [developer interview](https://blog.playstation.com/2023/03/29/pushing-the-envelope-achieving-next-level-clouds-in-horizon-forbidden-west-burning-shores/) explain the change from earlier 2.5D layer instructions to **modeled coarse 3D voxel clouds**, with compression, detail up-resolution, conservative/SDF traversal and accelerated lighting for ground-to-air viewing. The slides report approximately 2.2–4 ms in cited console scenes after optimizations; this is **not** a WebGPU/M2 prediction or an equal-quality comparison with a procedural source. Guerrilla's art direction, content preparation, lighting and temporal choices jointly contribute to the reviewed image. Its public material does **not** disclose a general live birth/growth/breakup mechanism; the retained slide study records the final comparison's “pseudomotion” label. Do not call an advected weather map “Nubis³ equivalent.”

## Default technique and source boundary

**Select a sparse, world-space 3D optical-density field rendered as a volume**, with coarse class-specific morphology, bounded procedural detail at the ray sample, conservative occupied-region skipping, shared cloud optical-depth/lighting products, low or mixed-resolution view marching and independently valid temporal reconstruction. The **default source** is a native, weather-driven procedural field producer that can regenerate dirty 3D regions as cloud structure changes; it is a proposed Thrum extension, **not a demonstrated Nubis³ feature**. The renderer consumes time-stamped extinction and optical coefficients plus conservative bounds, independent of how those fields were made. An imported voxel/VDB or artist-authored shape provider can replace or augment the native producer for hero formations, but no Houdini license, paid offline application, baked full weather episode or external service is a runtime or authoring prerequisite. Open tools, direct procedural recipes and optional imported assets are admissible inputs under the same source contract.

The native producer should maintain persistent cloud identities and a low-frequency 3D condensate/occupancy state per active region. Weather supplies advection by altitude-dependent wind and spatial lift/moisture/shear; local source and loss terms change the field's *support* (new towers, openings, anvil spread, fragmentation), rather than only scroll detail or fade a fixed volume. Genus-specific generators supply initial shapes, vertical envelopes and development rules: convective towers/anvils for Cu/Cb, shallow connected cells/sheets for Sc/St, and sheared high filaments for Ci. A bounded multiscale detail field conditions edges without owning lifecycle. This is an authored transport/source–loss approximation, not a claimed resolved atmosphere or a proven visual recipe. The producer interface must permit a stronger physical simulation or prepared sequence later without changing views, shadows or atmospheric consumers.

The first visual gate is therefore **hard**: show ordinary and accelerated Cu birth → tower/anvil → breakup, Sc/St openings and Ci shear from the native producer, from ground and close flight, with matching cloud shadows and reflected/environment views. Compare silhouettes, interior depth, motion, stability and sunset light with the Burning Shores target and real cloud references. If native morphology produces smooth inflating blobs, repeated noise, static topology or temporal ghosts, it fails the selected default; improve the class producer or use optional prepared 3D morphology where needed. A pleasing still or a moving weather map alone cannot pass.

## Why this, and what performance evidence can say

| Candidate | Visual and dynamical fit | Cost/availability judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Selected: dynamic sparse 3D source + Nubis³-like rendering economy** | 3D near-view structure matches the praised category; source can actually change support. Quality of this *combination* remains unproved. | Preparation is amortized across view/light consumers; occupancy skips and mixed resolution can cut samples. Dirty 3D updates and cache invalidation add work. Must measure complete scene, source updates and upload peaks on WebGPU/M2 and native. |
| **Full prepared 3D voxel animation** | Can encode excellent morphology and topology changes, with [Smolder 2026](https://www.realtimerendering.com/advances/s2026/content/AlexanderMueller_Smolder_Siggraph26.pdf) showing sparse packed offline volume animation. | Strong optional reference/hero provider, but clip coverage, source cadence, whole-animation residency and authoring burden are substantial; Smolder notes identify memory and cloud-lighting limits. It does not remove the need for live weather placement/response. |
| **Runtime 2.5D weather map + vertical profile/noise** | [Nubis 2017](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-authoring-real-time-volumetric-cloudscapes-with-the-decima-engine) and [Nubis Evolved 2022](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-evolved) establish excellent distant sky and art direction; 2017 explicitly targeted detail evolution rather than individual formation evolution. | Lower source residency and likely cheaper updates for distant layers, but **no comparable-quality close-fly-through proof** against Burning Shores. Keep as far-sky tier, not the visual-default substitute. |
| **UE 5.8 material-driven volume** | [Epic's current documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/volumetric-cloud-component-in-unreal-engine) proves a maintained dynamic-time-of-day, cloud-shadow and ground-to-flight production route with conservative density evaluation. | Useful system comparison, not a recipe for evolving morphology; public documentation does not establish equal quality/cost for Thrum. UE source/license is not assumed importable. |

At comparable *measured* image quality, prefer the lower total cost across the main view, reflection/environment products, shadow/air/fog joins, source regeneration, upload and memory peaks. No source reviewed supplies that controlled comparison on the target devices. [Epic's UE 5.8 documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/volumetric-cloud-component-in-unreal-engine) reports quarter-resolution tracing/half-resolution reconstruction in one quality mode and a conservative-density branch, while also warning that fully tracing clouds for reflections is expensive; these are transferable scheduling ideas, not Thrum timings. The native procedural source may outperform stored clips when many unique evolving events would otherwise consume memory or IO; prepared fields may outperform repeated procedural evaluation when appearance is reused across rays and views. Either claim depends on source complexity, change rate, compression support, cache hit rate and temporal error.

Recent challengers do not displace this bounded default: [2026 neural cloud shading](https://fileadmin.cs.lth.se/graphics/research/papers/2026/environmental_volumetric_neural_shading_of_clouds_for_real_time_rendering/) shades a prepared asset rather than generating weather lifecycle, and its [public code](https://github.com/LUGGPublic/evns-clouds-rtr) has an NVIDIA cooperative-vector path and no located repository license; [2026 meteorological WebGPU rendering](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2026/muth-2026-clouds/) addresses measured weather volumes but its cited large atlas/desktop GPU scope does not prove this browser budget or close-cloud art quality. The [MIT Takram reference](https://github.com/takram-design-engineering/three-geospatial) is inspectable weather-first ray marching/temporal/shadow code, but is WebGL-based and documents reconstruction artifacts; it is an architectural example, not the selected implementation. Public Guerrilla slides and Epic documentation explain methods but grant no general source-copy right. No source-code import is authorized by this selection.

**Rejection rule:** if the dynamic 3D source cannot approach the selected close-cloud cues within the assembled frame/memory targets after shared-cache, occupancy and sample-count reductions, retain the 3D visual target and revise the producer/quality tiers with measured evidence. Do not declare a 2.5D substitute “equal quality” from isolated stills or a cheaper ray pass.
