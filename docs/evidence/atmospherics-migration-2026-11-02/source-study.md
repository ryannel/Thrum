## Initial neutral selection

For a dynamic, art-directed clear atmosphere and aerial perspective, select Hillaire's 2020 compact-LUT family as the base: atmosphere-profile transmittance, approximate multiple-scattering closure, camera/sun-dependent sky view and frustum aerial perspective. Treat local fog as a separate spatially varying medium integrated over view segments, with exactly one ordered composition of its radiance/transmittance with the clear air; only use a shared froxel volume when its light/shadow/detail requirements and measured cost justify it. Preserve Bruneton's 2017 implementation as an independent math/reference oracle, not the default runtime representation. This is a research recommendation, not an implementation or performance certification.

Basis inspected before selection: [Hillaire's EGSR paper](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf), especially §§3–5 and 7; [Hillaire's author code](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere/blob/master/Resources/RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl), `IntegrateScatteredLuminance` and aerial-perspective functions; [Bruneton's 2017 documented implementation](https://ebruneton.github.io/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/index.html); and [Bevy's open unified-volumetrics proposal](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/issues/18151). The combined local-fog boundary is our deduction, not a published single-system result.

Further source inspection, exact revision pins, causal joins, budget account, alternatives, and gaps follow below.

## Decision and evidence boundary

**Recommendation (research-selected, unqualified on target devices):** one clear-air atmosphere profile drives transmittance, Hillaire-style higher-order scattering, sky, aerial perspective, sunlight tint, and the sky radiance used for ambient/reflection lighting. A weather/local-media provider supplies spatially varying fog extinction/scattering separately. The final view integration treats both media over any overlapping foreground interval, then composes the clear-air tail. Cloud extinction and shadows enter the sun/sky lighting contracts, with one owner for each attenuation. Keep a direct raymarch path for high-frequency terrain/cloud shadows and space/limb views. This preserves appearance and weather response while avoiding a permanent full-resolution march or an all-scene froxel grid.

**What is published:** Hillaire [EGSR 2020, §§3–7](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf) gives the four compact LUTs, closure and measured reference-platform timings. Bruneton [2017 documentation](https://ebruneton.github.io/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/index.html) gives a tested iterative reference and explicit sky/point/irradiance functions. **Our deductions:** dirty dependency tracking, a shared scene-lighting contract, local-fog/clear-air interval composition, and proposed quality reductions. **Unknown:** target WebGPU/Metal performance, ghosting, browser residency, cloud/fog crossover appearance, and whether Hillaire's isotropic closure is acceptable for chosen art profiles. Neither publicly accessible code nor another engine's result proves those.

## What the source actually teaches

### Hillaire's complete clear-air path

The medium is altitude-profiled Rayleigh scattering, Mie scattering/absorption, and ozone absorption over spherical ground/top boundaries; `σ_t=σ_s+σ_a`, `T(a,b)=exp(-∫σ_t ds)`. The paper's equations 1–4 and Table 1 disclose the terms and default Earth coefficients. Its later equations 5–11 and [author shader `IntegrateScatteredLuminance`, starting line 24](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere/blob/183ead5bdacc701b3b626347a680a2f3cd3d4fbd/Resources/RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl#L24) show the actual order: clip view ray to ground/top/depth, sample medium, get sun transmittance, planet/optional scene shadow, add direct single scattering and multi-scatter source, then integrate the homogeneous step analytically `ΔL=T_prefix·j·(1-exp(-σ_tΔs))/σ_t`. This segment formula needs the `σ_t→0` limit `jΔs`; simply dividing by zero is invalid.

The [transmittance pass `RenderTransmittanceLutPS`, starting line 547](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere/blob/183ead5bdacc701b3b626347a680a2f3cd3d4fbd/Resources/RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl#L547) maps radius and zenith to 256×64 and integrates 40 steps in the paper setup. It has no camera or sun dependency once the global medium/planet profile is fixed. The 2D multiple-scattering LUT maps altitude and sun-zenith cosine. [`NewMultiScattCS`, starting line 418](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere/blob/183ead5bdacc701b3b626347a680a2f3cd3d4fbd/Resources/RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl#L418) launches 64 angular directions per texel, 20 view steps each, reduces in group memory, then stores `Ψ_ms=L_2/(1-f_ms)`. The exact assumptions matter: second and later scattering is isotropic; a neighborhood receives approximately uniform second-order light; `f_ms<1`; local scene shadowing is omitted from the closure. The paper documents hue drift for very dense, high-anisotropy media (Fig. 12). Therefore this LUT must never be read as a local fog or cloud multiple-scattering solution.

[Sky view `SkyViewLutPS`, starting line 581](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere/blob/183ead5bdacc701b3b626347a680a2f3cd3d4fbd/Resources/RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl#L581) rebuilds a low-resolution directional sky from camera altitude, sun direction, transmittance and multi-scatter. The paper's §5.3 warps latitude toward the horizon and composites the sun disk separately. [Aerial `RenderCameraVolumePS`, starting line 645](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere/blob/183ead5bdacc701b3b626347a680a2f3cd3d4fbd/Resources/RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl#L645) computes a frustum 3D cumulative segment field at slice depths; [`RenderRayMarchingPS`, starting line 290](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere/blob/183ead5bdacc701b3b626347a680a2f3cd3d4fbd/Resources/RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl#L290) chooses sky LUT on empty depth, aerial LUT for opaque depth, or direct march. Author code stores mean RGB transmittance as alpha in the fast aerial path, and explicitly disables the colored-transmittance variant there. The paper §5.4 also says aerial RGB plus mean-transmittance alpha. This is a visible approximation at sunset; exact RGB extinction requires a second RGB field, a per-pixel analytic segment transmittance, or a validated equivalent. The 3D aerial LUT is a **clear-air cumulative image**, not a general local-fog density grid even though both use frustum coordinates.

Paper §7 reports 1280×720 NVIDIA 1080 total 0.31 ms, with table values 0.01 ms transmittance, 0.07 ms multiple scattering, 0.05 ms sky view, 0.04 ms aerial and 0.14 ms composite; its 2016 iPhone 6s case is about 1 ms with smaller sky/aerial tables. These are reported historical platforms and scenes, not target-device predictions. Space/limb views use direct ray marching, and high-frequency terrain/cloud volumetric shadows need jittered ray marching and reprojection; the author explicitly says a low-frequency sky LUT cannot represent those details.

### Bruneton as a reference, not the dynamic default

Bruneton's [`Model::Precompute` in `model.cc`, lines 1048 onward](https://github.com/ebruneton/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/blob/34f14e745cff948f4ca3157d1b62a445ffa7286f/atmosphere/model.cc#L1048) sequences transmittance, direct irradiance, single Rayleigh/Mie scattering, then scattering-density, indirect-irradiance and multiple-scattering passes for each order; `Init` at lines 866 onward allocates per-order temporary textures. [`functions.glsl` `ComputeScatteringDensity` (1163), `ComputeMultipleScattering` (1285), `GetSkyRadiance` (1705), `GetSkyRadianceToPoint` (1787), `GetSunAndSkyIrradiance` (1878)](https://github.com/ebruneton/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/blob/34f14e745cff948f4ca3157d1b62a445ffa7286f/atmosphere/functions.glsl) are especially useful to check sky, depth-limited aerial perspective, RGB sun transmittance, and direct versus indirect surface irradiance. The author's documentation describes dimension checking, unit tests, CPU spectral reference and ozone/density-profile support. Its 4D scattering table, per-order recomputation and texture-coordinate/difference hazards make it a poor default when weather/art changes the global air profile often. The 250 ms update cited by Hillaire §7 is a measurement of his particular Bruneton comparison implementation/configuration, not a universal bound. Preserve the reference formulation as an independent image/math oracle.

## Operating model and causal joins (our deduction unless cited)

1. **Authoritative inputs.** An atmosphere profile owns planet radius/top, altitude density functions and per-channel Rayleigh/Mie/ozone coefficients, phase `g`, ground albedo and artistic scale. A light/weather owner supplies sun/moon direction and scene-linear illuminance, cloud optical depth/shadow at a query point and time, weather-driven global aerosol changes, and local fog volume density/material fields. Camera/exposure are view inputs, not atmosphere material state. Distinguish physical profile changes from exposure/color grading, so LUTs do not rebuild for display-only changes. Coordinate units are meters for world distances; convert to the LUT's kilometers once at its boundary. Record planet origin/up and linear RGB space.
2. **Frame resources/validity.** On profile or planet change, refresh transmittance then multi-scatter; on sun direction or camera altitude (or upstream LUT change), refresh sky-view; on view matrix/projection/depth-range, sun, or upstream LUT change, refresh aerial. Per-view resources may share profile-only LUTs across cameras if profile/planet identity and device lifetime match; never share camera-dependent tables accidentally. Recreate on resize/device loss. For abrupt weather edits, publish a coherent generation of all dependent tables together or use a documented old generation until complete; mixing new transmittance with old multi-scatter/sky gives a false horizon. Slow changes may amortize work only with bounded, visible latency to all consumers.
3. **Clear sky and aerial.** The clear-air integrator evaluates `T_a(0,d)` and cumulative `L_a(0,d)`, with the sun disk filtered by `T_a`. A sky LUT samples distant air; AP samples finite-depth air. The clear-air source includes direct sun scattering and Hillaire's approximate higher-order term. No local fog density, cloud density or local shadow can be inserted into the 2D multi-scatter LUT without invalidating its altitude-only symmetry. A shadowed local aerial path is a bounded direct march/reprojection option.
4. **Surface lighting.** Direct sun at world point `x`: `E_sun(x)=E_TOA·T_a(x→top,sun)·V_cloud(x,sun)·V_terrain(x,sun)`, using **RGB** transmittance. A local fog layer additionally attenuates the light along the sun path only if the fog system supplies a bounded light-ray optical-depth query; otherwise treat this as an explicit visual limitation, not fake it from view-ray opacity. Diffuse sky irradiance `E_sky(x,n)=∫_hemisphere L_sky(x,ω) max(0,n·ω)dω` comes from sky radiance plus a specified cloud occlusion/ambient approximation. Reflection radiance samples a sky/environment map rendered from the same sun/air generation; direct sun belongs in it only under the chosen specular-light contract, to avoid duplicate highlight. Bevy's PBR transmittance and sky probe code show these consumers exist in practice; their exact update cadence and cloud corrections are our design choice.
5. **Cloud join.** Cloud raymarch/source shading reads `E_sun(x)` and an atmospheric sky/ambient estimate at its sample height. Its color/transmittance/depth and cloud shadow/sky-occlusion outputs are separate. The background clear sky must be attenuated and overlaid by cloud `(L_c,T_c)` in correct depth order. For a cloud segment at distance `d_c`, use front clear-air `(L_a(0,d_c),T_a(0,d_c))`, then cloud, then clear-air tail/background; do not apply full-distance AP again over the completed cloud pixel. A cloud-shadow map can feed direct surface/fog lighting; a cloud-cover scalar alone cannot replace local shadow detail. The treatment of broad cloud-induced sky ambient dimming is authored and requires perceptual qualification.
6. **Local fog overlap.** The combined extinction along a view ray is `σ_t=σ_{t,a}+σ_{t,f}`; total source is `j=j_a+j_f` with each medium's single-scatter light source and chosen ambient approximation. Integrate **both** in each overlapping near interval using the same prefix throughput; add bounded local-light and cloud/terrain shadow samples only where needed. A Godot-style cumulative `(L,T)` frustum grid is one implementation when there are enough overlapping lit volumes; sparse bounded volume raymarching/scissoring may be cheaper when fog occupies little screen area. This selects the *semantic integral*, not a compulsory full-frame froxel representation.
7. **Fog/air tail composition.** If local fog ends at ray distance `R`, the exact clear-air tail under the unchanged clear-air source is `T_a(R,d)=T_a(0,d)/T_a(0,R)`, `L_a(R,d)=[L_a(0,d)-L_a(0,R)]/T_a(0,R)`. Then `L_{final}=L_{af}(0,R)+T_{af}(0,R)[L_a(R,d)+T_a(R,d)L_{background/surface}]`. For `d<R`, simply use the combined integral to `d`. This algebra follows the linear transfer equation, but subtraction of two low-resolution AP values can be unstable near `R` or when `T_a(0,R)` is tiny. Implementers must use a stable segment lookup/re-anchored low-resolution march or a proven bounded error treatment there. When fog is absent, use the ordinary clear-air AP path. The fog source may be single-scatter plus an authored ambient fill; it must not inherit Hillaire's clear-air multiple-scatter LUT as if that solved spatial fog.
8. **Transparency and reflections.** Each transparent/cloud/water layer consumes the same `(L,T)` segment convention at its own depth, with premultiplied scene-linear radiance; compose front-to-back once. Reflections sample current sky/ambient and then the reflected surface/pixel is viewed through clear air/fog at the surface depth. This preserves sky color, haze, cloud lighting and surface lighting as responses to one sun/profile/weather generation. Near-camera fog does not automatically belong inside a global reflection probe; local probe captures need their own placement/validity policy.

The joins above need explicit checks for RGB-vs-scalar transmittance, linear-light units, directional conventions, world/planet coordinate transforms, atmospheric top/ground intersections, depth-buffer reconstruction (including reversed Z), and whether artistic scale affects the view ray, sun path, or both. `L` is premultiplied radiance; `T` is dimensionless. The physical clock and weather generation must be consistent for cloud shadows, fog density, LUTs and sky probes. Omitting any one can make a plausible sky with incoherent terrain, clouds, or reflections.

## Budget and profile choice

The commissioning targets are WebGPU/M2-class 1280×720 with clouds ≤5 ms, whole GPU ≤16 ms and whole browser ≤512 MiB; native clouds ≤3 ms and GPU ≤10 ms. These leave 11/7 ms for **all other GPU work**, not an atmosphere allowance. A proposed allocation target is ≤0.8 ms typical WebGPU and ≤0.5 ms native for clear air plus composition, with local fog admitted only from measured headroom; these numbers are design objectives, not observed performance. An expensive shadowed sky path may need a separate quality tier and must not silently consume the cloud budget.

Analytical work for the paper-size LUTs: transmittance `256·64·40 ≈ 0.66M` samples per profile edit; multi-scatter `32²·64·20 ≈ 1.31M` samples per profile edit; sky view `192·108·30 ≈ 0.62M` samples per view/sun edit; aerial `32³·30 ≈ 0.98M` nominal segment samples per view/sun edit. The latter varies by slice and implementation. The 720p composite touches 0.92M pixels plus depth/sky/aerial reads. A 64×36×32 local grid is 73,728 cells **before** light/shadow/GI evaluation and integration; 80×45×48 is 172,800. These counts predict ordering and sensitivity, not milliseconds; texture cache behavior, dispatch overhead, shader occupancy, WebGPU limits, GPU queues and simultaneous clouds decide actual time.

Logical unaliased RGBA16F payload at 256×64 transmittance + 32² multi-scatter + 192×108 sky + 32³ aerial is about **0.54 MiB per view** (trans 128 KiB, multi 8 KiB, sky 162 KiB, aerial 256 KiB), before RGB transmittance alternatives, padding, histories, filtered sky maps and multiple cameras. One 64×36×32 RGBA16F local grid is **0.56 MiB**; density, lighting, integrated result and history can multiply that. An RGBA16F 128²×6 sky cube is **0.75 MiB before mips**. These are logical texture bytes, **not** actual process residency; the 512 MiB whole-browser cap requires integrated accounting for cloud assets, scene textures, depth, compiler/pipeline state and JS/Wasm memory.

Rank reductions by likely saved work and visible risk, to be measured: cache profile-only LUTs on validated keys; avoid local-fog allocation/dispatch when empty; bound fog cells to occupied screen tiles and short depth; share sun/sky radiance computations; lower sky/AP size or update cadence only after checking sunrise and camera motion; reduce local lights/shadow samples with scissoring and importance; finally reduce march steps with temporal reconstruction. Preserve RGB sun extinction and sky/terrain/cloud consistency before removing physically small effects. A surface shader that recomputes atmosphere per pixel may be more costly than an added shared lookup; conversely a large froxel grid can cost more than sparse rays. Compare both assembled paths.

## Gaps and discriminating qualification

1. **Colored aerial extinction and overlap.** Compare RGB transmittance and combined air+fog path against the author mean-alpha path through low-sun silhouettes, thin distant cloud, colored haze and dense local fog. Reject mean-alpha if channel error visibly changes edges/hue. Test segment tails near `R`, near zero distance, and high optical depth against a direct reference march; a small AP difference error is not fixed by increasing local-fog samples.
2. **Closure/appearance.** Compare selected atmosphere profiles (clear, hazy, sunset, overcast transition, exaggerated art) with Bruneton and Hillaire's path-tracing comparator where available. Note separately model drift and LUT resolution. Restrict profile envelope or offer higher-quality reference mode if dense forward Mie creates unacceptable hue.
3. **Same-event lighting.** Hold one weather timestamp and sun; compare sky, RGB direct terrain tint, cloud shading/shadows, fog shafts, diffuse surface fill and reflections as a set during sunrise, cloud passage, fog appearance/removal and camera motion. A correct sky alone fails this check. Derive and observe whether cloud shadow optical depth and fog light attenuation agree with visual cloud/fog positions.
4. **Target performance/residency.** On the specified M2-class WebGPU browser and native profile, measure GPU stages and whole-browser residency with clouds, terrain, transparent water, local fog and environment-map updates active together at 1280×720. Record cadence/tails over sustained weather changes, allocations, device recovery and quality transitions. The published GTX 1080/iPhone 6s timings and source availability provide no target certification.
5. **Optional capabilities.** If local lights, multiple cameras, space views, moon, large-scale spatial weather fronts or cloud-induced ambient light are required, they must be declared with their update/visibility budgets. Hillaire's altitude-only global air LUT cannot encode horizontal weather patches; source material does not resolve their scalable full-system design.

This study is complete as source research and an analytical recommendation. It is **not** a build-ready proof of the integrated fog/cloud/air renderer or acceptance of its appearance; those require the owner to select the above contracts and qualify the listed transitions.
