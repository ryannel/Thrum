## Correctness and work account

The clock tests feed 30, 60, 120 and 240 callback timestamps for one second and get exactly 60 world updates at every callback rate; a separately selected 120 Hz world rate produces 120 updates under 240 callbacks. Further cases cover a failed authority publication held by eight snapshot readers, retry after release with one committed action/event, pause and resume without hidden-time catch-up, draining already admitted work during pause, nonmonotonic time rejection, and an explicit overload when more than eight updates are due. The overload report counts refused due updates and requires a fresh origin after the pending queue drains. At each host call, clock bookkeeping is O(1) and attempts at most four updates by default; `TickClock` has no per-call heap storage. The rule callback and its publication costs are separate and are not bounded by the clock's bookkeeping.

The cost fixture uses synthetic timestamps, 100 blocks of 1,000 measured callbacks after 10 warmup blocks, and a trivial callback. It checks tick counts and repeats an idle empty-world authority path at 120 callbacks per second. The two selected runs are [A](clock-a.txt) and [B](clock-b.txt). Block-level nanoseconds per callback varied: clock-only p50 was 6–11 ns, while clock plus idle authority p50 was 29–46 ns; the latter p99 was 31–49 ns. These tiny times are close to timer/optimizer and host scheduling effects, and some other block tails varied substantially. They support the limited conclusion that bookkeeping is small in this local synthetic run, not a nanosecond latency guarantee or a comparison with another host. The benchmark's 109,999 or 54,999 tick totals over its fractional final timestamp reflect integer-nanosecond truncation of that last synthetic callback time; the one-second correctness fixture reaches exactly 60 ticks.

## Unqualified boundaries

`cpu_tick_budget` is a host admission count, not a measured deadline. A real host must derive it from a conservative tick-cost bound and callback reserve, detect a callback that exceeds its actual budget, validate stage ordering/access, and apply the correct local or online overload policy. No renderer, display pacing, mobile suspend, thermal run, multiplayer transport, process-wide snapshot-byte limit or device timing was exercised here. The [I1 design](../../design/I1-HEADLESS.md#bounded-host-clock-slice) retains those obligations.

The subsequent [native headless host qualification](HOST.md) implements and measures the initial local callback and authority/replica stage path. Broader platform and online policies remain separate.
