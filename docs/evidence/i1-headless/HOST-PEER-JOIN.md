# Bounded peer join in the sealed headless host

1 November 2026. A sealed host may now declare peer capacity above its initial baseline count. The required replica precharges confirmed and candidate rows for that limit. `HeadlessHost::register_peer` checks the authority's registered peer count plus the proposed join, so several accepted joins before one callback cannot overfill the replica. The authority prepares a new sorted watermark column under the shared pool while the old owner remains live, then admits its bounded pending queue, peer-registry and due-key growth before adding the peer. A successful join installs the prepared owner, allowing its first clean idle tick without a new pool reservation. Failed capacity admission leaves the peer unregistered. The host refuses an existing replicated peer with `RequiresBaseline`; reconnect needs a fresh authoritative baseline to reset epoch and progress coherently.

The host test starts with two peers. At a two-peer replica limit, a third join is refused without increasing pool use or changing the baseline. At a three-peer limit, full competing pool use first refuses the join; retry after release succeeds. A fourth join before any callback is refused, covering the review-agent's P1 finding about unpublished joins. With the third peer accepted, another owner fills every remaining pool byte; the host still completes an idle tick and the required replica admits all three watermarks. After the competitor releases its bytes, the third peer submits an action and receives exactly one result and a contiguous watermark. All owned charges release after host and readers drop. `RUSTC_WRAPPER= ./dev check` passes; the review-agent found no remaining scoped issue after the capacity fix.

Join preparation scans P registered peers and allocates one charged watermark column of P + 1 entries. It is a membership transition outside the repeated tick path; this proof has no separate join-latency measurement. It covers the local in-memory match host, not remote transport, wire baseline delivery or reconnect construction. Accepted future inputs still need protected output byte credits, and the complete process admission overlap remains open.
