# Bounded structural world-growth design

1 November 2026. **Selected design target; no structural-growth runtime is qualified.** This extends the selected sealed, charged authority/required-replica path to spawn and despawn actions without weakening accepted-input progress under competing pool use. Local and multiplayer hosts keep the same rule, identity, result and snapshot contracts. The first profile declares a finite maximum physical slot high-water `M ≤ u32::MAX + 1`, maximum live objects `L ≤ M`, at most two inline edits per action, a finite peer maximum, and a required replica with object capacity at least `L`. A game that needs a larger or dynamic world requires another profile.

At submission, a pure game rule classifies the typed action into an enforced maximum spawn count `Sᵢ`, independent of future world legality. The authority stores that bound with the input and faults before its edits if preparation exceeds it. The global configured maximum remains an upper bound on every `Sᵢ`. This lets a zero-spawn despawn action enter when live or physical capacity is full; admission cannot rely on a pending future despawn to fund a new spawn. A game that always classifies every action with a positive spawn bound will conservatively refuse reclamation at capacity, so that classification is unsuitable for a bounded structural profile. An action that despawns and then spawns may also be refused at a full cap despite net-zero final occupancy; a richer enforced transient-occupancy bound would be needed to admit it safely. Local and remote inputs use the same classifier and canonical execution-time decision.

## World owners and same-tick identity

Activation must reserve and charge the retained slot vector through `M`, dirty-page flags through `ceil(M / PAGE_SIZE)`, a free-slot buffer through `M`, a pinned stable-ID table sized for at most `L` live entries, and a bounded change journal through the maximum due edits. Replacement of existing buffers must reserve old/new overlap before allocation; refusal installs nothing. Both the live-ID and journal tables' requested allocations must be bounded using the pinned hashbrown layout and verified after construction, then asserted stable through long insert/remove churn, including journal cancellation tombstones. Generation-exhausted retired slots never re-enter the free list. No structural callback may grow these buffers behind the pool. Finite live storage does not itself prevent a stable ID from being reused after publication; the identity issuer needs its own no-reuse policy and overflow behavior before network qualification.

The current no-spawn journal is keyed by slot, so one slot cannot represent `Despawn(A)` followed by `Spawn(B)` before publication. The structural profile uses a separately charged, pinned **stable-ID-keyed** journal and a bounded touched-ID list. It preserves same-tick slot reuse and the prototype's checked-handle behavior. `Spawn(A)` then `DespawnNew(A)` cancels; an already published `A` cannot be despawned and respawned with the same stable ID before the barrier; `Despawn(A)` and `Spawn(B)` remain two distinct records even if they use one slot. A canceled ID keeps a neutral journal entry and its one touched-list position until publication. Re-editing that ID updates the neutral entry in place rather than appending a duplicate. Publication sorts only touched IDs, skips neutral entries and emits final changes in canonical identity order. The charged output vector covers its requested capacity even if neutral entries reduce its final length. The no-spawn per-slot journal remains the faster specialized path.

## Future credit horizon

Let `H` be current slot high-water, `F` reusable free slots, and `Sᵢ` the stored spawn bound of each accepted pending input. A safe possible high-water for all currently accepted work is `B = max(H, H - F + ΣSᵢ)`, with checked arithmetic. The authority caches `ΣSᵢ` and updates it on admission, resolution, cancellation and reconnect. A candidate input is refused before sequencing if its new `B` exceeds `M`, or if current live objects plus pending spawn bounds exceeds `L` or required replica headroom. Each pending input's future snapshot/group credit must cover the root/page peak at `B`, even if that input itself cannot spawn: another accepted input may enlarge the root first. The authority also caches a funded per-input peak. On a new admission that raises that peak, reserve **all** pending top-ups plus the candidate's complete snapshot/group and output allowance atomically before changing any existing credit. Finish other fallible admission preparation before transferring the top-ups and consuming the candidate sequence. Otherwise a competing pool user could block an already accepted input after world growth. If `B` stays in the funded page-count/peak band, admission needs no old-credit scan or upgrade. When pending work resolves or cancels, older credits remain conservatively overfunded; the funded threshold resets to current `H` once all pending work is gone. New inputs while any older input remains use that same funded threshold so due preflight can verify homogeneous credits.

The horizon is monotone across a committed input: a spawn consumes a reusable free slot or grows `H`, while its stored `Sᵢ` leaves the pending sum. A despawn can only add reusable capacity or retire an exhausted generation. Thus the post-commit horizon of the remaining pending inputs cannot exceed the precommit `B`, provided actual spawns do not exceed their stored bounds. Snapshot preflight validates all due credit partitions before transferring their snapshot/group bytes; output allowances remain action-specific. Structural preflight prices the admitted **absolute** high-water `B`, including transient high-water when edits cancel before publication. It must not add due spawns to current `H` again: reusable free slots can make that estimate exceed the reserved horizon. The pool retains old snapshot owners until their final reader leaves.

This admission-time upgrade costs O(`Q`) reservation transfers only when the possible publication peak exceeds the cached funded threshold, with `Q ≤ max_peers × 8`; ordinary fixed-size input admission remains O(1) in world size and pending count. The action classifier adds submission-time work proportional to its own bounded action representation. Upfront reservation against all `M` slots would simplify admission but multiply the maximum-root charge across every pending input even in sparse worlds. The selected horizon funds accepted possible growth; conservative excess can persist while old pending inputs remain. Structural world map/journal work and page-root copies still require paired complete-path measurement.

## Proof and rejection criteria

Build the world owner and journal first, then connect per-action classification, authority credit upgrades and required replica admission. Tests must cover an activation one byte short, old/new activation overlap, exact final release, high-water crossing a page boundary, all-object removal and long churn, generation exhaustion, same-tick `Despawn(A)+Spawn(B)` in one slot, canceled new spawn, same-ID resurrection rejection, zero-spawn reclamation at capacity, two peers with delayed sequence order, full competing pool use, old snapshot readers, failed candidate top-up with unchanged older credits/sequence, and baseline/reconnect after growth. A false declared edit/spawn bound or unexpected postcommit allocation is terminal; expected capacity refusal is precommit and retryable. Compare no-spawn idle and changed callbacks, structural spawn/despawn callbacks, retained bytes and admission cost across small and 4,096/16,384-object worlds. Until those proofs run, the parent memory box and structural-growth maturity remain open.
