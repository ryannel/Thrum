# Independent authoring review of selected D2 baseline — 2 November 2026

Scope: read `docs/design/modules/CLOUD-PRODUCTION-BASELINE.md` and rewritten `CLOUD-MIGRATION.md`, then checked primary SideFX and Epic 5.8 documentation and the retained Nubis³/Smolder source map. This is a design audit, not a Houdini execution, content review, device measurement or code validation. Keep the chosen Houdini 22.0.459/SOP–sparse-Pyro–VDB/cooker route; the findings below close its contracts rather than reopen a tool menu.

## Findings requiring correction before the D2 review checkbox closes

### A1 — High: global ambient bake is keyed as a reusable per-master frame

`CLOUD-PRODUCTION-BASELINE.md` lines 48–51 say each source's 16³ accessibility samples **all** episode cloud constituents. Lines 85–89 then key immutable source products by `(master, frame knot, mip, layout, story/wind revision)` and share where identical. The accessibility at a point also depends on *which other events are present, their poses/frames, and the exact episode time*. Two identical Cu master frames in different positions or weather scenes cannot share that field. If they do, an anvil/deck blocks one cloud's ambient in the wrong region; if global dependencies dirty all source accessibilities every frame, cost/storage exceeds the per-master sharing account. The source-to-light join has no complete key.

**Complete fix:** separate the reusable optical brick key from the **episode-space accessibility product**. Key accessibility by episode snapshot/time, world support/tile (or event instance ID/pose), source-group revision set and bake directions/scale, with conservatively recorded dependency bounds. Publish it in the same compatible generation as the density; no fallback to an unrelated master accessibility. Count it as a distinct per-episode time-varying stream in offline cook, IO, decoded pool and old/new peak. The P1b pilot must put a second cloud/anvil above the same Cu with different poses to prove the accessibility changes while the Cu optical master stays identical. This remains the selected 16-direction bake, not a new lighting algorithm.

### A2 — High: source-format lock can precede the three representative content regimes

`CLOUD-MIGRATION.md` P3b (lines 34–37, 102–110) depends on P4a and vaguely on “source pilot conformance,” while P1b Cu/Cb, Sc/St and Ci is parallel after P4a. P3b can therefore freeze the uniform 3D brick/streaming and light product using only P1a's one Cu. Full-cover Sc/St is likely the worst 3D residency case; Ci is the thinnest occupancy/quantization case; Cb is the tallest swept-support and mixed liquid/ice case. A small Cu sentinel cannot reject a format that fails those regimes. At 8³ interiors plus 1-voxel guards, one RG16F page occupies `10³×4=4000` bytes for 512 useful voxels. As an illustrative **conditional** stress case, a 128×128 km deck at 200 m horizontal pitch needs 640² columns, one vertical brick per 8×8 footprint, about 6400 occupied pages or 25.6 MB *per frame before index/mips/accessibility*. Two endpoints consume ~51 MB of the 64 MiB decoded pool, even before Cu/Ci. The actual admitted scale/pitch and measured sparsity determine the result; this is not a forecast that the product fails.

**Complete fix:** make **P1b a hard prerequisite for source-format freeze and P3b product admission**, not merely for P4b. Its Sc/St representative must include a connected dense overcast patch at the actual planned spatial extent/altitude/voxel profile, not only a tiny hole demo; its Ci and Cb fixtures must include thin/upper and mixed-phase adverse frames. Carry physical page count, guard overhead, two temporal endpoints, 3 future presentation pairs, accessibility, IO/decode/upload, old/new overlap and all retained consumers into the P3b ledger. Tune resolution/cadence inside the selected 3D format if it can pass; if it structurally cannot, record the contradictory result and revise D2 before library production. The explicit P4a Cu sentinel stays early.

### A3 — High: event-local interpolation and accelerated IO need a precise admissibility equation

`CLOUD-PRODUCTION-BASELINE.md` lines 39–47, 85–96 choose baked wind, moving local frames and 4 Hz optical samples; they say interpolate at “the same source point,” but do not state which pose maps the *world query* into both endpoint frames. Applying each endpoint pose separately and blending densities at a fixed world point doubles or smears translational movement; applying the current interpolated pose to both fields is correct only where the two VDBs were exported in the **same local material chart**. Internal baked shear can move features across local voxels between knots even after removing the anchor. At 64×, 4 Hz authored time means 256 source knots pass per real second; although presentation can skip most knots, a 60 Hz display asks for up to 120 distinct endpoint frame sets per real second for visible unique sources. “Three future presentation-time frame pairs” is an in-flight policy, not an IO rate or memory bound.

**Complete fix:** state the query explicitly: for world point `x` at episode time `t`, `u = inverse(P(t)) x` and `q(x,t) = (1−a) q_k(u) + a q_(k+1)(u)` for phase-weighted nonnegative optical coefficients, with `P(t)` the **baked/interpolated authored pose**, no extra wind. Define both endpoint fields in that common local chart, transform the union of endpoint occupied bounds by `P(t)`, and reject or add knots if residual internal displacement/optical silhouette/intermediate connectivity at quarter/mid/three-quarter times deviates from the 24 Hz master. Pose interpolation must preserve its path and frame handedness. P1a/P1b should record unique requested keys, bytes and decode/upload rate at 1×/16×/64×, cache hits/misses and 3-frame prediction accuracy under seeks/cuts. If insufficient, a current-time acceptable mip or explicit missed frame is the documented outcome; no phase hold. This does not require runtime optical flow or warp.

### A4 — Medium: episode succession promises undefined state matching

`CLOUD-PRODUCTION-BASELINE.md` lines 72–75 says episode succession requires shared authored boundary state/IDs, but does not define supported edges among four independent 10-minute stories. The P6 weather requirement can be satisfied **within** each finite story, with surviving IDs through that story's transitions. An unqualified ability to switch from any story at any time would reintroduce the arbitrary branch problem the baseline intentionally removed.

**Complete fix:** list the supported story-transition graph and exact shared boundary snapshots if cross-story succession is intended. Otherwise state that story selection occurs at load/reset, each story contains its required clear→scattered/increasing-cover/overcast/storm progression, and changing stories starts a declared new epoch without pretending existing IDs survived. P6 should inspect survivors only along explicitly authored continuous intervals. No extra Houdini branch system is needed.

### A5 — Medium: class-specific recipes still leave the data-producing node operation implicit

The baseline's family table (lines 58–64) names Pyro, feedback and curves, but for Sc/St it does not state the local update that closes/open holes; for Cb it does not state how core and anvil density are partitioned and avoid double counting; for Ci it does not specify emission versus continuing tail state. An implementer could satisfy the node names with static masks plus animated noise, producing the rejected still-life substitute.

**Complete fix:** retain the compact table but add a short operational recipe contract: Cu/Cb emit tagged source density/temperature/velocity into sparse Pyro with staggered feeder sources and dissipation; Cb upper outflow uses height/time-dependent divergent/shear velocity under a cap, with liquid/ice fractions partitioning one optical density rather than separately adding a duplicate anvil. Sc/St use a SOP Solver feedback column `q_next=clamp(Advect(q,W_low,dt)+dt[S(1−q)−Dq+κ∇²q],0,1)`, then lift to a finite VDB slab with common base, thickness and relief; `S,D` are spatial/time authored fields, not global opacity. Ci emits tagged points from persistent heads each step, advects/shears/falls and attenuates them by local drying/age before `Volume Rasterize Attributes`; old tails remain after head source weakens. All shaped final frames are cached, and source IDs plus support/connectivity diagnostics demonstrate growth, mature renewal and breakup. These are **our authored recipes**, supported as operations by SideFX [Pyro Solver](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/pyrosolver.html), [Solver SOP](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/solver.html), [Volume Wrangle](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/volumewrangle.html), [Volume Rasterize Attributes](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/volumerasterizeattributes.html), not vendor-provided five-family assets.

### A6 — Medium: production lineage wording can overstate UE 5.8 SVT

`CLOUD-MIGRATION.md` line 3 calls D2 a “Selected proven-production baseline.” `CLOUD-PRODUCTION-BASELINE.md` line 14 cites Epic 5.8 SVT for adjacent-frame interpolation and prefetch. Epic's current documentation marks Heterogeneous Volumes/SVT **Experimental**, warns to use caution when shipping, says animation fidelity can fall to coarser mip if streaming cannot keep up, and describes two-frame interpolation as a controller/material option, not proof of Thrum's 4 Hz WebGPU schedule. Its VDB import is Windows-editor only; cooked SVT playback can run on capable platforms, but Thrum is building its own WebGPU representation, not using Unreal's streamer. Nubis³ supports prepared modeled voxels and optical acceleration, but its deck distinguishes pseudomotion from our required structural animation. Smolder's inspected notes support offline animated volumes and report whole-animation residency cost, but do not prove Thrum's five-family/weather/air/reflection stack.

**Complete fix:** call D2 a **selected production-derived baseline**, not a proven whole system. Attribute the UE citation specifically to *documented animated VDB/SVT import, playback, optional adjacent-frame material interpolation and sparse streaming limitations*. Cite [Epic 5.8 SVT](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sparse-volume-textures-in-unreal-engine) and [Heterogeneous Volumes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/heterogeneous-volumes-in-unreal-engine) with the Experimental status. Keep Nubis³ and Smolder as distinct precedents for content/volume scheduling, and keep all exact Thrum sizes, formats, cadence and simultaneous consumer timing labelled unproven deductions. This is claim hygiene, not a reason to reverse the choice.

## Verified strengths and limit

The chosen license gate is honest: no Houdini installation/seat was found here, no purchase is presumed, and SideFX's Apprentice noncommercial chain is not substituted for deliverable source. The source wind convention is now one application (baked pose plus residual fields), and the Sc/St Houdini column workflow is compatible with a uniform **exported 3D** optical query. The explicit 2 GiB pilot scratch cap, later full-episode storage estimate, staged package hashes and recoverable master policy are appropriate. The five families, four stories and same-time main/shadow/reflection/air/fog consumers stay required. No build, bulk cache, downloaded asset or target device result was used in this review.
