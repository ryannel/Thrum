# Inactive immutable render-v1 source definition

3 November 2026. A private 10,451-byte `thrum.render.scene@1` descriptor now binds the candidate source grammar as exact immutable UTF-8 bytes under the existing built-in SHA-256 domain. Its digest is `aff743ddb7c0fa8835efa417e51b9c083d76011a92add33df88e549d327f3cf0`, independently recomputed during adversarial review. The descriptor is **not registered** in `builtin_schema`, so its presence does not enable F3 admission or an F4 product. The three existing built-in descriptors and hashes remain unchanged.

The descriptor states concrete canonical UUID spelling; strict decoded keys and optional/null behavior; common record byte/node/depth bounds; binary64 and unsigned integer-token meaning; identity order and typed same-root/slot-format edges; camera, mesh, instance, material, texture and local-light fields; exact radius, triangle, quaternion, transformed-box and luminance predicates; and material defaults and equations. It incorporates the [ideal source sampler law](../../design/research/R0-SAMPLER-IDEAL-AND-TARGET-2026-11-03.md), including nearest ties, per-tap address modes, sRGB decode before filtering and alpha equality. R0 recipe restrictions and finite device evaluator details are deliberately outside source validity and require separate product/evaluator revisions and proof.

Three focused tests pin the domain-separated golden digest, reject a wrong hash domain, show selected semantic text mutations change the digest, and verify the descriptor remains unregistered while the existing three built-ins retain their bytes/hashes. Targeted tests and Clippy pass. Independent defect-first review found no mismatch with the candidate grammar, private predicates or RENDER-SPEC. The sampler-law review separately found no mathematical blocker and added reflected-tie and nearest-discontinuity proof cases.

Hash coverage does not prove that all source records are validated, that global UUID/edge/deferred closure is implemented, or that a target reproduces the ideal sampler or BRDF. Whole-record performance, parser/allocator peak, F4 product keys and device quality remain open.
