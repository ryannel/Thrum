# Asset experimentation, promotion and retention

Design handoff, 1 November 2026. This is a proposal grounded in current Broadsides packages, not a cleanup authorization or a claim of implementation. No asset files were moved or deleted for this review.

## Observed pressure and current contracts

- The [24 October cleanup](../../../provenance/CONTEXT.md#source-09b20c39d581) measured `tmp/` falling from 436.9 to 159.7 GiB after 277.2 GiB was reclaimed. The remainder included 52.3 GiB of captures, 45.5 GiB of `retired/` recovery material, 51.1 GiB of live/unfinished copies and 6.8 GiB of generation candidates. Those are historical measurements, not today's disk use. The recovery store contains unique ignored files and has no verified remote backup.
- `factory/ships/patchwork-privateer/work/` shows many `pre-*`, `review-*`, `rejected-*` and approved subfolders. Their names explain the path taken, but make the current rebuild chain and expiry of comparisons hard to infer from the directory tree alone. Its approved geometry and paint are distinct from runtime/game acceptance.
- The `factory/environments/clearing-conifers/asset.json` record has grown into a detailed multi-trial inventory with thousands of path/hash entries and many retained comparisons. It proves care with evidence, but a single package record is carrying both discovery and campaign history. Several scopes explicitly say remote recovery is unverified; selected later scopes have fresh empty-store LFS restore evidence.
- `factory/ships/sovereign-96/asset.json` maps one current master, delivery and dependency ancestry with remote recovery `not-verified`. `factory/props/traveller-lantern/asset.json` retains an original generated GLB, textures, prompt/task provenance and editable masters but also says recovery is unverified. Such selected generated geometry/pixels are unique inputs: a prompt or task ID can identify their origin, but cannot regenerate the same bytes on demand.
- The [storage lifecycle](../../../ASSET-LIFECYCLE.md) already requires peak-space planning, selected complete rebuild inputs, scoped cleanup and respect for active owners. The [asset contract](../../../ASSET-LIFECYCLE.md) already provides stable package IDs, local `asset.json`, source/runtime separation, LFS or durable-store retention, hashed runtime manifests and staged complete publication. The [factory README](../../../provenance/CONTEXT.md#source-4058a8c5b244) additionally says to preserve originals and make derivatives, while the repo-structure skill discourages versioned backup folders. Read these together as preservation of *needed original ancestry and selected derivations*, with Git/content hashes carrying revisions; do not preserve every numbered intermediate as a live version.

## Recommended lifecycle

| State | Home and minimum record | Exit and retention decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experiment | Owner-scoped ignored `tmp/generations/<date>-<topic>/` or an existing active workbench. A small local run note names owner, question, input identities, producing command/provider, budget and cleanup trigger. | Try a representative sample before a sweep. Keep generated output until its decision is recorded; no automatic promotion because a format check passed. |
| Review candidate | One candidate set plus only alternatives needed for a named visual, mechanism or cost decision. Record exact hashes, source/provenance, preview settings, consumer and expiry/review trigger. | Selection, rejection or a documented unresolved comparison. A candidate may stay in scratch while review is open, but its owner and next checkpoint must be explicit. |
| Adopted source | `factory/<category>/<asset-id>/` retains selected original/generated inputs, editable master, recipes, licenses/usage terms, exact dependencies and compact evidence. `asset.json` points to these and states recovery status. | Verify the complete rebuild chain and remote LFS/store recovery before claiming a durable handoff; then release known redundant scratch. Adoption means production ownership, not final art or gameplay approval. |
| Cooked delivery | Immutable, validated profile products staged outside `game/public`, then a coherent manifest/payload set published to the declared runtime destination. | A consumer and quality-specific review determines eligible use. `game/public` holds only selected deployable profiles and their required manifest/metadata, never the entire trial history. |
| Active release | A game/build selection references the exact delivery identity and dependency closure, with separately recorded visual, integration and target-device decisions. | Replacement retains the prior known-good release through rollback/release validation; old payloads retire when no build/cache/runtime lease needs them. |

The selected asset's stable logical ID survives edits. A source revision is its content identity (plus relevant Git commit/dirty state), a cook product has its own byte hash, a runtime delivery is the manifest plus complete dependency closure, and a game release chooses exact delivery revisions. A paint skin, LOD or fitting option is a named variant axis unless it has independent reusable ownership. Prompt strings, filenames, task IDs, approval labels and active runtime IDs do not substitute for source/product hashes.

Use a small package-local `asset.json` as the **current pointer and ownership record**, with links to compact review, QA, provenance and build records. Do not add a central per-file registry or turn `asset.json` into a transcript of every trial. Campaign logs can be append-only in ignored scratch while active; promote a compact selected comparison and any reproducible failure needed by future work to the owning package. Keep source recipes and paid-generation receipts with selected sources. For a generated result, retain the exact selected output bytes and any masks/textures/components the master needs, even if its prompt is saved. A rejected result survives only when it is a named comparison, a unique required ancestor, or an explicit user retention choice.

At each selection/handoff, the owner prepares a **retention manifest** of only the selected rebuild chain, unresolved alternatives, their consumers and a cleanup trigger. It can be a short section in the package or handoff, not a new global database. Check `git check-ignore`, LFS attributes, actual upload/restore or durable-store restoration, path hashes and active processes before releasing anything. For disposable directories, owner-scoped leases with creation time, expected maximum bytes and expiry/decision trigger make stale bulk visible. Expiry is a review prompt, never permission to delete unique bytes or another owner's work. Archive only when a real recovery requirement exists and test restore; an ignored `tmp/retired` copy on the same disk is neither release storage nor backup.

## Publication and acceptance boundaries

An export command stages the complete package, verifies source and dependency identities, validates format and the actual consumer, and produces a manifest listing required relative paths, bytes, hashes, supported profile/decoder and compatibility. It then switches the package root/manifest selection in one reviewed step. For deployment, content-hashed URLs or a revisioned release base keep older manifests paired with older bytes. A failed stage leaves the prior delivery active. Shared material/components are pinned by exact identity and rebuilt only through affected dependency closure. A manifest cannot reference `tmp/`, factory, another agent's live checkout or an expiring provider URL.

Record independent claims: `source-retained`, `exported`, `consumer-tested`, `visually-reviewed/accepted`, `game-integrated` and `target-device-qualified`. An artist's geometry approval can coexist with a blocked runtime profile; a game test can pass while motion or appearance remains unaccepted. Review evidence names actual product hash, camera/device/profile and reviewer decision. A user-rejected image is not silently replaced with the newest technically passing build. This applies to privateer, Sovereign skins, traveller motion and forest crown work already described in the production index/backlog.

## Current-repo steps and future-engine fit

1. Apply this at the next checkpoint of one active package, not as a mass relocation. Pick its current selected source, required ancestors and current candidate; state unresolved review, remote recovery and exact runtime consumer. Trim only the owner's inspected disposable outputs after verification. Correct `factory/PRODUCTION-INDEX.md` wording so “preserve originals; create new versions for derivatives” means required immutable ancestors plus selected meaningful derivatives, not permanent duplicate versions of every run.
2. Add a lightweight candidate/retention checklist to the existing `factory/ASSET-STORAGE.md` and storage skill, with owner, question, max scratch size, cleanup trigger and recovery test. Keep the index for discovery and the package record for the current asset. For large forest/ship trial histories, leave compact decision evidence and references to Git history; avoid another central inventory.
3. For performance campaigns, give each candidate an isolated, bounded scratch/build root and append-only *small* record of hypothesis, exact source/cook/tool/evaluator identities, measurements, failures and disposition. Retain the baseline, current best, finalists, representative failure evidence and inputs needed to reproduce them; release raw traces, full readbacks, duplicate asset copies and dominated candidate builds at reviewed checkpoints. Do not erase search history: text records and decisive artifacts survive. Hardware measurement uses the [AI performance campaign](../../../design/AI-PERFORMANCE.md) lease and independent qualification; storage pressure and shifted build/load cost are part of its objective.
4. In the future engine, F4's source/package identity, dependency DAG, content-addressed cook and atomic manifest pointer already provide the product boundary ([world/content](../../../design/modules/WORLD-CONTENT.md)). T1 exposes desired versus active authoring revisions and pending/failed edits; T2 records build lineage, inclusion reasons, package bytes and gate evidence ([tools/delivery](../../../design/modules/TOOLS-DELIVERY.md)). Add a bounded artifact-retention policy to their implementation: source authority and published products are durable; cook caches and trial products are reclaimable by verified reachability from active release, rollback release, saved-world compatibility and named campaign finals. Runtime CPU/GPU leases and fences govern *when* a published product may retire. Trial expiry never changes an active manifest.

## Proof before mechanizing cleanup

Demonstrate one package end to end: create two candidates, select one generated input, retain and remotely restore its exact source chain, cook two profiles, reject a partial/stale publication, load the exact selected closure in a consumer, then retire only the owner's losing candidate and confirm the selected package/release still rebuilds and loads. Separately prove a performance campaign can discard bulk trials while preserving its evaluator, baseline, best/finalist identities, raw decisive results and an independently reproducible final. Measure peak scratch bytes and post-handoff bytes. Until those proofs exist, this remains a process design and cannot justify deleting the existing `tmp/retired` recovery store or another agent's workbench.
