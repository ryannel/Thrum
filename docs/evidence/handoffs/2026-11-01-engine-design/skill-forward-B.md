# Forward task B — surface wetness and snow response

**Design status:** provisional operating model; implementation, experiments, visual approval and platform performance are unrun. Commission basis: the supplied wetness/snow request, `ARCHITECTURE-BRIEF.md` E01–E12, C2 contracts C01/C02/C04/C08/C09/C12. Scope is rain/snow, temperature, material appearance and character traction across isometric town, third-person, browser and mobile profiles. A weather provider supplies precipitation and thermal/radiative drivers; this module owns accumulated surface state. No module code or previous module design was inspected.

## Required outcomes and basis

| ID | Observable outcome | Basis / status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | Rain darkens receptive materials, adds a coherent film/puddle cue, and dries after rain | Requested; mechanism below provisional |
| S2 | Snow accumulates where exposed, responds to warming/rain, leaves liquid on thaw and can refreeze | Requested snow/temperature coupling; mass transfer required by C09 |
| S3 | A character changes footing at the same place and committed time as the visible surface | Requested traction; contact semantics provisional |
| S4 | No visible sky/weather renderer is required to drive S1–S3; retained consumers still work when presentation is absent | Reusable-engine composition requirement |
| S5 | Authored materials/regions, streaming, save/reload and mobile/browser profiles produce bounded state and cost | Necessary engine implications; numerical budgets open |

No seasonal climate, atmospheric snowflake transport, roof avalanches, tire hydroplaning or physically exact snow optics are commissioned. Wind drift, footprint deformation and water-body interactions are optional later contracts, not silently promised by this surface module.

## Chosen representation, producers and operators

Represent the active world as stable, region-local surface patches at a gameplay scale chosen by the collision/traction footprint, with authored subpatch masks for exposure, drainage, impermeability, material and shader detail. Each patch stores liquid film `W` (kg m⁻² or equivalent mm water), absorbed water `A` (kg m⁻²), snow water equivalent `S` (kg m⁻²), surface ice `I` (kg m⁻²), and a lumped surface temperature/heat store `T_s` (K and J m⁻²). These are authoritative for contact and material response; GPU noise/normal detail is derived. Region seams exchange runoff by stable neighbour IDs. Use a sparse active-patch budget and an authored climatological/clear-state initializer for previously unvisited regions; on streaming retirement, save authoritative patch state or an explicit compact event/reconstruction state. A patch cannot silently reset from snowy to dry on reload.

Weather supplies liquid/solid precipitation mass flux `P_l,P_s` (kg m⁻² s⁻¹), air temperature, humidity/wind and incident shortwave/longwave fluxes with validity/scale/time; lighting supplies insolation and occlusion once, not an independent visual estimate. The phase of falling precipitation is a weather-provider responsibility; a specified fallback may partition using air temperature but is not a universal physical threshold. Authoring supplies per-material absorption capacity/rate, drainage/permeability, heat capacity/conductance, emissivity/albedo, and wet/snow appearance curves. Exposure masks reject rain beneath cover while roof runoff enters declared receiving patches; unresolved building gutters may use a sink rather than invent an invisible accumulation.

For a patch of area `a`, advance once per world tick using finite-volume flux accounting (all fluxes per area). `A' = A + infiltration - release`, bounded by material capacity. `W' = W + P_l dt + snow melt + ice melt + release - infiltration - evaporation - freeze - runoff`. `S' = S + P_s dt - melt - sublimation - snow redistribution`. `I' = I + freeze - ice melt`. Every transfer is clamped to available source mass and entered once in a per-tick ledger; `runoff` crosses a region edge or a named world-water/drain sink. `W,A,S,I >= 0`, and total represented water changes only by precipitation plus named boundary exchanges. This is an authored reduced model, not a full hydrology solver. The snow-water-equivalent and energy bookkeeping follow the variables used by the [US Army Corps HEC snowpack account](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/hmsdocs/hmstrm/snow-accumulation-and-melt/snowpack-mass-and-energy-accounting); the spatial energy-flux drivers and limitations are illustrated in the [NRCS-hosted snowmelt study](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2023-03/Spatially%20distributed%20energy%20balance%20snowmelt%20modelling.pdf).

Use a reduced surface energy balance `C dT_s/dt = Q_short + Q_long + Q_sensible + Q_rain + Q_ground - L_v E - L_f(M-F)` with terms in W m⁻², where `E`, `M` and `F` are mass fluxes in kg m⁻² s⁻¹ and latent heats are J kg⁻¹. Snow/ice melting consumes energy; freezing releases it before temperature crosses the phase boundary. `M,F` are limited by available `S/I/W` and positive/negative energy; their per-tick transfers are flux times `dt`. The heat coefficients and under-snow insulation are authored/calibrated closures, not inferred from precipitation alone. If radiation/humidity is unavailable, an explicitly named temperature-index provider may produce melt/evaporation rates; it still conserves represented water mass but makes no energy-accuracy claim. This separation prevents a sunny and shaded patch with equal air temperature from being forced to behave identically without an explicit fallback choice. Fast temperature transitions may need substeps for flux positivity; a rate-capped operator is preferable to negative storage.

Drain `W` along an authored downhill adjacency with capacity-limited flux from local film excess; porous materials route some to `A`, impermeable ones to runoff. The patch model does not resolve centimetre puddle flow or roof-edge splashes. Its supported scale is outdoor ground/roof surfaces and character footing, with decorative fine puddles derived from `W`, slope and authored microtopography. Surface-water body exchange is a typed runoff boundary, not a second rainfall accumulator.

## Interfaces and causal schedule

| Interface | Producer → consumer | Quantity and rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weather and illumination | E1 weather/V3 → response | World position/time, precipitation phase flux, thermal/radiative drivers, validity and support; fallback selection explicit |
| Surface material recipe | F4/W2 → response | Stable patch/material IDs, exposure/drainage graph, capacities and temperature/appearance/traction parameters, revision |
| State/contact query | response → P3/P4 movement | At world contact position/footprint and tick `n`, return support/material ID, `W,A,S,I,T_s`, cover class, confidence and effective static/kinetic traction parameters for a specified footwear/character policy |
| Appearance | response → V3/V2 | Immutable committed patch snapshot plus derived wet-film, absorbed-darkening, snow/ice coverage and version; render interpolation does not alter contact authority |
| Boundary/persistence | response → W2/P7/water | Named runoff flux/event IDs, durable state and region revision; stale async products rejected |

Order: pre-tick weather/material/profile revisions commit; response samples weather at tick `n`, exchanges boundary fluxes and publishes `n+1`; physics contact for movement at `n+1` reads that committed state (or, if engine ordering retains pre-step contact, reads `n` and presentation of the resulting motion is labelled one tick later). This ordering must be fixed centrally before implementation. Foot traction is a contact-pair property: material/wet/snow/ice state plus footwear and gait policy. A piecewise continuous lookup `mu_eff = f(material, footwear, W, S, I, T_s, contact pressure/speed class)` supplies available friction and deformable-snow resistance; physics limits tangential impulse by `|J_t| <= mu_eff J_n`, while character control selects recovery/animation from achieved displacement. Do not reuse vehicle wet-pavement coefficients for a foot. [Measured winter-footwear walking](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4917907/) shows traction varies with sole, wet/dry ice, snow and slope; thus numerical curves require character-specific calibration and visual/game-feel acceptance.

Appearance uses the same state but distinct optical closures: `A` changes porous-substrate absorption/darkening, `W` adds a thin smooth-water reflection layer and possible puddle mask; snow/ice coverage occludes substrate and changes albedo/roughness/scattering response. [Jensen, Legakis and Dorsey (1999)](https://graphics.stanford.edu/~henrik/papers/egwr99/egwr99.pdf) distinguish water within from water atop material and show wet materials need not always darken. [Snow optical research](https://doi.org/10.1098/RSTA.2018.0161) ties snow appearance to scattering/grain properties, so a white blend is only a low-cost stylized provider. Micro-normal variation may be view-dependent; it cannot change `W/S/I` or traction. Renderer quality may replace the snow optical closure while retaining the same patch state.

## Worked transition and modularity

A sheltered cobblestone alley and exposed stone step receive the same regional rain. Exposure admits rain only to the step; some step water absorbs, excess film runs downhill into the alley mouth via a declared edge, and its wet layer becomes more specular while darkening follows stone-specific `A`. At the next committed tick, a walking character's foot query at the step samples the same `W` used for shading; footwear changes `mu_eff`. When cold precipitation turns to snow, `S` accumulates only at exposed patches, obscures the wet stone, and changes contact classification. Sunlit stone melts earlier than shaded stone because its energy input differs; melt enters `W`, then runoff or refreezing transfers to `I`. A save/reload restores `S/W/I` and a region-edge ledger before contact and rendering resume. Two cameras share this state but have independent shading histories. A weather renderer may be absent throughout.

`surfaceResponse=off` uses a declared dry/static material provider and allocates no patch state, flux scheduling or derived snow textures. `snowPresentation=off` can keep physical snow and traction. `dynamicWeather=off` may supply constant weather to the response. A game requiring dynamic snowy traction cannot disable the response without a substitute that promises equivalent query semantics; profile validation rejects that combination. Live provider replacement must migrate or intentionally clear authoritative state by an explicit game policy and invalidate derived render resources. Build-time omission and load-time selection are simpler than arbitrary live switching.

## Cost, alternatives, open qualification

For `N` active patches and `E` runoff adjacencies, state update is `O(N+E)` CPU work per response tick and storage `O(N)`; render derivation can be coarser/finer by view without redefining physical state. Exact patch size, cadence, streaming memory and transfer cost must be chosen against foot size, drainage gradients, camera distance and named mobile hardware. A GPU-only decal/accumulation system is rejected for required contact and persistence unless readback/reconstruction meets the same contract; independent shader wetness and physics friction maps would duplicate authority. A full energy/hydrology/continuum snow solver is unnecessary for the specified game outcomes and likely too costly, though this is an architectural judgment, not a benchmark.

**Unqualified:** energy closure calibration, visual wet/snow fidelity, wet-ice and packed-snow traction curves, whether snow depth/deformation is needed for animation, patch resolution versus visible/detail and contact discontinuities, region-edge runoff, mobile sustained cost and browser startup/residency. First future integrated proof should show rain → one saved surface state → material and footsteps, then snow → thaw → refreeze across a sun/shade and stream boundary; it must reject a merely animated shader or a traction map unrelated to accumulation. No proof was run under the instruction for tonight.

**Contract issue returned to architecture owner:** C2 states `physics/gameplay/environment then step`, but it does not settle whether character contact in the same world step uses just-published surface `X[n+1]` or pre-step `X[n]`. Choose one global ordering/latency rule before this module is build-ready; both are viable if rendering and movement agree. Thermal/radiative field producer and edge runoff receiver also need named owners in detailed commissions.
