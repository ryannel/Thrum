# First-use art and collaboration dogfood

1 November 2026. Analytical walkthrough of a proposed, **unimplemented** custom engine. These are creator actions and design gaps, not observed usability, image quality, device performance, or implementation proof. The baseline is [Building a game](../../../design/ENGINE-EXPERIENCE.md), the [scenario walkthroughs](../../../design/SCENARIO-WALKTHROUGHS.md), [rendering](../../../design/modules/RENDERING.md), [world/content](../../../design/modules/WORLD-CONTENT.md), and [tooling/delivery](../../../design/modules/TOOLS-DELIVERY.md). The [architecture brief](../../../design/ARCHITECTURE-BRIEF.md) requires contrasting camera/style games and Mac, PC, browser, and mobile ambition; device floors are open. Source files are canonical, setup is explicit, a full editor is not required, and arbitrary renderer compatibility is not promised.

## 1. A realistic indoor room: import to a credible first image

**Create.** An artist imports a room glTF with meshes, textures, named materials, and perhaps authored cameras/lights. Import reports converted units/axes, asset and node IDs, missing textures, unsupported extensions, alpha modes, material texture colour spaces, and whether cameras/lights were accepted as scene records or only retained as source data. It offers a reviewable scene insertion: room, collision policy, start camera, and a named **interior daylight** or **interior artificial light** recipe that expands to editable lights, fill/reflection sources, exposure and display settings. Imported glTF is an interchange asset, not assumed to contain a working game scene or collision. The artist sees the room on the target baseline renderer immediately, including which source revision, camera, target tier and display transform produced the image. A simple first-person walk or fixed camera is enough to inspect it; the programmer can add interactions later.

**First image and iteration.** A physically interpretable material baseline needs an explicit lighting source, indirect/probe fill or baked contribution, shadow policy, camera near/far and exposure. An empty room should not silently turn white, black, or emissive because a renderer invented ambient light. A named recipe may choose defaults, but the preview shows their values, owners and provenance. The artist adjusts light placement/intensity, exposure lock or metering, reflection source and material response in one visual loop; compares a diagnostic neutral-material view against the intended materials; captures the same camera with a fixed exposure. The programmer sees the same source diff, stable IDs, and accepted revision. Gameplay changes in play are discarded; selected authored placement can be applied back with revision checking.

**Change, delivery, recovery.** The team changes an external texture or room mesh, completes a coherent source sync, reviews the affected dependency closure, then previews native desktop and browser explicitly. Reimport must distinguish imported source fields from engine-authored overrides (for example light settings, material assignment and collision), match old/new nodes by stable import mapping where possible, and show removed/renamed/ambiguous matches before acceptance. A mesh rename must not detach a light or collider by accident. Different cooked textures, shader demands and lighting quality may be selected, but the room identity and interactable collision cannot drift unnoticed. A missing normal map can use an **authored optional** fallback; an unsupported required glTF extension, material/shadow pass or missing collision blocks the affected preview/build with asset path, consumer, target and actionable choices. The last accepted source/build remains identifiable. A browser image is assessed separately; a successful desktop render does not certify it.

**First-use obligation exposed.** Existing R7/T1 already promise import, inspection and preview. Prioritize a concrete first-scene recipe and import mapping, including how imported cameras/lights/materials become editable scene records and survive reimport. Without that bridge, a new user must know how to assemble illumination and display from separate providers before seeing their room.

## 2. A toon third-person game with the creator's character

**Create.** The artist imports their skinned character and clips; import shows skeleton, clip names/durations, material slots, scale/orientation, root-motion interpretation, attachment sockets and missing required data. The team creates one prefab with stable source IDs, a named third-person camera rig and a small controller/interaction example. The programmer owns motion rules, collision capsule and action mapping; the artist owns mesh, materials, clips, silhouette and visual animation choices. Their contracts meet at a declared pose/root-motion policy, skeleton/clip mapping and source-space socket positions. The first playable result is the character moving through a small lit test level, not a model viewer mistaken for gameplay.

**Style and iteration.** A named toon style is an **explicit shading closure/material extension plus scene-light and display choices**, not a global post-process that must repair every material. The author can inspect which materials follow the style, which remain realistic or unlit, how shadow bands and outlines treat skin, hair, transparencies and cast shadows, and how the same character looks from gameplay camera distance. A style switch is a source change and target recook when its material/pass demand changes; any live preview update is offered only for a declared safe subset. A side-by-side or reversible preview of existing and proposed source revision at a pinned camera/light/exposure makes visual changes reviewable without creating two canonical asset trees.

**Change, delivery, recovery.** The artist replaces a character mesh or animation and the programmer changes controller behavior in the same project. The asset diff reports whether skeleton IDs, clips, sockets, contact capsule or style slots changed; stale prefab/controller references fail by name and ID, not as a bind pose, T-pose, frozen body or silently missing hat. Native and browser cooks derive distinct products from one source package. If the browser profile cannot prepare a required skinned toon shadow variant, it reports the exact material/geometry/pass chain and keeps the previous complete build; the team can author an explicit compatible material or narrower browser excerpt. Gameplay semantics stay fixed unless the game author deliberately changes them.

**First-use obligation exposed.** S09 covers shared character source and target cooking, but the import-to-prefab path and visual diagnostics need to be made concrete for skeleton, root motion, attachment, alpha/shadow and style compatibility. Reimport should preserve stable skeleton/slot mappings or report a mapping conflict; it cannot overwrite deliberate prefab/material overrides silently. A typed schema alone does not show an artist why their character looks or moves wrong.

## 3. Painterly overhead game: no visible sky, deliberate light and shadow

**Create.** An artist starts an overhead scene with painted surfaces, an orthographic or elevated perspective camera, an authored directional light and fill, and optional cloud-shadow attenuation. The background can be a solid colour or authored image; no visible sky, cloud volume or full weather model is required. The scene setup names who supplies light, shadow visibility, indirect fill and any aerial/local media. A painterly style policy owns brushlike material response/palette and selected grading; V4 still owns one scene-linear-to-display path. The first playable result is a board/character interaction under the intended camera, not a beauty render from an unrelated camera.

**Change and inspect.** The artist changes palette, surface response, light angle and shadow softness while viewing a fixed camera/exposure capture. A diagnostic view answers: which source lit this receiver; is the cloud-shadow field present; which material/style closure was used; what did exposure/tone mapping do; and which target tier produced the frame? A close inspection camera may be added without changing world light or ticking another simulation. If later the game adds a visible sunrise/fog, it must select providers for the new background, direct-light and aerial outputs and declare overlapping-media composition. A fixed painted sky image cannot silently claim dynamic atmospheric illumination.

**Delivery and recovery.** Browser/mobile may use less costly shadow/filtering and texture products while preserving the same chosen light source and game rules. If the no-sky configuration still needs cloud attenuation and that provider is removed, validation reports the retained shadow consumer and offers either an authored constant attenuation/fallback or removal of that requested shadow effect. If a painterly material has no masked-shadow variant on mobile, the build identifies the material/pass/target demand; it does not silently drop the shadow. The previous revision remains usable while the team revises the style or target scope.

**First-use obligation exposed.** The architecture separates visible sky from illumination correctly, but the first-use surface needs a named no-sky lighting recipe and a simple way to inspect light/shadow provenance. Otherwise “turn sky off” could look like “turn light off,” leaving the artist to debug provider wiring.

## Minimum authoring surface implied by all three

These are bounded mechanisms, not a request for a general-purpose node editor or a full desktop GUI:

1. **Import review, mapping and explicit conversion.** A stable report and proposed source diff for hierarchy, units/axes, cameras/lights, material/texture semantics, skeleton/clips, unsupported extensions and required gameplay products. Persist source-file/node/primitive/material/skin/clip identity mapping to canonical asset IDs; record imported values separately from engine-authored overrides. Reimport shows additions, removals, ambiguous matches and override conflicts before changing accepted source. No success message that hides omitted content.
2. **A few expandable visual recipes.** Interior baseline, third-person toon and overhead no-sky lighting/style. Each expands to ordinary source records with named values, providers and ownership; no hidden mode and no claim that defaults guarantee artistic quality. Recipes should be removable without leaving private renderer state.
3. **A small target-aware visual workbench.** View the actual selected runtime; frame and inspect an object; adjust camera, light and material fields through the same revisioned operations as CLI/API; undo an authored change; see source/play/target and pending-versus-active status. Select camera, time, exposure policy and display output; orbit/walk/pan/zoom as appropriate, inspect neutral-material and shadow/light diagnostics, and capture the settings. The human interface may be a small viewer plus CLI/API. These few direct visual controls are necessary to judge composition, shading and motion; they do not require a full scene-editor suite.
4. **Source ownership and collaboration.** The asset, prefab, scene light, style policy, camera and game controller have different owners but shared stable IDs and revisioned diffs. External edits enter through explicit coherent sync. An artist can revise appearance while a programmer's play session stays pinned; a changed asset contract exposes the affected controller/prefab fields before a new session starts.
5. **Visible readiness and failure recovery.** For each preview/build, show desired versus active revision and why a required asset, shader variant, target capability or collision product is pending or failed. Preserve the last accepted image/build. A compatible fallback is authored and named; it never disguises a gameplay or required visual change.

The style boundary should stay narrow: materials expose selected shading closures and extension hooks, lights/environment provide semantic illumination and visibility, and V4 owns display. A painterly or toon look may need targeted render effects, but the design should enumerate supported passes and compatibility by target instead of promising arbitrary renderer replacement. These three traces are enough to require a pleasant visual editing path; they do not establish which GUI layout, exact recipe values, shader algorithms or device budgets will achieve it. Running sample games and human visual review are still needed.
