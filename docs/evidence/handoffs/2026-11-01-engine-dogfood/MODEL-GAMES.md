# Three paper developer walkthroughs for engine model and rendering choices

1 November 2026. Scope: bounded design walkthroughs, not implementations, benchmarks, or visual approvals. The developer needs below were derived from the game briefs before checking the engine proposals. The comparison then uses [the architecture brief](../../../design/ARCHITECTURE-BRIEF.md), [audit](../../../design/ARCHITECTURE-AUDIT.md), [system design](../../../design/SYSTEM-DESIGN.md), [composition](../../../design/COMPOSITION.md), [contracts](../../../design/CONTRACTS.md), and the environment, rendering, world/content, tools/delivery and play module notes. Those proposals are candidates; the audit's A01–A05 and J01/J02/J04 remain open. A walkthrough that can be described is not proof that the interface is selected or that a target device runs it.

## What these games ask of the developer

| Game | Needed authoring and game behavior | Presentation and delivery | Omit without loss of purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coastal exploration | Place a small traversable coast, a river mouth, a pool, a controllable character, collision, one collectible, and water contact rules. Most water can be scenic if the game declares that. | Attractive third-person desktop scene; bounded browser excerpt using the same player rules and content identities. | Full watershed simulation, ocean-wave force solver for a walking character, live solver replacement, MMO/network infrastructure. |
| Flood management | Place terrain elevations, barriers, drains, inflows and threatened buildings. Water depth/flow, arrival time and damage are player-visible rules. | Isometric planning view; reduced visual cost on mobile while the flood outcome remains the same within declared precision. | Spectral sea, volumetric clouds, photorealistic reflections, character animation, dynamic sky unless the art requires it. |
| Painterly vista | Place authored terrain/architecture, art-directed materials and lights, a movable camera, one ordinary interaction and atmospheric depth. | Stylized 3D desktop vista with a coherent browser/mobile reduction if later selected. Compare HDRI/simple air to dynamic scattering plus local fog. | Physics-based weather, every atmospheric model at once, universal renderer swap, a dynamic sky when fixed lighting serves the scene. |

The three need a stable scene/asset identity, inspectable data and errors, a game-controlled action/behavior seam, explicit model choice by world domain, shared physical and render facts where behavior depends on them, and target-specific cooked content. They do **not** imply that any chosen model must be an installable plugin. A finite typed game recipe with validated content and a few public extension hooks is a credible first composition surface. Optional build packages become justified where they demonstrably remove code/products/dependencies or admit independent provider work; more general registration should earn its complexity through a real game need.

## 1. Small third-person coastal exploration

### Initial choice and authoring

The developer chooses a perspective camera and character controller; collision/interaction, terrain, animation, audio, and a portable lit material path. The scene has three named water domains: a visually moving sea with a prescribed level and authored wave appearance, a shallow river with authored downstream velocity, and a still pool with constant level. Character contact needs wet/dry, water height and flow at foot scale; no boat or buoyancy behavior is promised. The river's motion can initially be a prescribed field rather than a mass-conserving solver because nothing in this first game uses upstream water storage or flooding. The sea may use a different visual provider. Both declare validity and interaction-scale error; subgrid ripples are visual only.

The author places height/mesh terrain, shore and bridge colliders, a player prefab, river spline/region, sea region, pool volume and a collectible shell. They can inspect each object's schema, required products and region ID. The collectible uses a game-owned `Collected` state and a small interaction command: player action → target query at named physics epoch → stable target ID and range check → rule mutation/event at a tick → sound/UI and hide or replace its render instance. This asks for ordinary component/rule extension, not a bespoke engine plugin. Physics owns achieved character motion; animation follows it and the camera reads the committed/interpolated body. There is one scene world step even if an editor camera and game camera are both visible.

### Edit, play, stop

The developer raises the shell onto a rock and adjusts its material. A source edit has a revision and impact report; cooking produces the material, collision/prefab dependency closure and target shaders. Play starts from a pinned authored revision in a **separate play world** (recommended for this workflow, pending A01 comparison). Collecting the shell changes only that play world's game state. Stop discards play-state deltas; an explicit `apply selected transform/material change` can create a new authored edit against the current source revision. A save made while playing belongs to that play session and pinned source, not to the editing world's journal. If the cook fails, the last active scene stays available, with desired versus active revision and failing dependency visible. If the editor crashes, recovery chooses a coherent source root and does not replay the collected-shell state into authored source. A01/A02 must specify these semantics before promising this loop.

### Demand change: river reaches the sea

The author extends the river to a visible mouth. A visual blend alone is a **declared visual-only edge**: the sea level and river flow need not exchange mass, and contact at the seam must have a defined query owner and transition tolerance. If the game only needs walking/wading, a prescribed/open boundary is enough: the river's downstream level is forced by the sea/tide function, and outflow is exported to a named sea sink; there is no claim that the sea responds physically. A short blending reach may be authored, but velocity, depth and wet/dry results must remain single-valued for character queries. The render seam samples the chosen authoritative geometry at foot scale, then adds decorative normal/foam detail.

This is a developer choice with a visible meaning, not a hidden optimization. A requested dynamic waterfall or overturning plunge pool is outside the first game's free-surface approximation and is rejected or deferred as a specialized model; a decorative fall may be explicitly visual-only. It must not fabricate contact/force results from a single height sample.

If the developer later adds a tide gate, upstream ponding or river reversal as gameplay, prescribed flow is outside its declared regime. They must choose a shallow-flow domain and an edge policy that transfers level and flux consistently, or intentionally accept a nonconserving game rule. A grid cell boundary is not automatically a solver boundary. The editor should report `river–sea edge: prescribed/open; sea level forces river; river flux exported; no sea feedback`, and reject an attempt to label it conservative without the required state/flux mapping and time step. This is the concrete A04 gap. It does not require the engine to implement every fluid technique now.

An underwater exploration request would be a new capability: swimming occupancy, potentially multiple water interfaces along a ray, underwater light/air transport, audio and camera rules. A height-only free-surface query cannot express a cave pool or overhang with multiple vertical intersections. The existing simple contact path stays valid; the new game feature names a volume/occupancy query and medium boundary. The engine should neither silently reinterpret a surface query as a volume nor demand that all simple water use a universal volume API.

### Browser excerpt and failures

The desktop showcase selects higher draw distance, shadows, reflections and perhaps dynamic sky/clouds. The browser excerpt selects a bounded region, reduced resident texture/geometry detail and a cheaper sea appearance. It retains collectible, collision and wading semantics and the same stable content IDs. The browser build has a finite content/profile closure: each material, geometry class, shadow/reflection pass and allowed quality transition demands shader variants and cooked products. Missing required collision or a shader needed by the excerpt blocks admission with an actionable dependency path; an optional water highlight can use a declared fallback. A03 is not closed merely by having a general asset manifest. Browser packaging records the profile lock, source/product hashes and startup/download budget as unmeasured until tested.

**Value:** the proposal's single world authority, region/product transactions and explicit capability/output selection fit this game. **Avoidable complexity:** an engine-wide plugin registry, a universal physically coupled water solver, arbitrary live model replacement, and a wholly replaceable renderer do not help the first playable coast. **Open contract:** river edge semantics and query ownership; play-world isolation/apply-back; source change-set authority; finite shader demand/readiness for both builds.

## 2. Isometric flood management

### Initial choice and authoring

This game makes water state a rule. The developer authors terrain elevations and land-use cells, inlet hydrographs, barrier segments, pumps/drains, damage thresholds and an isometric camera. One depth-averaged shallow-flow **solver domain** is a plausible model: cell water depth `h` and horizontal flux `h u` evolve under declared rainfall, inlet, outlet, barrier and terrain terms. Many streamed terrain/content cells can partition its storage and activation without becoming independent solvers or introducing cross-model exchange. The exact numerical method, grid spacing and substep remain detailed choices; the game must set tolerated errors for arrival time, maximum depth and conservation before qualification. Buildings sample committed depth and duration; their damage state is authoritative and saved. A graphical water shader never supplies those values.

The developer's first behavior is `place sandbag`: pointer picks a stable ground/barrier edge in a named view/scene epoch → game validates cost and placement → world operation installs a barrier/collision or flow-boundary change at a tick barrier → water step uses the new boundary → UI displays predicted/observed inundation. A temporary drag preview may draw an overlay without modifying the solver. The engine needs pick, command and inspection contracts, plus a way to author this game's domain data. It does not need sandbags in core. Only the flow owner advances water; rendering reads a committed depth/velocity field and may interpolate between ticks.

### Edit, play, stop and a model boundary

The developer changes a road crest and presses play. The play session pins terrain/collider/flow-grid products as one coherent revision. The first simulation step cannot combine old elevation with new barrier geometry. On stop, flooded cells and lost buildings are play state and are discarded unless the developer explicitly promotes a chosen authored parameter, such as inlet rate, through an operation against current source. A failed grid cook keeps the prior active play-ready revision and shows the affected terrain/barrier product. External terrain files arriving in several writes are staged as one declared change set before validation; a watcher must not briefly run a half-updated flood basin. This is A01/A02 plus C05's existing transactional principle.

The scene includes an estuary tile at the downstream border. The author selects a **prescribed/open tidal boundary** when the sea is only an external water-level source: time-indexed sea level drives the flood solver's boundary; outgoing flux is accounted for as export; no reverse claim is made about sea response. This still uses one conservative flood domain; the external sea is a boundary condition, not a second solver. If a later storm-surge game requires two-way sea feedback, that is a new design need: compare one enlarged shallow-flow domain with a specified coupled-domain exchange and reject unsupported pairs. `Visual seam` is invalid here because building damage depends on water crossing the border. A channel that changes model resolution within the domain needs a conservative remap with declared error; merely sharing a colour or height texture is insufficient. A04 must give the author an explicit admissible edge type and diagnostic mass balance, without building generic cross-solver exchange ahead of need.

### Mobile reduction and failures

The mobile profile may reduce water mesh tessellation, foam, transparency layers, reflected scenery, shadow quality, UI density and nonessential animation. It can render a simpler colour/depth overlay from the same authoritative flood field. It must not lower the flow grid, change the tick or drop wet/dry cells merely as a graphics quality setting: a different authoritative resolution can change who floods, so that needs an explicit **game model** and save/score compatibility decision. If this game deliberately offers a coarser mobile simulation, its precision bounds and divergent outcomes are stated, tested and treated as a distinct rules profile. The same save cannot be reinterpreted silently across model resolutions.

If the device is under thermal pressure, optional visual work can retire at a safe boundary. Mandatory solver steps have an overload/loading policy rather than silent frame-dependent advancement. A paused/suspended app checkpoints a committed flood state, inlet clock and game actions, while render caches rebuild on resume. A missing terrain collider/flow product blocks the affected region; an unloaded neighbor is a closed or prescribed boundary, never assumed to be empty/dry. The package may omit atmosphere, ocean spectral shaders and character assets altogether; its finite shader/product closure still includes every mobile water-overlay and barrier state used during play.

**Value:** explicit physical authority, stable identities, tick barriers, headless execution and visual independence are essential here. **Avoidable complexity:** the desktop sea renderer and dynamic atmosphere need not be linked; automatic provider optimization must not swap to decorative water. **Open contract:** admissible water-domain edges with conserved/exported quantities; model-resolution and save compatibility; edit/play/source transaction semantics; profile product and shader closure. Numerical stability, mobile cost and visual clarity remain unmeasured.

## 3. Painterly 3D vista with atmospheric depth

### Initial choice and authoring

The developer builds a short first-person path through a painted-looking grove to a lit tower. They author terrain/mesh assets, a limited palette, material closures, key lights, distant silhouettes and a `light lantern` interaction. The behavior is ordinary: action and target query → game rule changes lantern state → one authored light and sound event update under stable identity. The developer first chooses a fixed HDRI/environment image plus authored Sun/ambient terms and simple height/local fog. This meets a fixed-time vista if the artistic intent is stable. The HDRI supplies background/illumination according to what the provider actually exports; it does **not** imply dynamic aerial extinction or physical solar attenuation. A local fog volume supplies declared extinction and in-scatter; if a sky background remains visible through it, the compositing path needs distance/segment semantics.

The material style can use a named painterly shading closure (quantized response, brush/albedo texture treatment, authored rim/fill) inside V3's material/light contract. V4 still owns scene-linear to output/display transform; the painterly closure must not smuggle a second tone mapper into every object. This asks for a material/style extension point and perhaps a post effect, not an arbitrary complete renderer replacement. If the style later demands fundamentally different visibility or pass ordering, compare a distinct pipeline then, using an actual target image/workload. J02's question should be settled at that level.

### Edit/play and style iteration

The author changes a tower material, views the edit in an editor camera and starts play from a pinned scene revision. Lighting the lantern in play changes only session state. Stopping discards that state; an authored initial-on value can be applied explicitly. Shader compilation for the new painterly closure is part of the content/profile demand before a capture is called ready. If the shader fails, the previous active material stays visible and the diagnostic points to the closure/variant and content object. A quick code change to the closure requires rebuild/restart or another explicitly supported workflow; dynamic binary hot reload is not required. A code restart should restore the pinned source and inspectable camera/selection state if promised, but must not merge the prior play world's lantern state into source. J04/A01/A03 determine the exact handoff.

### Demand change: moving Sun and valley fog

The director now wants a visible sunrise, changing aerial colour and ground fog wrapping the tower. A fixed HDRI cannot generate time-consistent Sun direction, daylight attenuation and aerial perspective by itself. The game chooses a dynamic scattering/sky provider for global air and a local fog provider for the valley. Source facts for Sun direction/intensity/time remain one authority; V3 evaluates them consistently for materials, fog and sky. Quality may use precomputed LUTs or another selected method, but the method is not the contract.

The difficult ray crosses **overlapping** global air and local fog. Whole-interval pairs `(T, S)` independently preintegrated by each provider cannot generally be combined into the exact result because the distribution of fog absorption relative to air scattering matters. The author must choose one of: (a) ordered disjoint ray segments, where pair composition is valid; (b) coefficient/source access for joint integration over overlap; (c) one provider that jointly preintegrates that overlap; or (d) a declared approximate layering policy with expected failure conditions. The current C08 sentence requiring coefficient combination conflicts with allowing only independently aggregated pairs, as A05 records. A profile validator should reject a claim of exact overlapping composition when the selected providers expose only those aggregate pairs. The art team may accept approximate layering for this painterly scene, but it must be visible as a choice and tested at the tower/valley views.

The sunrise switch invalidates relevant sky, lighting, reflection and temporal histories by generation. It does not force a full renderer swap or a new weather simulation. A simple air provider remains appropriate in the earlier fixed-light version. On mobile/browser, the developer may bake distant light/air for a fixed time or choose lower sample counts, while preserving lantern interaction; a moving-sun version needs a source/representation that actually honors time. The target cook includes both style closure variants and atmosphere/fog passes required by supported transitions, or rejects an unavailable live switch before play. Captures state camera, time, exposure/output transform and profile, since a pleasing screenshot alone cannot establish consistent lighting across motion.

**Value:** one light registry, material closure selection, separated sky/illumination/aerial outputs and explicit media composition support the art direction. **Avoidable complexity:** no mandatory meteorology, cloud volume, fully replaceable renderer, or all-platform dynamic atmosphere in the first vista. **Open contract:** exact versus approximate overlap representation; finite style/atmosphere shader demand; edit/play/code restart; target-specific visual and performance qualification.

## Cross-game simplifications and decisions to carry forward

1. **Start with a finite typed game recipe and selected extension seams.** Declare capabilities, chosen provider/model by domain, target tier and content requirements in one inspectable lock. Keep public hooks for game components/rules, material closures, environmental queries and a bounded render pass interface where needed. Compare optional packages only when build exclusion, external contribution or independently replaceable implementation has value. No general runtime plugin machine-code loader or universal renderer swap follows from these games.
2. **Make model validity and domain edges author-visible.** A water region names state, queries, time/scale/error and edge policy: closed, prescribed/open, one-way, conservative coupled, or visual-only. A `sea`, `river` or `pool` label is content vocabulary, not an algorithm. Reject unsupported requests rather than converting physical gameplay to an image effect. Define a specialized volume capability only when a game asks for swimming/underwater occupancy.
3. **Keep visual reductions separate from game rules.** Mobile/browser may spend less on geometry, shadows, effects and history while retaining the same world step and authoritative fields. A different physics/water resolution is a game variant with migration/compatibility policy, not a hidden quality tier.
4. **Use the simplest atmosphere that supplies requested outputs.** Fixed HDRI/authored light can be valid for fixed scenes; dynamic scattering earns its cost for moving Sun/aerial conditions. The baseline accepts disjoint integrated segments or compatible joint coefficient integration; approximate layering is a labelled art policy. One `(T,S)` pair per overlapping provider is insufficient for an exact claim.
5. **Treat ordinary development as an architectural path.** Source change sets, pinned play sessions, stop/apply-back, code restart, finite shader/product demand and package errors are needed before an agent or human can trust an edit/capture loop. The proposals' revision/transaction machinery is valuable, but A01/A02/A03 need concrete choices before their interfaces freeze.

These traces do not select a water discretization, a scattering algorithm, a renderer library, minimum devices or acceptance thresholds. The design next needs contracts for A01–A05 and the narrower D0 choices from the audit. Then one complete representative creation/change/port path per game can qualify the predictions, with headless semantics, rendered motion and named target devices kept as separate evidence.
