# Phased accepted-source admission — 3 November 2026

`NativeSourceStore::admit` now holds one admission permit across its entire candidate attempt. Pin registration and release take that permit, preserving the single writer's planning and pin-order state. Existing accepted leases copy through the SQLite-core mutex only. Admission releases that core mutex after each completed large-blob chunk and tiny-file batch, and while it reads and hashes staged source bytes. It reacquires the core for each bounded SQL phase. The final manifest stage and root/outcome transaction remain serialized on the core. The first-profile store remains append-only, so a pinned A lease can read its immutable blob while B's complete but unselected chunks accumulate. One reusable admission scratch vector belongs to the owner; its active length is at most 4 MiB per chunk, while retained capacity depends on allocator growth. The change adds no fresh scratch allocation per chunk or world-tick work. SQLite's internal allocations are not measured here.

The focused interleaving fixture holds an A pin and stages a 12 MiB B. Immediately after B's first blob chunk commits and the core mutex is released, a reader thread copies and verifies A's scene and observes A still selected. Only after that reader reports completion does B stage the remaining chunks and commit. A later root query sees B. The precommit stage-kill child now stops the **phased** public admission pipeline after B's complete manifest but before final root commit; fresh open sees A and no B outcome, then public retry succeeds. A test-only VFS fault is armed immediately before the phased final transaction and reports an error after WAL COMMIT sync with an A lease live. The owner quarantines later lease SQL, and fresh open resolves B's exact keyed outcome and A's retained pin. The full repository gate and opt-in admitted A/B build pass.

The [serialized baseline](SOURCE-OWNER-LOCAL-COST-2026-11-03.md) originally observed 86.750–89.134 ms reader wait plus copy behind one 12 MiB admission. Repeating its unchanged core fixture alongside the final phased code gave **90.423, 85.272 and 89.202 ms** reader wait plus copy, with **85.348, 81.259 and 85.081 ms** writer time after preflight. Under the final phased path, three local runs sampled ten small A copies each over B admission. Their maximum copy times were **27.672, 23.477 and 23.958 ms**; B wall times were **94.938, 89.019 and 88.942 ms**. The reader slept 1 ms between timed copies to sample different writer phases. Separate three-run public 12 MiB admission timings without concurrent copies were **82.630, 86.291 and 87.120 ms**. The serialized writer-after-preflight timer and phased B wall timer cover different intervals, so they cannot establish a writer-speed change. These are not matched repetitions under controlled host load. The decrease in observed reader wait is consistent with chunk-level interleaving, but no p95/p99, starvation bound or throughput improvement is established by ten samples per run.

A second slice keeps the admission permit through stored-blob verification but copies each committed SQLite chunk into the reusable scratch vector under the core mutex, hashes it after releasing the mutex, and publishes the complete marker in one final transaction. The permit excludes cooperating writes to those incomplete chunks. A focused test corrupts a committed chunk before verification: admission reports a corrupt store, keeps A selected with no B outcome, and restages B successfully on retry. In three 64 MiB local samples before this split, 36–37 A reads per run had observed maximum copy times of 52.995, 51.889 and 52.934 ms; B wall times were 291.509, 287.636 and 338.471 ms. After the split, 59–60 A reads per run had maxima of 16.503, 17.461 and 18.444 ms; B wall times were 292.662, 292.694 and 298.941 ms. The read distribution was sampled with 1 ms sleeps and is not a p95/p99 or starvation guarantee. Host load and sample counts differ, so the B times do not establish a throughput change. The separate 100,000-entry, 16.4 MB manifest stream still held the core for 97.862 ms in one optimized run. The full repository gate and opt-in sandboxed admitted A/B build were rerun after the verification split and passed.

A third slice streams the canonical manifest through the same bounded writer, holding the core only for each committed chunk and stored-chunk copy; serialization and hashing run outside it. The final publication still checks all direct file references and the root/outcome CAS in one SQLite transaction. Three isolated optimized 100,000-entry manifest-staging probes sampled 35, 31 and 31 old A copies. Their observed maximum copy times were 11.932, 11.306 and 12.143 ms, while staging wall times were 107.085, 95.352 and 95.494 ms. The earlier monolithic core helper took 97.862 ms in one run, but the fixtures differ and establish no staging throughput change. The synthetic manifest references 256 repeated one-byte hashes and is staged without publication, so it does not qualify the final 100,000-reference transaction or a full maximum-profile candidate.

The longest remaining core holds include final direct-file closure/publication and individual SQLite chunk transactions. A larger source, blocked checkpoint or writer reacquisition can lengthen waits. Repeated writer mutex reacquisition could also starve a reader. The admission permit still blocks new pin registration and pin release until the candidate finishes; only already-pinned reads benefit from this slice. Source capture/validation, peak allocations/RSS, physical WAL/main overlap, reader-blocked checkpoint behavior under sustained edits, other platforms and whole source-to-build latency remain open. The selected [concurrency design](../../design/research/SOURCE-PINNED-READER-CONCURRENCY-2026-11-03.md) keeps those gates visible.
