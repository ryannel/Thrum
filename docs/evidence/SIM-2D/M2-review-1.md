## Cost and quality assessment

The old measurements openly show the regressions: Mac serial Crowd at 2,000 and 5,000 is 31–41% slower than M1; Chrome Crowd is 62–82% slower across all three sizes at identical accepted-neighbour counts. [The investigation](M2/performance.md) explains the added eight-pass radix ordering and higher measured build cost, identifies predicate and steering work with native and Chrome samples, and explicitly declines to assign unsupported causal percentages. Those explanations agree with the rows and code. Parallel results are retained regardless of outcome. Dense 10,000-body rows retain approximately 469,268 Crowd and 474,752 Mixed neighbour visits per tick; the record does not hide dense work behind a sparse fixture.

The observed Mac serial 10,000-body ticks are about 9.7–9.9 ms, forced-parallel about 2.1–2.4 ms; Chrome is about 13.9–14.5 ms. These remain shared-host historical baselines, not a quiet-machine qualification or a pass/fail budget. The index and ordering implementation meet the correctness quality bar on the reviewed proofs. The remaining Must Fix is that both required cost validators must accept evidence for the integrated build before M2 can be Done.
