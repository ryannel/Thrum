# PUDDLES design proofs

Reproductions run while [the design](../../../plans/PUDDLES/DESIGN.md#check-that-it-can-work) was written, on 14
November 2026. They are evidence for the design's choices and the source of the bounds in M1 and M2 of
[the done-when file](../../../plans/PUDDLES/DONE.json). They are not engine code and no engine test depends on
them. All six are arithmetic in f64 on small grids and sections; none is a GPU or a visual measurement.

Run `python3 puddle_proof.py`, about two seconds; its output is `puddle-proof.txt`.

| Part | What it shows |
|---|---|
| 1 | A bed 3.2 m square at 5 cm cells, with three bowls, a rut, a slope and small relief, is cut into 20 hollows in a tree of 30 nodes by one flood from its pits. Rain of 30 mm/h fills them and a loss of 40 mm/h empties them: 10 merges and 10 partings. At every merge and parting and every fortieth step, the cells a point test calls wet (the level of the cell's hollow stands above the cell's bed) are the cells a flood from the pits reaches: none differs. Water in equals water held, lost and spilled over the edge to 1.5e-12 l in 199 l. The largest change of a hollow's level in a step that holds a merge or a parting falls with the step, 1.76, 0.85 and 0.21 mm at steps of 1, 1/4 and 1/16 s, so a merge and a parting are continuous; the largest change in any other step is a one-cell hollow taking a spill. Declared as basins with a seed each, 10 pairs of these fills would overlap and be refused |
| 2 | A pothole of 4 l with six times its own area draining to it fills in 8 minutes at 10 mm/h on paving and in 16 on earth that soaks 5 mm/h; on sand that soaks 60 mm/h no puddle forms at any rate tried. 20 mm of water takes 67 hours to evaporate at 0.3 mm/h, so a demo's drying rate is a control |
| 3 | Water's waves under gravity and surface tension have a least group speed of 17.8 cm/s at 4.4 cm and a least phase speed of 23.1 cm/s at 1.7 cm. A 3 mm drop's momentum, spread over a crater three drop radii across, raises rings 0.30, 0.20 and 0.14 mm high after 0.2, 0.4 and 0.8 s, with a calm disc inside them that grows toward the least group speed (16.0 cm/s by 0.8 s) and more crests with age. Those rings carry 1.1% of the drop's energy; over one drop radius they would carry 29.5%. On 5 mm of water the rings are 0.76 of their deep-water height, on 2 mm 0.41 and on 1 mm 0.25, and never more than 9% of the depth limit. Marshall and Palmer's drops at 2, 10 and 50 mm/h number 14, 152 and 918 of 2 mm and over on each square metre each second, and by superposition give a mean square slope of 0.001, 0.009 and 0.065. At 64 rings a square metre a second about 4 rings lie over a point |
| 4 | With a film returning a share `p` of the ground's light to it, wet over dry albedo is `(1 − R)(1 − p) / (1 − p a)`: 0.55 at an albedo of 0.1 and 0.83 at 0.8 for `p` = 0.47, and 0.32 and 0.67 for `p` = 0.7. A brown's saturation rises from 0.53 to 0.57 and 0.59 |
| 5 | Two stores, surface water and pores, through ten minutes of 10 mm/h. On paving the sheen comes after 125 s in the open, 251 s where half the rain reaches and 504 s where a quarter does; after the rain it is gone in 10 minutes and the pores are half dry 2 minutes later. On earth whose pores take 3 mm no sheen forms and the pores are half dry after 21 minutes. Ground that 0.4 of the rain reaches, read between the stores for a quarter and a half, is off by 0.10 mm of film and 0.23 of the pores at worst; read as 0.4 of the open store it is off by 0.60 |
| 6 | An interaction level with a ladder of depths from 5 mm to 160 mm and the deep operator from 320 mm, with surface tension, on waves of 8 cm to 1 m over 5 mm to 1 m of water: the blended step's phase speed is within 2.0% of the true speed at steps of one and of two ticks, never grows, and loses at most 0.2% and 1.9% of a wave's height a second. A step of eight ticks keeps the speed within 2.2% and loses up to 42% a second, so the level never takes one. An 8 cm wave on 30 mm of water travels at 36 cm/s and a blend of two ticks misstates it by 2.7% of its height. The water under a foot, 0.6 N s, raises rings 3.4 mm high a quarter of a metre off after half a second |

From memory and not re-read: Marshall and Palmer's drop sizes, Atlas's fall speed, and the form of Lekner and
Dorf's model, which part 4 derives again as a series of returns inside the film. Lagarde quotes about 0.68 for
their model on asphalt where this form gives 0.55, so the original holds a term this one lacks.
