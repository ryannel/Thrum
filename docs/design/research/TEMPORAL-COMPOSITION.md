# Temporal reconstruction and scene composition — bounded research

31 October 2026. Research for the [custom-engine brief](../ARCHITECTURE-BRIEF.md) and proposed [rendering family](../modules/RENDERING.md). Scope: one committed 3D world viewed through orthographic/top-down/isometric and perspective third-/first-person cameras on Mac/PC, browser WebGPU and native mobile. This is a contract recommendation, not a selected upscaler, measured implementation or visual acceptance. The parent rendering draft was read before this study, so the design challenge is not blind.

## Decision that this study can change

**Recommend a provider-neutral reconstruction input contract and an ordered scene-linear composition contract.** Treat a temporal image upscaler as optional per view. Keep native-resolution spatial AA as a qualified profile. Preserve histories for the particular view *and effect*, not for an undifferentiated camera image. The difficult producer is previous *presented* geometry/shading state: current and previous animation, morph, wind, water and particle samples must refer to the two image sample times. Two adjacent simulation ticks alone do not necessarily describe those samples when presentation interpolates snapshots. If a producer cannot reconstruct its previous sample, it declares invalidity/reactivity, and the profile must still look acceptable spatially.

This follows AMD's current FSR upscaler requirements for depth, unjittered current-to-previous motion, exposure, reactive and transparency/composition inputs; AMD explicitly recommends coverage for opaque, masked and blended pixels and notes that blended content often has no depth/motion footprint. [AMD FSR 3.1.6, “Input resources,” “Motion vectors,” “Coverage,” “Reactive mask,” “Transparency and composition mask,” “Exposure”](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fsr_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler/). Apple's MetalFX presentation likewise specifies jittered colour plus depth and motion, current-to-previous vectors in render-pixel space, a reset on cuts and a temporal pass before disruptive post effects. [Apple WWDC22, “Boost performance with MetalFX Upscaling,” temporal integration segment](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10103/?time=556). These are *shipped vendor integration contracts*, not evidence that either library is available or suitable on every target. Native MetalFX is an Apple-specific challenger; browser and mobile paths may use different algorithms or spatial AA.

### Analytical operating model and why this baseline

**Representation.** For each view-ray pixel, use a sorted sequence of opaque/transparent surface events and bounded medium intervals in scene-linear radiance. The visible opaque/masked first hit has one depth, stable instance/material identity, coverage and prior-point correspondence. A transparent event carries its own depth, alpha, reflection/refraction policy and motion confidence. A medium interval carries optical depth and source radiance or its preintegrated `(T,L)` pair. This representation follows the causality of transport along a ray and can be reduced to a single opaque hit plus sky for cheap profiles. It avoids forcing atmosphere, cloud, water and VFX to each run an unrelated full-screen “fogged” colour operation. It does **not** imply storing a deep image for every pixel: sorted passes and bounded transient layers may evaluate the same operator. For overlapping transparent layers, an explicitly approximate sorted or weighted method is needed; one nearest layer is only accurate when it dominates coverage/opacity.

**Motion operator.** Let a rendered material point at local coordinate `q` have deformation `D(q,t)` (including skin/morph/vertex displacement), instance transform `M(t)`, and view/projection `V_v(t), P_v(t)` for view `v`. With unjittered projection, `x_v(q,t) = pixel(P_v(t) V_v(t) M(t) D(q,t))`. The engine motion vector is `m_v = x_v(q,t_prev_present) − x_v(q,t_present)` in pixels of the current render target; both projections must preserve the same physical point `q`. Jitter `j_v(t)` offsets *sampling*, not physical motion, so raster colour uses `x+j` while vector generation uses `x`. A provider that changes topology, stochastic cutout realization or particle identity cannot solve this correspondence by retaining only `M`; it must reject affected history. A screen reflection or refracted background has a different apparent correspondence from the reflecting/refracting surface, so one vector is knowingly approximate. [AMD FSR 3.1.6, vector and jitter definitions](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fsr_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler/); [Epic TSR, WPO previous-frame evaluation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/temporal-super-resolution-in-unreal-engine).

**Transport operator.** For a ray segment `[a,b]` parameterized by metres from the camera, `T(a,b)=exp(-∫_a^b σ_t(s) ds)` componentwise and `L(a,b)=∫_a^b T(a,s) j(s) ds`, where `σ_t ≥ 0` is extinction in m⁻¹ and `j` is in-scattered/emitted radiance per metre. Applying the segment to the radiance behind it gives `C_a=L(a,b)+T(a,b) C_b`. For adjacent near/far segments, `T_comb=T_near T_far` and `L_comb=L_near+T_near L_far`; associativity permits separate atmosphere/cloud/local-fog providers **only if** they partition the ray or explicitly account for overlap. In an overlapping interval, the closure is `σ_total=Σ_i σ_i` and `j_total=Σ_i j_i` (with each `j_i` evaluated under the *combined* extinction and agreed lighting); independently fogging the final colour for each medium is generally wrong. An opaque hit at `b` supplies `C_b=L_surface`; an alpha surface at `b` instead supplies `C_b=α L_surface+(1−α) C_behind` before the foreground segment, with refraction replacing the simple behind lookup when enabled. A water interface changes the medium definition on each side and its refracted ray direction; underwater ground must use an underwater path length, not camera-to-opaque terrain depth through air. These are the minimal mathematical closure and assumptions of single-scattering/preintegrated real-time media; multiple scattering, wavelength dependence, shadowing and anisotropy are supplied as explicit approximations in `j`, never claimed solved by this algebra. [Hillaire 2020, equations 1–4 and §5.4](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf).

**Temporal operator.** A temporal candidate samples history at `h=x+m_v` and computes `C_out=(1−w) C_current+w C_history_validated`, after compensating for pre-exposure. `w` is reduced to zero for a cut, out-of-bounds `h`, changed identity, or incompatible depth/coverage; shading discontinuity or reactive classification lowers it. For like-for-like geometry, a depth check compares previous depth reprojected from the *current point* against history depth in the same metric and with a slope/precision-aware tolerance; a fixed device-Z epsilon fails across orthographic/perspective and depth range. Local colour bounds can prevent conspicuous outliers but cannot create unavailable disoccluded samples. The NVIDIA paper explicitly identifies this insufficiency, including shadow/reflection motion that disagrees with surface vectors. [NVIDIA adaptive TAA, §2–3](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2018-08_Adaptive-Temporal-Antialiasing/adaptive-temporal-antialiasing-preprint.pdf). This is a contract model, not a claim that every vendor implements this exact filter.

**Derived cost and error envelope.** For `P` render pixels, `Q` output pixels, `K` active transparent/medium layers per pixel and `S` volume samples per represented interval, the simple spatial baseline needs O(P) visibility/shading and O(Q) spatial reconstruction; sorted layered composition adds O(PK) evaluations once layer order is known, and marched media O(PKS) in a naive full-resolution implementation. Froxel/LUT methods replace much of that with a view-volume preparation cost plus O(P) lookups, at finite angular/depth interpolation error. Temporal reconstruction adds O(Q) history reads/writes and persistent O(Q) colour history per active view/algorithm, plus at least O(P) depth/velocity/mask production and any extra previous-deformation evaluation. With `N` retained views and `H_i` effect-specific histories, residency grows as Σ_view[O(Q_v)+Σ_i O(H_i,v)], not a constant tied to one main camera. Any unexplained lower-cost claim must account for the removed passes and resources. Approximation error rises when projected deformation, occlusion, specular/refraction or volume illumination changes faster than the available samples; no accumulation weight can recover a never-observed feature. Orthographic projection changes vector/depth formulas but not the contract. Numeric error and frame-time ceilings require named hardware/content; these symbolic bounds are design predictions, not measurements.

**Reasoned alternatives.** Native-resolution spatial AA/MSAA (where the shading path permits it) has no temporal ghosts or history residency, but cannot integrate thin/subpixel or noisy shading over time. A simple TAA can accumulate those details at O(Q) history cost but inherits all correspondence failures and may blur on motion. A vendor temporal upscaler can lower shading resolution but has stricter input/capability/quality coupling; it cannot be the only way to render a game view. Full deep transparent motion/visibility, per-effect temporal reconstruction and adaptive ray samples can improve hard cases, but their extra layers, memory or ray capability are not justified as a portable baseline. Thus the **provisional baseline** is scene-linear depth-ordered composition at native rendering resolution with spatial AA, with well-defined temporal inputs made available for qualified per-profile reconstruction. The cross-effect scene tests whether the derived limitations are perceptually tolerable at each target, not which causal representation to invent.

## Recommended contracts and owners

1. **Times and motion.** F1 chooses monotonic `t_present` for the image and retains `t_prev_present` for *that view's last accepted image*, plus committed simulation ticks and an origin epoch. P4, E1 deformation providers, V5 emitters and material animation expose evaluable current/previous samples or a validity/low-confidence classification. If a view was skipped for several frames, the previous evaluation must reach back to its own last accepted sample; a two-frame global pose buffer is insufficient. Stateful providers therefore retain bounded sample history or invalidate that view; time-analytic wind/waves can reevaluate at the prior time if their parameters/source epoch are preserved. V2 rasterizes a pixel's visible current point and projects its corresponding previous point through the previous **unjittered** view-projection. Define the engine vector as `previous_pixel_xy − current_pixel_xy` in render-target pixels, origin at top left; X right, Y down. It includes camera and object motion. Resolution changes require normalized-coordinate conversion or reset, since previous and current pixel grids differ. Explicit adapters convert this to each vendor's units/sign; test with a translating checkerboard in both camera projections. Camera-only motion is insufficient for skin, morph, leaf wind, displacement water or moving particle cards. When the previous point no longer exists (birth, topology/LOD replacement, teleport, stream-in) validity is false; do not invent rigid motion. AMD's vector convention and coverage requirements are the evidence for this choice. [AMD FSR 3.1.6, “Motion vectors” and “Coverage”](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fsr_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler/); [Epic TSR, “Animation World Position Offset”](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/temporal-super-resolution-in-unreal-engine).
2. **Depth, coverage and layers.** P2/F3 publish handedness, world origin, near/far and depth encoding once; all consumers receive a depth descriptor (projection kind, reversed/ordinary Z, finite/infinite far, clip convention) plus metres along the view ray for composition, rather than interpreting raw device Z independently. Opaque/masked first-hit depth and velocity are mandatory for temporal providers. A masked leaf's covered samples get its actual displaced depth/motion; changing cutout coverage gets coverage-change/reactive classification. V3 transparent water/glass and V5 particles separately describe nearest meaningful depth, alpha/coverage, velocity confidence and reactive/composition intent. A blended pixel may contain water, its reflection and the ground beneath; one depth and vector cannot encode all three. Material/profile policy therefore chooses which layer supplies primary reprojection, marks the mismatch reactive, or reconstructs layers separately. [Epic TSR, “TSR and Translucency”](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/temporal-super-resolution-in-unreal-engine); [AMD FSR 3.1.6, “Reactive mask” and “Transparency and composition mask”](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fsr_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler/).
3. **Scene-linear composition.** V1's frame graph must have one explicit scene-colour owner and pass ordering agreed by V3, E1 and V5. Each medium segment reports transmittance `T` (dimensionless RGB) and in-scattered radiance `L` in the declared scene-linear colour basis, evaluated over a bounded view-ray interval in metres at `t_present`. Equivalently, an unintegrated provider reports extinction `σ_t` in m⁻¹ and source radiance per metre. The segment operator is `C_front = L + T · C_behind`; compose far to near, split at surfaces and volume boundaries. Opaque surface radiance terminates the ray; sky is the terminal radiance only on rays without an opaque/terminal surface. Clouds are bounded participating layers, with depth/occlusion against opaque geometry; atmosphere before a cloud and behind it is accounted once. Translucent water/glass mix surface reflection/refraction with content behind at their actual depths; underwater fog begins below the water interface. Spray/particles sit in the correct interval. An atmosphere, local fog and cloud provider must declare whether its output already contains another medium, so no full-screen fog pass double attenuates it. This algebra is a synthesis from Hillaire's radiative-transfer equation, not a claim of exact production pass order. [Hillaire 2020, equations 1–4](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf); [Epic Sky Atmosphere, “Sky Rendering Options”](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sky-atmosphere?application_version=4.27).
4. **Reconstruction input and order.** V4 receives complete jittered scene-linear colour before tone mapping, render-resolution opaque depth/velocity, declared transparent depth and coverage/reactivity, current exposure and pre-exposure metadata, view identity, projection/depth descriptors and discontinuity flags. It also receives a *composition signature* of included effects and post effects; V1 rejects a graph where an included layer is added only after the reconstruction that is expected to stabilize it. Reconstruct once for that input, then apply motion blur/film grain, output transform and UI under a declared profile. An intentional after-upscale layer is separately antialiased/composited and cannot inherit missing motion from the main upscaler. The precise order of depth of field and translucent layers is a per-provider design choice that must be qualified together. [Apple WWDC22, temporal pass ordering](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10103/?time=556); [AMD FSR 3.1.6, camera jitter and input resources](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fsr_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler/).
5. **Exposure and history.** V4 owns image-AA/upscaler history keyed by stable view ID, algorithm, output surface and quality epoch. V3/E1/V5 own *their own* shadow, reflection, volume or particle temporal histories; V1 supplies persistent-resource lifecycle and graph barriers, while P2/F2 broadcast discontinuity causes. A history record includes previous matrices/jitter, sampled presentation time, render/output dimensions, exposure/pre-exposure, origin epoch, content/material versions and projection mode. History validation uses geometric depth/identity/coverage and shading-change confidence, then clamps or rejects where correspondence fails. Exposure adaptation is a current-view metering policy; temporal provider receives the same exposure convention as later tone mapping so history can be rescaled or reset when lighting changes abruptly. A camera cut, view reuse for another target, projection-mode change, incompatible resize/provider switch, origin rebase, device loss and substantial content replacement invalidate the affected histories. Gradual sun/exposure changes should rescale/validate rather than cause constant full resets; a lightning flash or fast spotlight may reject volume history locally. [AMD FSR 3.1.6, exposure and camera cuts](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fsr_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler/); [Epic Volumetric Fog, “Temporal Reprojection”](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/volumetric-fog?application_version=4.27).
6. **Per-view/off lifecycle.** The primary camera, map, editor, reflection and capture view receive separate histories only if a temporal algorithm is selected for that view. A secondary view may instead render spatially, at a bounded cadence/resolution; it must never borrow the primary history or advance world state. F2 declares which histories and intermediate buffers survive a hidden view, suspend, viewport change or profile switch; inactive histories retire after GPU fences under a budget. Disabling temporal reconstruction retires jitter sequence, history and velocity-only work when no other consumer needs them, while a native-resolution spatial path remains valid. A volume or denoiser that still needs motion retains its *own declared* inputs. This is a proposed lifecycle; no platform cost is claimed. [Unity URP 15 AA manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/kr/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal%4015.0/manual/anti-aliasing.html) shows why view composition must be qualified, not assumed.

## Cross-effect execution and rejection proof

Use one small scene rather than disconnected component demos: a moving third-person character with skinned face/hands and morphs; wind-deformed alpha foliage casting shadows; a shoreline with displaced water, underwater ground, refracted/reflected highlights and spray; glass and blended particles; sky, moving cloud layer, local fog, sun and a flashing local light. A second orthographic/top-down view runs simultaneously, then the main camera changes to first person. Stream a shore cell and material revision while it moves. Run native Mac/PC, a WebGPU browser and representative iOS/Android app builds with the same semantic content, but separately chosen providers. Include spatial/native-resolution reference captures and motion clips, not only stills.

| Transition / observation | Contract stressed; reject a candidate when… |
| --- | --- |
| Camera track, stationary and fast pan in both projections | A static checker and thin ropes should stay fixed under jitter; moving leaves, skin and water should not trail behind rigid velocity. Reject for persistent ghosts, shimmering geometry or projection-specific vector sign errors. Inspect depth, vector, reactive and history-rejection overlays. |
| Shore crossing and camera underwater | Sky/cloud/land/water/spray occlude in depth order; aerial perspective reaches surfaces at actual depth and underwater medium starts at interface. Reject for double haze, cloud through foreground, water-ground depth mismatch, or particle ghosts surviving contact. |
| Cut, FOV/projection change, resize, switch to map/secondary view | Histories reset or resample by explicit compatible policy. Reject for old-camera image leakage, one-frame smear, stale volume light trails or a secondary view using the primary image history. |
| Stream-in/out, LOD swap, morph/wind parameter change, hot material swap | New/changed pixels have honest correspondence or localized rejection. Reject for carried silhouettes, shadow/reflection ghosts or broad full-frame resets on ordinary local updates. |
| Sun/cloud transition, flash, exposure adaptation | Compare scene-linear and displayed images separately. Reject for exposure pumping that changes history colour incorrectly or persistent volume lighting trails. |
| Temporal provider off and device-loss/restart | Spatial/native path produces coherent composition and all abandoned histories/resources retire; recovered view starts clean. Reject if the profile silently keeps full temporal work or requires unavailable motion to display water/clouds correctly. |

Metrics remain proposed until target budgets and hardware are named: history allocation per active view/effect, velocity/depth pass cost, pass count, frame-time tails, streaming spikes and sustained mobile power; visual review uses synchronized clips and masks. Compare full pipelines at matched content, render/output resolutions and art settings. A no-temporal path may trade edge quality for lower memory/power; that is acceptable only after appearance acceptance. A successful FSR or MetalFX integration on one platform does not certify the other targets.

## Boundary changes to return to architecture/rendering owners

- **V2 is not the sole prior-state producer.** P4 must supply previous skin/morph pose; E1 vegetation/water/cloud and V5 particle/material animation must supply previous presentation-time evaluations or invalidity. W2/F4 report activation and representation/version changes. F1/P2 retain per-view previous accepted presentation time and matrices. Without these owners, V2 cannot truthfully emit all vectors promised by [rendering contract 3](../modules/RENDERING.md#frame-state-and-data-contracts).
- **V4 cannot own every temporal cache.** It owns image reconstruction/exposure/display histories; V3/E1/V5 retain effect-specific temporal state behind a shared per-view invalidation protocol and V1 persistent-resource lifetime. Otherwise a fog/light trail survives an image-history reset, or V4 is forced to understand every provider's internals.
- **Composition needs an explicit graph owner.** The draft says V3/V5 make media explicit, but it does not assign the authority that orders water, cloud, atmosphere, fog and particles across V3/E1/V5 before V4. Recommend V1 graph validation of typed scene-linear layer/depth products with one composition contract owned jointly by V3's renderer integration and E1/V5 producers. This is a boundary requirement, not a demand for a new module.
- **The environment draft places composition in V4.** Its storm worked case says V4 composes cloud/fog/water; the rendering module defines V4 as reconstruction and display. Resolve this as one depth-ordered scene-colour composition before V4, with V4 consuming the resulting image and metadata. The environmental providers remain causes/layer producers, not final image authorities. [Environment worked case](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#worked-operating-cases); [rendering V4 boundary](../modules/RENDERING.md#frame-state-and-data-contracts).
- **Single transparent depth/velocity is only an approximation.** Keep primary opaque and optional nearest transparent descriptors, but add layer/coverage/velocity-confidence metadata and per-material profile policy. Refracted scenery and animated reflections can move independently from the water surface. Make vendor masks adapters, not canonical world facts.
- **Profile qualification remains open.** Temporal upscaling, dynamic resolution, volume reprojection and multi-view retention are separately selected. No algorithm or shader binary is presumed portable to Metal, PC native, WebGPU and mobile. The full cross-effect scene must decide whether the portable baseline is spatial AA, a simple TAA, or a qualified temporal upscaler.
