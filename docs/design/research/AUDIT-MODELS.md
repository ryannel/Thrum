# Audit: alternative physical and presentation models

1 November 2026. Independent challenge to the proposed reusable 3D engine architecture. I wrote the [pre-design derivation](../../evidence/handoffs/2026-11-01-engine-audit/models-independent.md) before opening the engine-rebuild notes. This audit compares that derivation with the integrated design, composition model, C01–C12, environment/render/play/tooling modules and relevant source/review notes. **Recommendations below are proposed, not selected changes to the architecture.** Sources establish mathematical or production precedent within the stated scope; none measures this engine.

## What the design already gets right

The notes do **not** prescribe a single water solver or universal meteorology: [SYSTEM-DESIGN](../SYSTEM-DESIGN.md#why-these-representations-fit-together) says environment providers share source fields rather than a solver; [ENVIRONMENT](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#selected-mathematical-relationships-and-domains) distinguishes dispersive waves, shallow flow, static water and local contact. [COMPOSITION](../COMPOSITION.md#packaging-and-dependencies) allows regional bindings, explicit combiners and build/load/live differences. [C08](../CONTRACTS.md#c08-light-materials-and-transport--v3-with-e1-and-v4) defines ordered medium transport rather than stacked screen fog, and [E1](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#authority-and-the-causal-chain) can retain Sun/cloud attenuation when no sky/cloud pixels are drawn. [V2–V4](../modules/RENDERING.md#frame-state-and-data-contracts) distinguish authoritative simulation samples, view-specific representation and temporal histories. These are legitimate high-level decisions. The findings below target unresolved *semantics between alternative models*, where simply naming more providers would not close the design.

## 1. Water region boundaries need a physical coupling classification

**Prior claim/location.** [ENVIRONMENT lines 38, 65, 85–86](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#selected-mathematical-relationships-and-domains) selects region-specific dynamics under one free-surface query and says a local contact solver may add a displacement/impulse field with a transfer rule. [COMPOSITION lines 68–84](../COMPOSITION.md#packaging-and-dependencies) permits multiple regional providers and a declared overlap combiner. [C09 lines 185–191](../CONTRACTS.md#c09-environmental-fields-and-physical-exchange--e1-with-p3) names runoff and body disturbances. None specifies the **edge between two simultaneously active water regimes**: which state crosses it, whether the boundary conserves water, or whether it is only a visual seam. W2 streaming regions are a different partition and cannot implicitly stand in for hydrodynamic domains.

**Alternatives and primary evidence.** A single shared solver can conserve flux across its cells, but loses the cost/phenomenon advantage of heterogeneous regions. Independently solved regions can use (a) a prescribed one-way boundary, (b) conservative exchanged fluxes, or (c) an expressly visual blend with no physical exchange. A shallow-flow interface has mass flux `F_m = h u·n` and momentum flux `F_p = h u(u·n) + ½gh² n` in the hydrostatic idealization; equal-and-opposite discrete flux is needed to avoid invented water at a shared face. A prescribed spectral sea has no local stored water mass to debit when it drives a shallow estuary, so a tidal/open boundary may be honest while claiming two-way mass conservation is not. Waves at a boundary may need matching phase/energy flux or a deliberately absorbing/radiating condition. [Lannes' mathematical review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09655) distinguishes nonlinear shallow-water, dispersive/Boussinesq and breaking/vorticity extensions; [Tessendorf's original notes](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Jerry-Tessendorf-2/publication/264839743_Simulating_Ocean_Water/links/5ba8ecb0a6fdccd3cb6f6ffd/Simulating-Ocean-Water.pdf?origin=publication_detail) state the extent/detail limit of large 3D fluid simulations. As a production comparison, [Unreal Water Body Actors](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/water-body-actors-in-unreal-engine) use different river and ocean/lake behavior and transition materials; their documented seamless transition is a **render/authoring precedent**, not evidence of conserved hydrodynamic exchange.

**Failure/use case and consequence.** A river that rises during a storm and empties into an ocean could display a seamless surface while its receiving basin never gains water; a boat crossing the mouth could query two incompatible heights/velocities on successive ticks. A waterfall can have disconnected sheets and a plunge pool: one value of `height(x,z)` cannot represent both, even if a splash effect hides the gap. There is a consequential choice between a gameplay physical connection and a presentational connection, not a generic “blend.”

**Classification: missing decision. Proposed resolution.** Make water-domain boundaries authored, validated objects distinct from W2 streaming boundaries. For each adjacency, select `closed`, `prescribed/open`, `one-way exchange`, `conservative coupled`, or `visual-only` semantics (exact names are detailed design). State transferred quantities, units, normal convention, update/substep time, back-pressure/reflection handling, and what a query returns inside the transition band. Require conservative exchange only where both model state and numerical closure can support it; otherwise document the sink/source and prohibit a conservation claim. Falling/overturning water needs a separate volume/particle/mesh domain and a receiving-basin transfer policy, not a forced height-field provider. E1 water owns exchange; F2 validates compatible model pairs and required budgets; F4/T1 cook and inspect edge data; W2 activates required adjoining products together; P3, P5, V2/V3/V5, audio and P7 consume or retain the resulting state. This is an architectural contract before solver implementation.

## 2. “One free-surface query contract” is too broad for all water models and consumers

**Prior claim/location.** [ENVIRONMENT line 38](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#selected-mathematical-relationships-and-domains) says “one free-surface query contract”; [line 65](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#consequential-boundaries) bundles height, normal, velocity, depth, wet/dry/contact and force query. [C09 lines 170–173, 187–189](../CONTRACTS.md#c09-environmental-fields-and-physical-exchange--e1-with-p3) already records scale/validity and separates body integration, but the common query's semantic floor is not defined.

**Alternatives and primary evidence.** A height/normal sample is a useful narrow contract for a graph-like surface `y=η(x,z,t)`; it cannot express multiple vertical intersections, a submerged cave current, or water occupying a falling volume. Distinct typed capabilities could instead expose (i) surface intersection(s) and signed/inside occupancy, (ii) depth/current/velocity field, (iii) wave kinematics at a hull sample, (iv) wet/dry/shoreline state, and (v) a provider-specific fluid-force operator with its assumptions. Consumers can require only the subset they need. [Unreal's `FWaterBodyQueryResult`](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/Water/FWaterBodyQueryResult) separates unperturbed plane, optional waves, depth, velocity and exclusion status—useful precedent for query distinctions, not a complete volumetric-fluid abstraction. [Enright, Marschner and Fedkiw's original paper](https://www.graphics.stanford.edu/papers/water-sg02/) handles breaking/pouring water as a 3D free surface, illustrating the topology the graph representation omits. [Unity HDRP's water scripting](https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition%4015.0/manual/WaterSystem-scripting.html) offers height queries for its particular surface model, not a reason to make height mandatory for every engine water type.

**Failure/use case and consequence.** Swimming AI needs occupancy, current and a traversable volume, while a hull may need integrated pressure/drag over immersed geometry; neither is answered honestly by a single surface height. A first-person view underwater needs ray interval entry/exit and medium properties even where no upward ray intersects a surface. Returning fabricated zeros or one chosen height would make P3/P5/audio/underwater rendering silently disagree.

**Classification: premature choice for the universal query; legitimate detailed work for exact per-model types. Proposed resolution.** Retain a *small common identity/time/support/error envelope*, not one compulsory physical payload. Define water capabilities per consumer quantity and an explicit unsupported/out-of-domain result. Include sample time, spatial support/resolution, normal/current frame, vertical multivaluedness, fidelity/error bound, latency and determinism class. Let simple surface water implement the narrow height path cheaply; make volume and force capabilities opt-in. Require a game profile/content validator to reject a swimmer or volumetric underwater camera when only a graph-surface provider is installed. E1/F3/F2 own capability definitions and admission; P3/P5/P6/audio and V2/V3/V5 declare their minimum query fidelity. A force model remains a separately chosen law, because `F_b≈ρgV_displaced` and drag require displaced volume/relative velocity and do not follow from `η` alone.

## 3. Model selection has regimes named but no acceptance matrix across game, region and target

**Prior claim/location.** [ENVIRONMENT lines 38–44, 85–86, 116](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#selected-mathematical-relationships-and-domains) derives deep and shallow equations and lists water families. [F2 lines 37–45](../modules/TOOLS-DELIVERY.md#f2-finite-capability-resolution) asks providers to declare valid content/scale regimes and cost upper bounds. [COMPOSITION lines 77–83](../COMPOSITION.md#declaration-validation-and-activation) allows regional binding. The design has not yet identified the required **regime descriptor and acceptance rule** that let a game/world/target select one model or several without accidental semantic downgrade.

**Alternatives and primary evidence.** For linear gravity waves, `ω²=gk tanh(kh)` gives `kh≫1` deep-water behavior and `kh≪1` shallow-water speed `√(gh)`, but the latter alone does not license a hydrostatic shallow-flow solver for steep/short breaking waves. A spectral or imposed wave field assumes limited steepness and cannot respond to a dam's mass balance. A depth-averaged shallow solver assumes depth small against horizontal variation and loses vertical velocity structure/dispersion unless extended. A 3D free-surface method supports topology changes but its state grows with resolved volume/detail. [Lannes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09655) derives these shallow-water model families and the approximations between them. [Tessendorf](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Jerry-Tessendorf-2/publication/264839743_Simulating_Ocean_Water/links/5ba8ecb0a6fdccd3cb6f6ffd/Simulating-Ocean-Water.pdf?origin=publication_detail) explicitly contrasts large-ocean and detailed breaking/spray scales. In production, [Unreal's water-body types](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/water-body-actors-in-unreal-engine) encode river versus ocean/lake differences, while [Unity HDRP's quality guidance](https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition%4017.6/manual/water-quality-and-performance-decisions.html) ties surface types, simulation bands, resolution and CPU interaction to cost. Neither is a numerical validation of this engine.

**Failure/use case and consequence.** A desktop sea scene may need spectral swell plus local vessel contacts, while a mobile town needs only a still canal and a shallow flood zone. Applying “simplified water” as one global quality knob could change flooding/navigation or buoyancy. Conversely, a world with a coastal sea, river, indoor fountain and falling sheet needs distinct domains even on one target. The axes are game capability, region physics, presentation view and target budget; collapsing them into a single water quality tier obscures validity.

**Classification: weak justification, with provider-specific thresholds legitimately detailed work. Proposed resolution.** Before implementation, publish a compact model selection matrix for representative game/region cases. Every candidate declares supported topology, relevant nondimensional regime (`kh`, steepness `ka`, `h/L`, flow Froude number where applicable), boundary forcing, interaction type, required query capabilities, state residency and resolution/cost law. The game/domain author chooses the **physical model** explicitly; F2 validates it and may resolve only among alternatives the author has declared semantically equivalent, then selects admissible presentation tiers per target. Estimated cost must not silently choose a different physical law. A visual tier may reduce ripples/mesh/detail but cannot change conserved flooding or body response without game consent. Authoring validates each water domain against the selected model's support and transition contracts. Exact thresholds and device budgets are determined per provider and game, then qualified with predicted rejection conditions; they should not be invented globally now.

## 4. CPU/GPU residence and query synchronization need model-specific policy

**Prior claim/location.** [SYSTEM-DESIGN lines 27–29, 101–105](../SYSTEM-DESIGN.md#architectural-position) separates CPU/GPU graphs and environment queries. [C02](../CONTRACTS.md#c02-time-and-committed-state--f1-coordinator-w1-world) and [C06](../CONTRACTS.md#c06-gpu-execution-and-lifetime--v1) specify snapshots and GPU lifetime. [ENVIRONMENT lines 38, 56, 65](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#selected-mathematical-relationships-and-domains) requires contact/image alignment and validity. Yet no placement policy says which water/cloud state may be GPU authoritative, replicated on CPU, reconstructed coarsely, or read back with delay.

**Alternatives and primary evidence.** CPU-authoritative fields uploaded to GPU make fixed-tick queries direct but consume CPU and upload bandwidth. GPU-authoritative visuals can update densely, but gameplay readback introduces asynchronous latency and synchronization. A split coarse CPU field plus fine GPU residual saves CPU, provided the visual residual is bounded at contact and receivers know the difference. Duplicate CPU/GPU evaluation can improve matching at a cost. [Unity HDRP explicitly documents](https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition%4017.6/manual/water-quality-and-performance-decisions.html) that CPU script interaction duplicates GPU water simulation work; its [water properties](https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition%4017.0/manual/WaterSystem-Properties.html) expose full/half resolution and optional ripples for CPU queries. This is direct production evidence that query fidelity and residence have a measurable design cost, not evidence for one universal choice.

**Failure/use case and consequence.** If a ship's fixed-tick buoyancy reads a wave texture from the previous GPU frame, forces lag visible crests and may vary with frame queue/device timing. If a GPU foam field is promoted to wetness or navigation truth, lowering quality or device loss changes gameplay. A cloud shadow for two cameras may be one shared source map plus per-view projection/history; duplicating the source per camera wastes work and can desynchronize them.

**Classification: missing decision. Proposed resolution.** Add a per-provider *residence/synchronization declaration*: authoritative location, CPU query representation, GPU derived products, upload/readback direction and cadence, maximum query age/phase error, replay/determinism envelope, and recovery from device loss. Classify GPU readback results as asynchronous/presentation or explicitly latency-tolerant; baseline fixed-tick P3 queries use CPU-owned or analytically reproducible state unless a game accepts the delay as part of its physical law. Cost models include `bytes transferred × update rate`, duplicate evaluation, staging/overlap and multi-view reuse, in addition to solver complexity. F1/E1/P3 agree on tick sample; V1/V2/V3 own GPU products; F2 budgets/validates placements; T2 measures actual transfer and sync stalls.

## 5. Atmosphere alternatives need a semantic selection rule, not only a transport compositor

**Prior claim/location.** [ENVIRONMENT lines 24, 28, 34, 61–68, 85, 94–99](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#authority-and-the-causal-chain) correctly separates source facts, cloud attenuation, visible sky and near/far media, and names Hillaire/Bruneton as alternatives. [C08 lines 144–166](../CONTRACTS.md#c08-light-materials-and-transport--v3-with-e1-and-v4) gives light/medium units and ordered `L_out=T L_in+S`. [RENDERING lines 54–56](../modules/RENDERING.md#frame-state-and-data-contracts) specifies composition inputs. The missing decision is when a game may choose an HDRI/gradient/analytical sky, a precomputed planetary atmosphere, a dynamic atmosphere, or independently authored background and environment light, and which outputs each must actually supply.

**Alternatives and primary evidence.** A visible sky image can supply background radiance but not necessarily path-dependent aerial extinction, Sun transmittance or local-light fog. Hillaire's [production-oriented technique](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf) supports ground-to-space views and dynamic atmospheric properties with compact LUTs; Bruneton's [tested implementation](https://ebruneton.github.io/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/index.html) is a precomputed-scattering candidate with different update constraints. [Unity HDRP lists gradient, HDRI, physically based and custom skies](https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition%4017.1/manual/sky.html), permits the [visual sky to differ from the lighting sky](https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition%4017.1/manual/ambient-lighting-configure.html), and changes sky by camera volume. [Unreal separates atmosphere, cloud, Sun, Sky Light and fog](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/environmental-light-with-fog-clouds-sky-and-atmosphere-in-unreal-engine); its [local fog documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/local-fog-volumes-in-unreal-engine) names a concrete composition order and cheaper local-volume path. These demonstrate viable alternatives and composition responsibilities, not proof that one vendor's exact ordering fits this renderer.

**Failure/use case and consequence.** A first-person interior can show an HDRI through a window while using authored directional daylight and local fog; a no-sky overhead game may still require aerial perspective and cloud shadows. A flight game needs altitude-dependent path lengths a ground-only gradient cannot supply. If a captured background is treated as physical sky illumination without a declared conversion, material reflections/ambient may disagree with Sun and cloud shadow; if a full atmosphere and local fog both integrate the same aerosol over an interval, haze doubles. C08 prevents the last error algebraically but does not select which model owns the overlapping optical coefficients or cache invalidation when changing sky type.

**Classification: weak justification and missing selection semantics; the ordered `(T,S)` compositor is legitimate selected work. Proposed resolution.** Define separate atmosphere capabilities: terminal background radiance, direct-source transmittance, environment/ambient radiance, aerial perspective over a ray interval, local-medium optical properties, and cloud directional visibility. A provider may bundle them, but F2 must validate each consumed output and whether two providers represent the same medium. Authoring declares physical coupling versus an intentional art override, light/spectral basis and calibration, supported camera altitude/range and update cadence; compositing assigns each optical interval/quantity one owner. Choose precomputed/dynamic/analytical/image models by required range, time/weather changes and target budget, not by a universal “sky quality” slider. Keep visible-sky omission independent of retained illumination/shadow/media. E1 owns source/medium identity, V3 owns integration and light accounting, V4 display, F2 compatibility, F4 cooked LUT/HDRI/cloud products and T1 preview/inspection.

## 6. Model-specific authored and cooked requirements are the next necessary closure

**Prior claim/location.** [ENVIRONMENT line 88](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#family-responsibilities-and-plausible-providers) calls for editable sources, region boundaries and cooked per-platform payloads. [F2 lines 37–43](../modules/TOOLS-DELIVERY.md#f2-finite-capability-resolution) declares content regimes and preparatory products; [F4/W2](../modules/WORLD-CONTENT.md#streaming-transaction-and-consistency) atomically activate required products. These are sound generic contracts, but model selection cannot be validated until model-specific source/cook products and dependencies are named.

**Alternatives and primary evidence.** Water authored by splines/terrain, flow maps and distinct body types is a concrete shipped workflow in [Unreal Water Body Actors](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/water-body-actors-in-unreal-engine); it also shows that transition materials require authored compatibility. [Unity's water properties](https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition%4017.0/manual/WaterSystem-Properties.html) expose distinct simulation, mask, foam and CPU query settings. Atmospheric LUT inputs and validity differ between [Hillaire](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf) and [Bruneton](https://ebruneton.github.io/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/index.html). These are examples of dependencies a generic `asset.json` cannot infer from an untyped “water/sky asset.”

**Failure/use case and consequence.** A river can cook a mesh and visual flow map while lacking bathymetry, velocity boundary conditions or a CPU query product required by P3. A mobile atmosphere variant can retain a sky texture but omit the cloud attenuation field that roofs need. The build may be valid by file hash yet semantically incomplete.

**Classification: legitimate detailed work with an architectural admission dependency. Proposed resolution.** The water/atmosphere commissions should publish typed source and cooked-product closure per model: domain topology and adjacency, bathymetry/bank/solid boundaries, source/receiver fluxes, flow/wave parameters, query representation and error metadata, visual material/LOD/underwater products; for air, light/medium parameter basis, LUT/HDRI/cloud field provenance and supported view range. F3 schemas and F4 cook DAG encode dependencies; F2/T1 reject a profile/scene whose semantic products are absent, and P7 records durable model state/revision where needed. Keep editable masters in the existing factory contract rather than inventing a second asset tree. This can be completed in detailed design, but water/atmosphere implementation must wait for it.

## 7. A preintegrated atmosphere pair does not generally close an overlapping-medium contract

**Prior claim/location.** [ENVIRONMENT line 61](../modules/ENVIRONMENT.md#consequential-boundaries) lets a fog provider supply extinction/scattering **or an equivalent preintegrated transmittance plus radiance pair**. [C08 lines 155–165](../CONTRACTS.md#c08-light-materials-and-transport--v3-with-e1-and-v4) says overlapping media combine extinction and source terms before integration, while disjoint intervals compose `(T_near T_far, S_near + T_near S_far)`. [RENDERING line 55](../modules/RENDERING.md#frame-state-and-data-contracts) repeats the alternative preintegrated input. The two output forms are not equivalent for arbitrary overlap.

**Alternatives and primary evidence.** For a ray, `T(a,b)=exp[-∫_a^b σ_t(s)ds]` and `S(a,b)=∫_a^b T(a,s)j(s)ds`. If medium A supplies only aggregate `(T_A,S_A)` and B only `(T_B,S_B)` over the *same* interval, those pairs lose the spatial ordering of `σ_A` relative to B's `j_B`; the combined source integral cannot in general be reconstructed. Counterexample: A is a purely absorbing slab before versus after B's localized in-scattering. Both placements yield the same A pair and B pair in isolation, but B's contribution is attenuated only when A is in front. The pair algebra is exact for **ordered disjoint intervals**; a homogeneous/coincident slab adapter can derive an approximation under explicit uniform-coefficient assumptions; arbitrary overlaps require coefficient/source samples or one provider that jointly preintegrates all participating media. [Hillaire's atmosphere paper](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf) is an example of precomputed/integrated atmosphere outputs for a specified atmosphere; it does not imply independent arbitrary fog volumes can be combined from terminal pairs. [Unreal's local-fog documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/local-fog-volumes-in-unreal-engine) documents a specific ordered composition/voxelization policy, illustrating that production renderers choose an approximation rather than assume a universal algebra.

**Failure/use case and consequence.** A valley fog bank overlaps the far atmosphere while clouds scatter light behind it. If both send only ray-total `(T,S)`, a compositor can double haze or put the cloud/fog light on the wrong side of absorption depending on camera motion. The existing C08 promise of no double integration cannot be fulfilled by every currently allowed provider output.

**Classification: contract inconsistency / missing decision. Proposed resolution.** Keep `(T,S)` as a useful *segment result*, but require one of three declared modes at admission: nonoverlapping ordered segments; coefficient/source access to a joint integrator for overlapping support; or a named precomposing provider that owns all overlapping media and returns their combined pair. If a profile intentionally uses ordered screen layers or homogeneous overlap approximation, label its domain and error policy; do not claim exact coefficient combination. E1 declares medium support/representation, V3 chooses integration and owns no-double-counting, F2 rejects incompatible overlapping bindings, and V4 receives only the resulting composed scene-linear image. This is a small shared contract correction, not a demand to implement every volumetric algorithm.

## Priority and coverage

Findings 1, 2 and 7 change shared contracts and should be resolved before accepting water or air module APIs. Findings 3–5 define model admission, target behavior and light/media ownership before provider construction. Finding 6 is detailed design that must close the admission path. A useful **analytical stress case**, not a requirement to build every model now, is one world containing sea → river → falls → pool, one submerged passage, a boat and swimmer, two cameras, and a no-visible-sky cloud-shadow profile. For each edge and consumer, write down owned state, exchange/query quantity, sample time, accepted approximation and cooked input; reject any case that requires fabricated height, hidden mass source, duplicate optical integration or frame-dependent gameplay. Only then choose narrowly targeted proofs for unresolved numerical error and device cost.

Coverage includes `SYSTEM-DESIGN`, `COMPOSITION`, `CONTRACTS`, environment, rendering, play, F2/T1/T2, world/content, the rendering-source ledger and prior integration reviews. I inspected public vendor documentation and primary author papers linked above; I did not run their sample projects, inspect this engine's implementation, measure any target device, or assess images. Published algorithms and vendor feature lists do not prove visual quality, performance, portability or production readiness for this engine. The design's current maturity remains proposal/commissioning status; this audit neither selects providers nor changes user product decisions.
