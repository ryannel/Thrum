# APFS physical admission options for native source

3 November 2026. Bounded research for F3's [physical admission gate](../../BUILD-PLAN.md) and [Foundation §4](../FOUNDATION-SPEC.md#4-working-files-crash-recovery-and-gc). This note owns no contract change, production implementation or capability promotion. It follows the [checkpoint overlap counterexample](../../evidence/i2a/SOURCE-PHYSICAL-ENVELOPE-2026-11-03.md), [reservation alternatives](SOURCE-PHYSICAL-RESERVATION-OPTIONS-2026-11-03.md) and [requested-I/O fence](SOURCE-REQUESTED-IO-FENCE-2026-11-03.md). The author read those designs before this study; this is not blind independent discovery. Independent adversarial review is separate.

## Recommendation and decision boundary

**Keep the exact physical gate open.** Select a common cross-process allocation broker, conservative closure retention and the private requested-I/O fence as useful enforcement work. Do not promote their requested-byte credits, APFS preallocation, a volume quota or observed free space to a proof that every admitted operation leaves **8 GiB actually unallocated**. The examined public Apple interfaces do not supply an upper bound for all allocation caused by a SQLite operation, including later CoW, filesystem metadata and sync/checkpoint work.

For ordinary developer machines, the practical candidate is the **shared-volume guarded profile S** below, with the already canonical external-consumer assumption stated prominently. A dedicated APFS volume with reserve/quota is an optional isolation improvement; a dedicated container on a real partition or device removes more shared-pool competition. Neither closes the operation-growth proof. Requiring new disk provisioning before solving that gap would increase setup cost without satisfying the gate.

The unresolved human choice is exact: **retain the current pre-dispatch physical-floor requirement and continue refusing accepted-source promotion until a supported allocation bound/mechanism is found, or explicitly revise the canonical requirement to qualified, measured free-space guarding with possible own-operation overshoot and ENOSPC recovery.** The latter is a change of guarantee, not completion of today's gate. Selection of S, a reserve or a dedicated container cannot silently make that choice. No answer is needed to finish this research or implement the independently useful request ledger.

## Required outcomes and authority

| Required outcome | Owner / present resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Budget all simultaneous retained old/new bytes, application staging, WAL/main overlap, managed temporaries and recovery | Source actor computes closure and operation requests; physical adapter needs a qualified growth model. Input-byte multipliers are rejected. |
| Refuse before Thrum's own over-budget allocation and preserve 8 GiB actually free | Broker serializes cooperating allocators and checks observations. **Unresolved:** bounded requests do not establish the physical peak. |
| Keep exact committed A/B outcome through faults | SQLite root/outcome transaction remains authority; a broker grant is permission to attempt, never a source outcome. |
| Reopen after crash without minting space or deleting pins | Durable grant/recovery protocol, conservative unresolved charges and exact keyed outcome; not implemented by this note. |
| Distinguish logical reuse, allocation entitlement, physically free space and durability | Report separate quantities and qualified platform assumptions; none is inferred from another. |

The 64 GiB project profile includes 52 GiB main, 2 GiB WAL, 2 GiB managed temporary/cache and 8 GiB headroom. Those buckets alone do not price ordinary working-file staging, APFS overhead or retained snapshot versions. The implementer must account for every path and affected volume; putting only the database on a constrained volume leaves checkout/application staging on its original volume. Either colocate all managed allocating paths or acquire all involved brokers in stable volume-ID order, preflight each independently and retain each unresolved grant. This is a proposed implementation rule, not a new shared contract.

## What primary sources establish

**APFS allocation is broader than file length.** APFS containers share free space among volumes. Its object/checkpoint machinery uses CoW, snapshots preserve historical filesystem state, and its space manager has allocation/free-queue state. The format exposes volume reserve, quota and allocation block counts, but does not specify an application-call maximum for allocation caused by write, sync or metadata operations. A physical object's modified copy moves to a new block address. These facts justify accounting for overlap; they do not yield a safe multiplier. The publicly served reference is dated **22 June 2020**, despite access in 2026. [Apple File System Reference, pp. 7–10, 53, 115–116, 155–166](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/Apple-File-System-Reference.pdf).

**Preallocation has a narrower useful guarantee.** `F_PREALLOCATE` sets aside backing storage for a particular file; `fst_bytesalloc` reports allocated bytes. `F_ALLOCATEALL` requests all or none, and `F_PEOFPOSMODE` requests additional allocation from physical EOF. Current Apple source documentation warns that `F_ALLOCATEPERSIST`, intended to retain unfilled allocation at close, may be ignored. The syscall forwards to filesystem `VNOP_ALLOCATE`; XNU dispatch code is not the APFS allocator implementation. Inspect success, returned bytes and later file state; never treat a future close/reopen as a preserved grant by assumption. [Apple current fcntl manual source](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/main/bsd/man/man2/fcntl.2), [XNU F_PREALLOCATE dispatch](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/main/bsd/kern/kern_descrip.c#L3093), [Apple backing-store operation description](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/kernel/vnop_pagein_args?changes=_7).

**Deduction:** successful preallocation is real allocation to that file, but is neither a transferable reserve for SQLite's other files nor a promise that later overwrites, snapshots, directory updates and sync need no additional blocks. Reserving a separate ballast file and releasing it just before work creates another free-space race and does not prepay that work's metadata. Keeping the ballast allocated makes it unavailable to fund work and reduces actually free bytes. Both fail as a proof of the 8 GiB floor. No attempt should set Apple's documented snapshot-CoW exemption inode flag; the APFS reference says to preserve it rather than add/remove it.

**A volume reserve is entitlement; a quota is a ceiling.** Apple's user documentation says a reserve keeps storage available for a volume and a quota limits its allocation. Volumes otherwise draw from one container pool. The locally installed `diskutil(8)` further describes reserves as guaranteed user-file-data capacity and quotas as limits on user-file-data capacity; that wording is not an assertion that container metadata is prepaid. A reserve is consumed by that volume's own use, rather than being an everlasting free-space margin. A quota without a reserve need not be reachable if siblings fill the container. [Apple volume size options](https://support.apple.com/guide/disk-utility/add-delete-or-erase-apfs-volumes-dskua9e6a110/mac), [Apple space-sharing explanation](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/mac-help/sysp560a2952/27/mac/27).

**Free-space observations need distinct meanings.** `fstatfs` reports free filesystem blocks and free blocks available to a non-superuser; multiply by `f_bsize` with checked arithmetic. Neither field is a grant. Apple's capacity guidance recommends a different API for important user-requested resources; Time Machine explicitly counts snapshot space as available storage and can remove snapshots when needed. Therefore a user-facing available-capacity number cannot be assumed to mean presently unallocated physical container blocks. [Apple statfs manual source](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/main/bsd/man/man2/statfs.2), [Apple capacity guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/checking-volume-storage-capacity), [Apple snapshot capacity explanation](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/102154).

**Proposed measurement policy:** capture both credential-usable filesystem availability and APFS container unallocated capacity, plus volume quota/reserve state. Refuse if identity, meaning or a required reading is unavailable or unsupported. Use the smaller applicable limit; quota headroom and a volume reserve are additional constraints, never extra free bytes to add to the container reading. A version-qualified adapter must establish which native observation excludes purgeable capacity. `diskutil apfs list -plist` is a useful read-only qualification/diagnostic source, not a per-write subprocess or an atomic reservation API. Public `statfs` documentation alone does not establish that every APFS configuration reports the exact container physical quantity required by this contract. Do not label that telemetry as proved until qualified.

**SQLite has multiple allocation producers.** WAL commit appends records; checkpoint later copies pages into main while WAL may remain retained, and readers can block checkpoint completion. WAL and SHM are part of the database's live state. A crash does not authorize removing WAL to recover space. [SQLite WAL algorithm and checkpoint lifecycle](https://sqlite.org/wal.html). `max_page_count` limits database pages; `journal_size_limit` is not a prewrite WAL cap, and `temp_store=MEMORY` does not eliminate every kind of temporary file. Pin/read back the relevant settings and deny unsupported roles. [SQLite pragmas](https://sqlite.org/pragma.html), [SQLite temporary-file categories and version limits](https://sqlite.org/tempfiles.html).

The examined local SQLite **3.53.2** Unix implementation's `fcntlSizeHint` can issue native writes below an outer `xWrite` wrapper when chunking is enabled and `posix_fallocate` is absent. It also has a separate mmap extension path. This corroborates keeping zero chunk/data-mmap settings, suppressed hints and the complete file-control allowlist from the requested-I/O design. The public interface includes separate write, truncate, sync, SHM and fetch methods; observing only `xWrite` is incomplete. [SQLite Unix VFS source, `fcntlSizeHint`](https://sqlite.org/src/doc/trunk/src/os_unix.c), [I/O methods](https://sqlite.org/c3ref/io_methods.html), [file controls](https://sqlite.org/c3ref/c_fcntl_begin_atomic_write.html). The live trunk link is reference material, not the pinned deployment identity.

## Deployable profiles compared

All profiles retain root/outcome semantics, finite request credits, explicit checkpoint ownership and managed-temporary enforcement. “Deployable” here means ordinary OS mechanisms can provision the environment; none is qualified for canonical physical admission by this study.

| Profile | Preconditions and measurable refusal | Benefit and remaining guarantee gap | Cost / portability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S: shared APFS volume, cooperating broker** | All Thrum stores/managed staging on the container use one broker namespace; no noncooperating path changes or unaccounted consumer growth during protected work. Bind directory/store/volume/container identity and policy version. Fresh checks before each bounded allocating operation/checkpoint; refuse unsupported telemetry, exhausted request/bucket credits, unresolved recovery or low observed space. Snapshot creation/retention must be included in a qualified policy, otherwise refuse that policy. | Least setup; serializes Thrum spending. External processes, Time Machine/admin actions and filesystem work remain outside the lock. No hard physical growth bound follows. | Constant-size broker/ledger state plus finite grant records; short allocation windows serialize disk work. Decode/read leases run outside permit. Separate adapter qualification on every platform. |
| **V: dedicated APFS volume with reserve and quota in shared container** | Explicit operator provisioning; authenticate mount UUID, container, configured reserve/quota and exclusive managed ownership. Do not create/resize volumes automatically. Include all managed paths or keep S's additional brokers. Recheck limits and free capacity; refuse configuration drift, uncontrolled same-volume writers/snapshots, unavailable observations or exhausted limits. | Reserve protects entitlement against sibling-volume competition; quota bounds the volume's permitted allocation. Sibling/container activity, APFS metadata and actual unallocated floor still need proof. `reserve=quota` is not a physical-floor reservation. | Low ongoing process memory, operational setup and reduced flexible disk sharing. Apple-specific provisioning. File extents and snapshots still require accounting. |
| **C: exclusive container on a real partition/device** | Operator-provisioned fixed backing capacity, one managed volume, exclusive writers, controlled snapshots/clones and no thin/sparse backing. Verify physical stores/container/mount identity and capacity. Refuse drift or any low-space/request condition. | Removes ordinary sibling-volume consumption. A partition/device boundary provides a stronger capacity domain than another volume. Still no documented per-operation CoW/metadata bound or guaranteed post-operation 8 GiB floor. Hardware/admin activity remains an external assumption. | Strongest isolation and highest provisioning burden; wastes unused capacity and changes backup workflows. Separate filesystem qualification elsewhere. A sparse disk image adds an outer allocation domain and is unsuitable; even a filled image on APFS retains outer CoW/snapshot assumptions. |
| **R: retain all complete closures, finite grants/recovery tranche** | Overlay on S/V/C. No destructive GC; exact roots/pins and unresolved application/grant state persist. Every create, reset, cleanup, pin/outcome and recovery write has a finite authorized request envelope. Refuse before logical/request cap or unsupported physical allowance; stop staging at bounded WAL limit. | Reduces GC/pin-release hazards and avoids speculative reclamation. Retained data monotonically consumes budget; restart does not restore spent grants. Does not eliminate overwrite/metadata allocation or turn retention into reservation. | Simple liveness rule but earlier refusals and potentially much higher retained bytes. Portable policy; native allocator semantics remain platform-specific. |

For V/C, a **proposal** is to size provisioned usable capacity above the project's data ceilings plus managed application staging, separate recovery allowance, qualified filesystem overhead and the 8 GiB margin. No numeric extra overhead is selected: assigning a plausible GiB value here would disguise the missing bound. A 64 GiB quota is not automatically sufficient for the Foundation profile's total physical obligations.

## Implementor-ready control boundary

1. **Bind and lock.** Acquire store owner first, then the common container allocation permit. Keep owner protection through leases/quarantine; never wait for another store owner while holding the container permit. For several allocation domains acquire permits in stable identity order. Revalidate native identities through already opened handles, not only path strings. Namespace stability and absence of hostile native code remain assumptions.
2. **Reconstruct obligations.** Read bounded authenticated broker/store recovery records using the finite bootstrap profile. Count retained selected/old closures, unresolved application copies, staged chunks and every live file object. Deduplicate a future physical obligation only when the same managed storage object/allocation is genuinely shared, not merely when two paths or stores hold identical hashes; cloned objects retain separate future CoW allowances. Carry WAL/checkpoint debt and unlinked-open objects separately. Existing allocation is already reflected in fresh free-space telemetry; do not subtract its whole length again as if it were future growth.
3. **Require an allocation certificate.** Each operation plan needs a version-qualified physical peak allowance `P` including data, CoW, metadata, sync, managed temporary and error/cleanup paths; a separate recovery allowance `R`; and request-vector grants. For each domain require `observed_actual_free >= 8 GiB + outstanding_unmaterialized_grants + P + R`, plus usable-volume/quota and project-bucket checks. All arithmetic is checked. A physical allowance that is unknown has **no grant**, not an empirical default. Observation errors refuse before dispatch.
4. **Persist permission before dispatch.** Record exact owner epoch, policy digest, operation key/base/target, grant sequence, retained object identities and full conservative allowance using a pre-funded bounded recovery/metadata mechanism. Its own writes, syncs and possible failure are part of the allowance. Do not recursively fund its journal from an unrecorded grant in the same SQLite store. Preallocation may support this mechanism only after its actual persistence/CoW semantics are qualified.
5. **Execute and reconcile.** Debit the request fence before native callbacks. Recheck space before each bounded allocation/checkpoint under the permit. On native partial error burn the full relevant grant, retain old closures and stop allocating until the actor barrier. Resolve final source state from the exact SQLite keyed outcome. An after-operation floor observation is a violation detector, not retrospective proof that dispatch was safe.
6. **Release conservatively.** Only after SQL autocommit, no callbacks, known close/unlink results and fresh native observations may the broker retire an unresolved grant or object. Logical deletion, freelists, WAL reset, truncation or a snapshot deletion request do not imply physical space has returned. A smaller requested extent alone is insufficient for a refund.

Steps 1, 2, request fencing and refusal are implementable now. Steps 3 and 4 are **required unresolved producers** for canonical admission, not tasks already specified by the vector ledger. A durable broker's representation and finite write/recovery envelope need their own design/proof before production integration.

## Crash, restart and floor walkthrough

- **Crash before grant durability:** no funded source operation may have begun. Recovery must identify and bound partial broker creation; an unauthenticated record cannot create credit.
- **Crash after grant, before/inside staging:** the entire unresolved allowance remains unavailable. A new owner takes the lock, verifies records and selected root, then reconciles exact file objects and intent. A PID or elapsed time cannot refund a grant or retire a live reader pin.
- **Crash during final sync/commit:** query exact publication key and target. B may have committed despite the caller seeing an error; preserve B if verified and do not report A merely because the budget path failed.
- **Recovery needs writes while the floor is low:** refuse allocating admission/recovery work without a qualified pre-funded recovery tranche. Offer read-only diagnosis/export only if that path is itself nonallocating within the managed domains; do not truncate WAL, discard pinned old roots or borrow the 8 GiB margin silently. Operator freeing space or explicitly changing the budget is an external action.
- **External consumer races a check:** S explicitly cannot preserve its floor against that consumer. V limits sibling entitlement interference, C excludes ordinary sibling writes, but neither corrects an unknown own-operation allowance. An OS/device that fails its flush contract remains outside the local durability guarantee. [SQLite hardware and flush assumptions](https://sqlite.org/atomiccommit.html).

The first accepted path can use conservative retention, but “keep everything” must stop before its finite budget is spent. Recovery progress is a separate liveness promise from root safety; truthful refusal can preserve the last exact root without guaranteeing immediate write recovery.

## Performance account and qualification queue

The request fence meters frequent writes in constant time with fixed role slots; a broker should similarly keep only bounded active-grant state in RAM. Avoid a full manifest or filesystem scan at each VFS callback. Source actor plans closure once, prepares a batch, acquires the permit for preflight/grant/SQL/barrier, then releases it before decode or returned leases. Raw filesystem capacity calls at every tiny native write may dominate tiny-file throughput; qualification must choose an operation boundary whose proven `P` covers all hidden callbacks. Reducing checks without such a bound is unsafe, while broadening transactions can increase cache-spill/WAL/memory peaks.

V/C provisioned capacity has little process-memory cost. Large upfront preallocation adds disk work and occupied capacity; ballast release/refill adds I/O, fragmentation risk and new crash states. CoW copies and retained snapshots can dominate a small source edit. These are modeled costs, not measured overhead results. Reuse the earlier fresh/1,000-tiny-file/neighbor-edit workload only as a baseline; measure synchronized durable broker overhead, permit wait/hold percentiles, requested and observed physical deltas, whole-worker RSS and repeated crash recovery separately.

The next proof must reject the selected guarantee, not just show a successful small write:

1. Obtain a supported physical-allocation bound or enforceable reservation mechanism for the pinned APFS/macOS/VFS profile, including metadata, repeated overwrite, snapshots/clones, sync, close and failure recovery. If unavailable, return the exact contract choice above; finite stress tests cannot prove a universal bound.
2. Qualify telemetry against actual APFS container free blocks, quota/reserve behavior and purgeable snapshots, including errors and identity drift. Avoid assuming the two observations are atomic.
3. On an explicitly provisioned disposable constrained environment, exercise real quota/ENOSPC, reader-blocked checkpoint overlap, repeated edits, snapshot retention, same-container competing volumes and restart at each durable grant/source boundary. No such environment was created here.
4. Verify refusal before native dispatch for every allowance/bucket/floor failure, distinct root outcome resolution after uncertain commit, and no credit resurrection after process death. Then measure performance with the final durable mechanism.
