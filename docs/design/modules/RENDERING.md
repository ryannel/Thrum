## Outcome and scope

One committed 3D world must look coherent through orthographic/isometric, top-down, third-person and first-person views without stepping simulation for each view. The engine must produce attractive, stable moving images at selected quality on modern desktops and scaled images on browser/mobile, including complex materials, local and environmental light, animation, vegetation, volumes, water, transparency and effects. Games may omit any environment family, but consumers retain a valid source for the light or shadow facts they require. Quality levels share gameplay state. Pure 2D and MMO rendering are excluded.

| ID | Required observable outcome | Owner and proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Changing cameras preserves world placement, lighting meaning and object identity, while view resolution/history differ | V2/P2 view extraction; dual-camera moving scene |
| R2 | Many dynamic objects, lit surfaces and authored detail have bounded visibility and streaming work | V2 with W2/F4; moving near/far route and stream churn |
| R3 | Directional and local lighting, shadows, indirect fill and reflections remain usable with or without visible sky/clouds | V3 with E1; top-down and exterior A/B |
| R4 | Opaque, masked, translucent, water, particles and volumes compose without obvious occlusion, velocity or exposure contradiction | V3–V5; moving cross-effect scene |
| R5 | A display transform gives stable bright/dark appearance and output colour on qualified targets | V4; calibrated cards plus day/night and HDR/SDR checks |
| R6 | Off/provider/profile switches retire avoidable work, resources and histories without dropping required game state | F2/V1–V5; allocation and pass trace |
| R7 | Artists and AI agents can create, inspect, validate and revise assets, materials, scene settings and performance through stable schemas and CLI/preview operations | T1/T2/F4; deterministic edit/cook/inspect loop |

## Proposed operating model and decisions

**Selected boundary, provisional technique.** The world (W1/W2) owns identities, committed transforms and activated regions. Animation supplies posed/deformed geometry at a presentation timestamp. V2 creates a versioned render scene optimized for GPU use. V1 owns device resources, shader/pipeline preparation and pass dependencies. V3 owns material evaluation and illumination provider contracts; V4 owns per-view image reconstruction, exposure and display; V5 owns local visual effects. E1 owns environmental causes and some representations, while submitting render products through V2/V3/V5. Camera P2 supplies view descriptions, never a second world. This follows the pass-resource and persistent-resource separation in [Epic RDG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/render-dependency-graph-in-unreal-engine) and [Unity URP render graph](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/render-pipelines/universal-render-pipeline/customizing-urp/render-graph/introduction), but a custom graph implementation is a proposal until a backend and host are selected.

**Portable baseline selection.** Use depth-prepared clustered forward rasterization, explicit light lists, physically interpretable material inputs, conventional dynamic shadow maps with authored/static or probe fill, reflection probes with planar/screen refinement where the scene requires it, and a spatial antialiasing/display fallback. This is the recommended structural baseline for all profiles. It shades opaque, masked and transparent materials through the same light/material interface while avoiding a required external multi-target G buffer. [Olsson et al.](https://highperformancegraphics.org/previous/www_2012/media/Papers/HPG2012_Papers_Olsson.pdf) derive clustered forward and deferred variants; their scene-specific results do not establish our performance. Apple documents the external-bandwidth cost of traditional deferred rendering and a Metal-specific tile solution in [tile guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/tailor-your-apps-for-apple-gpus-and-tile-based-deferred-rendering). The actual culling grid may be screen tiles or depth clusters: choose logarithmic/perspective and linear/orthographic depth bins analytically from projected light overlap and scene depth range. This design does not inherit Unity's [URP Forward+](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal@15.0/manual/rendering/forward-plus-rendering-path.html) orthographic limitation. A deferred/tile hybrid is an optional specialized provider for extreme local-light loads, with an identical material/light contract; it is not the unresolved foundation.

**Optional providers, not default promises.** GPU-driven/virtualized geometry, virtual shadow maps, hardware/software ray traced GI/reflections, advanced temporal scaling, and native tile/mesh features compete behind contracts after realistic platform qualification. Nanite requires cooked geometry and has disclosed material/deformation limits; VSM cache savings can be lost to wind and moving lights; Lumen has platform and representation limits. These are production references, not drop-in modules or evidence that the browser/mobile profile can run them. [Nanite](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine), [VSM](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/virtual-shadow-maps-in-unreal-engine), [Lumen](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine).

**Backend selection remains open.** A graphics abstraction may use wgpu, selected native APIs, or both. API restrictions, shader translation, feature availability and debug tooling are evaluated as a whole path, not inferred from “runs on Metal.” V1 has one semantic contract, but it need not flatten every backend to the least capable API; advanced providers declare capabilities and fallback. The [WebGPU spec](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/) makes features/limits explicit, while [Metal capability tables](https://developer.apple.com/metal/capabilities/) show family variation. No claim of a universal shader binary or automatic API parity is made.

**Renderer extension limit (J02).** The supported public seams are V2 extraction, V3 material/style and light/media inputs, optional render features, and typed pass hooks executed through V1's device, graph and submission authority. Headless execution omits the renderer. A different GPU backend may implement V1's semantic contract; a different material or effect declares its supported passes, resource edges, output units, depth/layer behavior, target limits, shader demands and history invalidation. Arbitrary whole-renderer replacement, automatic material compatibility across unrelated pipelines and live renderer swaps are not baseline promises. A materially different deferred/tile provider needs a named workload and a complete material/light/media/depth/velocity/display mapping plus its own finite demand closure before F2 admits it; the clustered-forward baseline remains provisional within its stated workload. A painterly style can use material/light/display policy when those seams suffice, without forcing a second renderer. [Unity SRP](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/render-pipelines/scriptable-render-pipeline-introduction) demonstrates a stronger custom-pipeline choice; [Godot rendering architecture](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/engine_details/architecture/internal_rendering_architecture.html) distinguishes methods and drivers. Neither precedent establishes a need for that stronger promise here.

**Finite shader demand and device readiness (A03).** F2 resolves one declared game setup, target and admitted profile/tier plans, including permitted live transitions. F4 extends its existing cooked DAG/manifest with the finite *reachable* shader module and pipeline-descriptor tuples implied by actual material closures/features, geometry/deformation interfaces, pass roles (including masked shadow, depth and velocity), selected providers, target limits, render formats/layouts and compile-time specialization. Declared dynamic asset roots/families and required fallback/transition paths contribute to the same closure; an arbitrary future code lookup cannot be established by static scans. The builder records why each tuple is included and strips only unreachable products, rather than forming the full Cartesian product. F2 rejects a required tuple unsupported by a material/pass/provider/target. Imported or external content enters only through the same validated target cook/admission path, never as raw shader injection or an undeclared runtime variant.

F4's immutable cooked-product key includes canonical shader source/IR and dependency hashes, schema/layout, toolchain and translation revisions, target API/features and compile-time choices. V1's distinct runtime pipeline key additionally includes the concrete pipeline descriptor, adapter capabilities and device generation. A cooked module/descriptor does **not** mean that a GPU pipeline is ready; native driver caches are disposable accelerators, not authority. V1 prepares newly required pipelines before first playable use, later required region activation or an admitted live profile commit, and rebuilds handles after device loss. Backend APIs differ: WebGPU recommends async pipeline creation to avoid a deferred queue stall, but no general asynchronous/nonblocking guarantee is inferred for every native backend; the selected adapter must expose preparation completion, failure and measured stalls. [WebGPU pipeline creation](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/#dom-gpudevice-createrenderpipelineasync) documents this distinction.

Required shader absence or preparation failure blocks that target/view/region or leaves the previous profile generation active, with a tuple and dependency cause. An optional missing feature may use only a declared, cooked, prepared and semantically acceptable presentation fallback; masked occlusion/shadow or velocity may not silently disappear or turn opaque. F2/F4/T1/T2 distinguish source validity, cooked closure, package/offline availability and actual device readiness. A live switch reserves old/new overlap, prepares required pipelines, commits one generation before extraction, invalidates affected histories and retires old resources after leases/fences. Repeated runtime specialization is limited to finite admitted on-demand content; dynamic uniform/branch dimensions are suitable only where bindings, geometry and pass meaning stay compatible. Tuple count, bytes, compile time and peak transition cost are reported and capped, with excessive variant growth rejected rather than silently stripped. [Unity variant stripping](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.5/manual/materials-and-shaders/shaders/shader-troubleshooting/shader-reducing/shader-variants/shader-variant-stripping) records the real missing-variant risk; this design's RENDER-SPEC now fixes WGSL/Naga, the shared ABI and finite target specialization policy.

### Analytical closure for the baseline

The baseline solves a bounded approximation to the outgoing radiance at a visible surface: `L_o(x,ω_o) = L_e + ∫_Ω f_r(x,ω_i,ω_o) L_i(x,ω_i) V(x,ω_i) |n·ω_i| dω_i`. Direct lights discretize the integral over authored area/directional/point sources; shadow maps estimate `V`; baked or dynamically updated probes approximate the remaining low-frequency incident field. The material BRDF uses energy-accounted diffuse plus microfacet specular (GGX distribution, Smith masking, Schlick Fresnel as a practical candidate). [Walter et al. §5](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~srm/publications/EGSR07-btdf.pdf) derive GGX/Smith reflection/transmission terms; the engine's diffuse and layering closure remains an explicit material-design commission. Texture content supplies surface parameters in declared colour spaces, not prelit colour. Reflection probes encode missing offscreen radiance; planar reflection is reserved for known planar receivers; screen traces may refine only where matching screen depth exists. This closure does not pretend probes solve sharp dynamic GI or that SSR sees offscreen objects. High-end GI/ray providers replace the indirect/reflection estimate while preserving the same incident-radiance contract. [Lumen's disclosed representation limits](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/lumen-technical-details-in-unreal-engine) support treating geometric proxy fidelity and update cost as part of a GI provider, not mere quality knobs.

Let `P` be shaded pixels, `K` average lights touching a pixel, `N` total local lights, `C` clusters, and `I` light-cluster intersections. A naive forward pass performs roughly `O(PN)` light evaluations; cluster construction costs approximately `O(N + I)` plus binning, and shading `O(PK)`. It does not remove overdraw or shadow-map cost. A deferred path also shades `O(PK)` but requires writing/reading `G` bytes per pixel of externally materialized G buffer unless a backend keeps it tile-local; at resolution `P`, that transfer is at least order `P·G` and grows with MSAA/sample count. Thus clustered forward is the portable bandwidth-aware default when `K ≪ N` and transparency is material; dense overlapping lights can justify a specialized hybrid after predicted crossover is measured. Scene content and viewport determine `K`, `I`, overdraw and G-buffer traffic; no universal fps number follows from these equations.

Projected detail drives LOD: for bound error `e_world` at depth `z`, perspective screen error is approximately `e_px ≈ e_world·f_px/z`; orthographic error is `e_px ≈ e_world·s_px_per_m`, independent of depth. Therefore V2 must choose geometry/texture/shadow LOD from projected error and author-defined silhouette/coverage limits, with hysteresis, rather than world distance. Conservative culling keeps casters that can affect visible receivers even if their own mesh is offscreen. A simplified shadow or ray proxy may be used only with an explicit maximum silhouette/occlusion error by light/view regime. Virtualized geometry can reduce authoring LOD burden but adds cluster storage, feedback/residency and fallback costs documented by [Nanite's production design](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf); it is optional.

For a ray parameter `s` increasing from camera to surface/background, a segment `[a,b]` transforms radiance as `L(a) = S(a,b) + T(a,b)L(b)`, with `T(a,b) = exp(-∫[a,b] σ_t(u)du)` and `S(a,b) = ∫[a,b] T(a,s)j(s,ω_o)ds`. Here `σ_t` is extinction in m⁻¹ and `j` is scene-linear radiance per metre. Ordered **disjoint** near/far segments compose exactly under their declared scattering approximation as `(T_n T_f, S_n + T_n S_f)`. For a single-scattering closure, `j = σ_s ∫ p(ω_i→ω_o)L_in(ω_i)dω_i + emission`; incident direct light uses the *total* relevant light-path extinction, shadows and cloud visibility once. Other multiple-scattering/ambient closures must be declared. For overlapping media, combine their extinction and jointly evaluated source/light inputs before integrating, or select one provider that jointly preintegrates all overlap. Independently preintegrated whole-interval `(T,S)` pairs do not retain enough spatial/source information to combine arbitrary overlap: an absorbing layer moved before versus behind a narrow fog emission can keep each isolated pair unchanged while changing the combined image. Ordered screen layers or a homogeneous-mixture reconstruction are admissible only as named approximations with support and error policy, never as exact overlap. E1 declares medium identity, support and input representation; V3 owns integration, depth order and one-time light accounting; V1 executes the resulting pass graph. Sky is terminal boundary radiance and an opaque surface truncates the ray; water/transparent interfaces declare their optical boundary and depths. Sampling cost is roughly pixels × samples per ray, with temporal reuse trading current sampling for history error. [Hillaire's sky paper](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf) offers a production-oriented atmosphere closure for its specified model, not automatic interoperability with arbitrary local fog.

Temporal reconstruction maps current unjittered pixel `x_t` to previous `x_{t-1}` using motion `v = x_{t-1}-x_t`, samples prior colour, then uses depth/normal/material/coverage and reactive tests to bound accumulation. In a simple stable-surface model `H_t = (1-α) H_{t-1}(x+v) + α C_t`; `α` rises on disocclusion, rapid shading changes and unreliable velocity. This is an estimator, not a fixed engine-wide blend factor. Previous deformed position must be evaluated for wind, skinning, morphs and water; treating these as static would bias `v` and produce ghosts. Transparent effects without trustworthy depth/velocity communicate coverage/reactivity so reconstruction does not lock to stale opaque history. The current/previous clip-space convention, jitter subtraction and exposure ratio are part of the interface. [AMD's integration guidance](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler/) supports these inputs. Each view carries its own projection, jitter, exposure and history; world state is shared.

## Frame, state and data contracts

**Snapshot/packet lifetime:** consume W1 read-model state through C02 SnapshotLease, never direct mutable world/solver
storage. V2 owns extracted RenderPacket data and its staging/GPU leases before releasing snapshot readers. GPU fences
retire the packet independently. Prior deformation/effect samples have bounded per-view retention; a skipped view
outside that horizon resets history. Optional unsubmitted extraction can be cancelled/deferred under pressure, but
in-use snapshot pages cannot be reclaimed; required reader backpressure/overload is owned by F1.

**Future N1 peer boundary:** N1 replicates a purpose-specific authoritative
gameplay projection, not V2 packets, GPU products or render histories. A peer's
target-specific `BuildRevision` may differ because its shader, texture and GPU
payloads differ; N1 negotiates a separately derived gameplay/rule/schema/content
compatibility lock. Authority-owned E1 weather, visibility and physical-medium
facts that affect rules retain that lock and their committed sample/version;
local atmosphere/fog drawing may approximate them only within its declared
presentation policy. A peer may synchronize a coherent replica while V1 is
still preparing its local GPU pipelines, but its playable view remains loading
until required tuples are device-ready or a declared fallback is ready. N1's
bounded baseline/queue policy continues during that wait; slow local
preparation cannot pin server/world snapshots indefinitely or silently weaken
gameplay content. A predicted interaction uses the authority's physical
dependency closure, never local GPU ripple, shader or media history.

Coordinate basis, handedness, camera depth convention, exact scene-working colour primaries, precision, world-origin policy and frame budget must be fixed in F3/P2 and D2 before implementing these contracts. Proposed values below are semantic, not an API signature. World positions and bounds use metres in a documented world frame; velocities use m/s; time uses monotonic seconds plus a committed simulation tick. The portable baseline uses one declared scene-linear RGB working basis for V3 transport composition; a spectral provider either converts through a declared, qualified approximation or retains spectral evaluation inside one joint provider before conversion. All overlapping media and incident-light inputs must share the selected integration basis; incompatible bases fail F2 admission. Radiance/illuminance use declared linear photometric or radiometric units; artist-authored multipliers are labelled separately. Render targets remain scene-linear until V4 output. RENDER-SPEC selects Rec.709/D65 photometric RGB, WGSL/Naga and the initial joint air/fog approximation with explicit error targets. A richer spectral provider requires its own conversion/error contract.

1. F1 advances simulation and publishes one immutable `WorldSnapshot(tick, t_sim, origin_epoch)` after physics/animation/world/environment producers commit. V2 extracts once per snapshot or incrementally from versioned changes. A rendering frame may interpolate between two committed snapshots at `t_present`; it never advances world state. Every `ViewRequest` has stable view ID, pose/projection (perspective or orthographic), near/far policy, viewport/output resolution, jitter and camera-cut generation. Each temporal provider also tracks `history_sample_time` and `history_frame_id`: the sample actually retained in *that provider's* history for this view. We use `t_prev_present` only as shorthand for `history_sample_time`, not the last OS-displayed or merely submitted frame; queued/dropped frames and skipped reflection/editor/map views make those differ. Prior matrices, deformation and exposure must refer to that same retained image. Reflection/capture/editor views use the same world snapshot and explicit budget class.
2. W2/F4 activate stable asset/instance IDs and provide cooked mesh/material/texture dependencies and bounds. V2 owns GPU-ready instance tables, visible sets and LOD selection per view, with a CPU fallback. Geometric LOD, shadow LOD, ray-query proxy and collision form may differ; versioned source IDs, transforms, conservative bounds and declared tolerances join them. Loading substitutes a visible, shadow-safe proxy or intentionally omits a not-ready **optional** presentation object only under an authored policy; required geometry/material/pass pipelines block the corresponding playable view or region until ready. It must never silently use unloaded geometry for physics while pretending the full visual is present. W2 owns region lifetime; V2 retires representations after GPU fences and handles device recreation from F4 cooked products through V1 pipeline preparation.
3. V2 emits per-view opaque/masked draws, transparent order groups, skinned/deformed current and previous positions, material and light-link IDs, conservative shadow-caster sets, and depth/velocity data. **Velocity contract:** `previous_pixel_xy − current_pixel_xy` under unjittered projection, in current render-target pixel units, origin top left, X right/Y down. P4 owns pose/skinning/morph samples and conservative deformed bounds with skeleton/content version; E1 owns prior wind/water/cloud deformation, V5 owns prior particle state, and material animation owns prior parameter evaluation. V2 combines those samples with current/previous instance and P2 view matrices after the committed physics pose. Providers must retain/evaluate samples at that view/provider's `history_sample_time` or mark correspondence invalid. New, teleported, topology-changing or ragdoll-authority-switched objects mark history invalid unless explicit old/new correspondence survives; alpha-tested foliage writes covered depth/velocity or explicit coverage/reactive classification. A visual displacement that has no matching prior-state path cannot quietly use rigid-body velocity. [Temporal composition study](../research/TEMPORAL-COMPOSITION.md#recommended-contracts-and-owners) derives the motion operator.
4. V3 resolves scene lights and environmental illumination at the same `t_present` (or names allowed lag). Direct source radiance, directional transmittance/occlusion and indirect radiance are separate contributions, each counted once. Shadow providers receive caster geometry/proxies and return visibility by light/position/direction, with resolution/support and update age. Cloud attenuation is a light visibility input, not a sky-background tint. The material contract specifies at least base reflectance, normal, roughness, metalness/transmission where applicable, emissive output, two-sided and alpha mode, plus extension/version/texture-space metadata. Material and light extensions must declare which forward/deferred/shadow/reflection paths support them; unsupported combinations fail content validation or select an authored fallback, never silently change physical meaning.
5. V1 validates one explicit scene-linear composition graph before V4. V3/E1/V5 produce typed surface and medium layers; V3 renderer integration specifies their depth ordering and V1 checks pass/resource edges. Opaque depth provides the first surface. Transparent water/glass/particles provide per-layer depth, alpha/coverage, velocity confidence and reactivity where available; a single nearest transparent depth/vector is an explicitly approximate adapter because refracted or reflected background can have different motion. E1/V5 medium inputs declare identity, bounded support and validity in ray/view/time/light/basis, then one of: preintegrated `(T,S)` for ordered nonoverlapping intervals; coefficient/scattering/phase/emission access to V3's joint integrator for overlapping support; or one jointly precomposing provider that owns the entire overlap. F2 rejects independent overlapping pairs without a declared approximation and prevents a constituent from being integrated again. Composition follows ordered depth intervals: background radiance attenuated by foreground medium, then accumulated in-scattering, then surface/translucent layers at their correct depths. Source lighting for overlapping fog/air/clouds uses shared total attenuation and occlusion, not an unqualified sum of independently lit `S` values. Surface shading samples atmosphere/fog transmittance at its actual depth; sky is terminal background, not an implicit sea or wall. Clouds occlude terrain and are occluded by foreground geometry; water receives environment light/reflections and occludes underwater content. Outside a provider's altitude/range/direction/time support, F2 rejects the request or uses an authored, labelled fallback; no stale LUT or silently clamped optical ray. Where sorting or multi-layer transparency is approximate, that limitation is an explicit material/profile rule. [Temporal composition study](../research/TEMPORAL-COMPOSITION.md#analytical-operating-model-and-why-this-baseline) derives the operator.
6. V4 consumes complete scene-linear colour before tone mapping, opaque and optional transparent depth, velocity, coverage/reactivity and layer metadata, exposure/pre-exposure, view ID and discontinuity flags. V4 owns **image reconstruction/display** history per view and algorithm. V3/E1/V5 own their shadow/reflection/volume/effect histories, with V1 managing persistent GPU lifetime and a shared per-view invalidation signal. Resize, camera cut, FOV/projection switch, world-origin shift, shader/material replacement, significant lighting discontinuity or incompatible profile/provider change invalidates or explicitly resamples affected histories, including effect histories. A future N1 correction carries confirmed sample time plus relevant content, domain and environment-source epochs: V2/V3/E1/V5 reset or explicitly reproject affected geometry, shadow, reflection and medium histories as well as V4 image history. Rendering may smooth a permitted pose correction but cannot blend across an incompatible optical/physical source as continuous history. Spatial AA/native resolution is the selected baseline; temporal reconstruction is optional. AMD's [FSR integration](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler/) identifies depth, motion, exposure and reactive/transparency inputs; [MetalFX](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/MetalFX) similarly requires colour/depth/motion for temporal scaling. Their exact masks and history algorithms stay provider-owned.
7. V4 maps linear scene light through exposure, tone/gamut transform and output encoding to SDR or qualified HDR targets. Exposure uses the view and an authored metering policy; editor comparisons can hold exposure. A cloud, sun or window transition can change exposure, but that change must not make temporal histories compare unlike luminance without compensation. The scene-linear layer set included before reconstruction is part of the composition signature; an intentionally after-upscale effect needs its own AA and cannot inherit absent velocity. The colour transform and output metadata are versioned content, with debug cards and reference captures for each target.

Submitted-image evidence uses the planned [T2 capture handoff](../../plans/T2-CAPTURE/DESIGN.md) under C06/C07/C12: V1 owns graph
copy/readback lifetime, V4 identifies output/colour stage, and T2 packages samples. A sequence taps the actual
submitted temporal output; an endpoint still remains a different capture mode. G3t/G4 consume this shared path
without introducing a second capture producer. Physical display/EDR qualification remains separate.

V2 retains existing determinant/membership and raster-policy ownership under C07. The scoped
[G3t design](../../plans/APPLE-TILE/DESIGN.md) preserves legacy two-sided behavior, open/unknown and near-singular meshes, direct tie
order and off-camera shadow casters without refactoring the experimental bucket route. Shared-range consolidation
is later V2/G3c work, not a G4/native-boundary prerequisite.

## Ownership and lifecycle at each boundary

| Boundary | Owner → consumer; units/time | Activation, off and failure rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Device and frame graph | V1 → V2–V5/E1; typed resource descriptors, usage, lifetime, queue and pass edges per render frame | Persistent assets/history have explicit owners; transient resources end with graph. Unsupported optional feature rejects provider selection. Device loss pauses presentation, recreates GPU products from F4/world snapshot and clears histories; game authority persists. |
| Render scene and visibility | W1/W2/P4/F4 → V2 → V3/V5; IDs, metres, bounds, current/previous pose at `t_present` | No view means no per-view culling/draw, though shared render assets may remain for another view. No render provider retires GPU representations at fence. Stream-out cancels pending work by generation. |
| Lighting and shadows | W1/E1/V3 → V3/materials/volumes; direction, radiance, declared scene-linear RGB working basis, visibility, spatial support, sample time | If visible clouds are off, a selected cloud-attenuation provider can remain; if none is needed, cloud cost retires. If dynamic GI is off, declared baked/probe/ambient substitute supplies indirect light. No substitute means incompatible profile. |
| Reconstruction and display | V2/V3/V5 → V4; linear colour, depth, pixel velocity, masks, exposure, view metadata per presentation frame | Spatial fallback if temporal provider absent/unsupported. Each inactive view retires its histories under policy; profile switch invalidates changed inputs. Output-surface failure suspends presentation, not world simulation. |
| Effects | Gameplay/physics/environment events → V5; stable source/event ID, world position/normal, strength, lifespan, seed and simulation time | Event authority stays with producer. V5 may discard distant cosmetic particles; audio/gameplay do not derive from their visible count. Disabled effect has no particle/decal GPU work; persistent gameplay traces remain in owning systems. |

V1 should report actual GPU allocation, pass time where queries are supported, pipeline compilation stalls, shader variants and culled passes. Optional timestamp queries are not guaranteed by WebGPU. Debug views expose overdraw, residency, visibility rejection, shadow invalidations, missing velocity, reactive masks, history rejection, volume depth and each light contribution. Diagnostics are gated so normal output does not retain readback buffers. [Epic RDG Insights](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/render-dependency-graph-in-unreal-engine) is production precedent for graph and resource inspection.

## Representation and content obligations

- **Visibility:** CPU frustum/occlusion and instance batching form a simple reliable baseline; GPU culling, meshlets, virtualized geometry and HLOD are candidate providers. LOD selection must preserve silhouettes, material coverage, shadows and velocity, with stable dither/reactive rules across transitions. Orthographic views often keep distant geometry large on screen; distance alone is not an LOD rule. V2 can use coarse proxies for offscreen shadow/reflection casters. A visibility result is scoped to one view; shared GPU scene data is not.
- **Materials and decals:** A small validated baseline material model with texture packing and colour-space declarations supports cooked assets. Authoring previews use the same material/lighting path as the runtime target or identify a fidelity difference. Decals may be projected, mesh-based or material-layer changes; receiver masks, opacity, depth ordering, normal/roughness contribution and mobile fallbacks need an explicit path. A G-buffer-dependent decal system cannot be assumed with forward shading.
- **Lighting:** Sun/local lights, shadow casting, light linking, sky/ambient probes and reflection capture have distinct ownership. Stationary baked light/probe data and dynamic providers can coexist only with clear double-counting rules. Advanced GI or ray reflection inputs require matching geometry/material proxies and update budgets. Screen-space-only reflections cannot provide offscreen content; water and mirrors need a declared fallback.
- **Effects:** Particles/decal/trail emitters are visual consumers of typed events. GPU particle simulation is optional and cannot be the owner of gameplay collision or persistent weather deposits. Particle shadows, lighting, depth fade, velocity and transparency classification are material/profile decisions, with an authored lower-cost fallback.
- **Automation:** All renderer assets, quality profiles, materials and scene settings use versioned schemas and stable IDs. CLI/API supports import, validation, cook, comparison capture, shader warmup, unsupported-combination report and measured profile trace. An AI agent can change a scoped resource and ask “what changed?” via dependency hashes, semantic diff, visual captures and pass/resource metrics; no hidden editor-only state is required. Human approval of appearance remains separate.

## Quality profiles and cost reductions

The same semantic world may select different representations. No numerical frame, RAM, energy or download budget has been agreed; the following order is a hypothesis for measurement, not a performance claim.

| Profile | Plausible path | Reductions and cues at risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native desktop/Mac | Full raster baseline, higher shadow/volume/texture budgets; qualifying optional GI/reflection/reconstruction providers | More resident assets and history consume memory; advanced paths require family/API checks and capture comparison. |
| Browser WebGPU | Same material/world meaning; bounded lights/instances, compressed cooked assets, fewer shadow/volume samples, spatial or qualified temporal AA | First load, pipeline compilation, memory and feature limits may dominate. No native Metal-only path assumed. |
| Mobile app | Tile/bandwidth-aware forward candidate, smaller resident working set, simpler shadows/ambient/reflections/cloud representations, lower overdraw, thermal pacing | Alpha foliage, particles, full-screen buffers and external G buffers can dominate power; preserve silhouette/light cues before effects. |

Rank actual reductions after profiling: remove invisible work and redundant passes/uploads; reduce view/secondary-view frequency and resolution; simplify shadow and indirect providers; reduce volume and reflection update costs; reduce particle/transparent overdraw; then geometric/material detail. Each switch names work and residency saved, visual cue lost, transition and history rule. Turning off a visible cloud draw is not proof that cloud-shadow preparation was removed if lighting still consumes it. Whole-frame measurement includes simulation, animation, loading, CPU submission, GPU, memory, shader preparation and sustained thermal behavior. The [performance skill](../../../.agents/skills/thrum-engineering/references/performance.md) governs evidence; no current measurement is being transferred from the older water build.

## Representative execution cases

1. **Top-down game without a visible sky.** F2 selects lighting plus a cheap authored/cloud attenuation source, excludes sky/cloud draw, and validates dependent reflection/ambient substitutes. At committed time `t`, V3 receives Sun direction/radiance and attenuation, V2 extracts orthographic casters/receivers, V4 owns this camera's history. A cloud-shadow change touches light visibility; it does not require rendering a volume. Disabling the attenuation too removes its caches and falls back to unoccluded Sun only if the profile allows it.
2. **Exterior camera crossing a shore.** Weather/wind and water update once, terrain/foliage/ship/water publish render products and previous-state deformation, and V2 makes the current view plus a bounded reflection view. V3 combines Sun/cloud visibility, sky/indirect, local shadows and water reflection fallback. V5 composes spray at actual water contact; V4 uses water/foliage velocities or reactive flags. Streaming a shore cell in changes V2 geometry and shadow validity; V4 rejects the affected history. A surface contact query comes from environment/physics, never inferred from the water pixels.
3. **Interior with many local lights and a window.** V3 selects bounded direct-light lists, qualified indirect provider and environment radiance at the window. The opaque/transparent/volume compositor handles glass, local fog and exterior depth. A deferred/hybrid challenger may outperform forward here; it must still work with orthographic views and mobile/browser fallbacks. Disabling outdoor sky draw does not erase window illumination.
4. **Device loss or profile change.** F1/F2 pause or change presentation at a safe boundary, V1 retires/recreates GPU resources and prepares new-device pipelines from F4's cooked products, V2 rebuilds render tables from stable IDs, and V3/E1/V5/V4 invalidate affected effect and image histories. World, physics, saves, audio and weather continue according to platform pause policy. A failed optional provider falls back only to a declared substitute, with a logged capability diagnosis; required pipelines keep the playable view loading or the prior profile active.

## Predictions, risks and qualification queue

| Structural prediction/risk | Bounded validation and rejection |
| --- | --- |
| GPU API semantics | Baseline requires compute, storage/indirect where actually used, render attachments, explicit resources and recovery; optional native features are isolated. Verify feature/limit matrix on target WebGPU, Metal, D3D/Vulkan devices. Reject a proposed backend if it cannot implement the *selected* baseline without semantic forks; do not change light/geometry mathematics merely because a demo backend is convenient. |
| Clustered forward baseline | From `O(PK)` lighting and no required `P·G` G-buffer transfer, predict advantage on tile/bandwidth-limited devices when light overlap is bounded. Measure traffic, overdraw and complete frame for derived stress ranges, including transparent materials and orthographic projection. If overlap makes `K≈N` in a supported profile, add a specialized deferred/tile-hybrid provider; retain the common baseline. |
| Projected-error LOD | Prediction: `e_px` thresholds preserve geometry appearance across perspective/orthographic cameras when silhouettes and coverage are accounted for. Check stream/LOD motion with foliage, skinned character, offscreen casters and two views. Reject a proxy error model when visible/shadow/velocity bounds fail; revise representation, not the acceptance camera. |
| Temporal estimator and media algebra | The reprojection and ordered `(T,L)` composition predict stable cross-effect motion when all motion/depth/reactivity producers are correct. Test water, glass, foliage, character, particles, clouds, fog, camera cuts and exposure with isolated ground truth. Reject a provider if systematic ghosts or wrong depth remain after correct inputs; retain spatial fallback. |
| Indirect/reflection closure | Probe/bake baseline predicts low-frequency indirect and offscreen reflection coverage, with known limits for dynamic sharp bounce. Check exterior/interior/top-down against a high-sample reference and measure stale-stream updates. Add higher-order provider when a required cue is outside the baseline approximation, rather than tuning probes to fake it. |
| Cross-platform visual review | Establish reference captures/video, calibrated cards, daylight/dusk/night and moving cameras on each output target; automated image checks and agent visual review compare them with references or ground-truth oracles. This qualifies implementation and perception; it does not choose the structural model from an attractive still. |

Detailed commissions should derive algorithms, bounds and expected output from these structural models, then run a discriminating validation at agreed target budgets. Questions that can still change architecture are backend/API exposure, mobile tile specialization, full material/transparent composition across shading modes, and geometric/shadow/temporal representation consistency. A failed prediction revises the model or declared support domain; it does not initiate a sequence of unrelated visual experiments.

Even disjoint `(T,S)` segments compose exactly only relative to a shared declared transport closure: each source
term must use incident lighting consistent with the combined scene, including other media on its light paths.
View-ray disjointness alone does not justify independently lit preintegration. Otherwise recompute jointly or
declare the lighting approximation and its validity.

## Reconciled first-game and replica scope

The first R0 game needs only its named camera/material/light closure, not every renderer family provider. Pipeline
counts and boot preparation caps in RENDER-SPEC/BASELINE-SPEC are reference-recipe gates, not general SDK ceilings.
Gameplay-affecting source edits restart; qualified visual preview retains F4/V1 preparation, lease and history rules.
A replica admits lifecycle plus required first/dependent state atomically before V2 extraction. N1 action results and
state admission do not wait for GPU preparation; a synchronized world can have a loading view. Optional speculative
V5 cues reconcile by confirmed event origin, never by envelope acknowledgement. Water/atmosphere style providers can
vary by target only within their shared semantic/contact/cue bounds. No full-world renderer replacement is required.
