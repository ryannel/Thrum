## Decision and observed fault

Recommend one bounded replacement of the flat coarse-cohort grammar: **an exposed-surface hierarchy with measured cap protrusion/spacing and a physical-metre optical boundary**. Retain the world/event/lifecycle interface, but replace the shape construction; do not merely increase the existing loop's node count or noise amplitude. The hierarchy is an authored geometric approximation informed by cloud-shape research, not a buoyancy solver. If its controlled source-only witness still reads as a compound of balls, adopt a detailed prepared geometry master at the same seam rather than growing another sequence of cosmetic patches.

Two source-level reasons are analytically visible:

1. All10 cohorts occupy essentially the **same large scale**: nominal component radii range229–428m in X and163–275m in Y. Repeating generations gives renewal, but does not create an exposed-medium/fine hierarchy. Smaller values from the same coarse-radius distribution cannot create nested cauliflower relief.
2. The density threshold width is dimensionless `.24`. For a representative axis radius R, derivative of the threshold argument with respect to radial distance is approximately `(1+p*eta*(1-m)^(p-1))/R`. The physical density transition is therefore about `.24R/(1+...)`, often30–80m here. A fixed37m detail octave can be buried within that optical transition. More field resolution cannot make a missing medium-scale surface or an overly broad density boundary into a cloud.

These are mechanisms capable of producing the observed fault. Their individual contribution was not isolated by this still. The proposal below corrects both explicitly, then tests representation separately.

## Concrete geometry producer

### 1. Gross formation and scale bands

Keep a filled condensation body and3–5 active plume/head cohorts for a first approximately1.2km-wide,500–700m-high Cu. Shape the gross silhouette with separated source centres/head trajectories and a shallow cap, not one stretched oval. The initial correction uses **spheres at every level**, including the gross heads/backing body, with exact d=r-|q-c| metre distances. There is no generic ellipsoid closest-point solver, anisotropic scale or unselected coarse implicit primitive in this first producer. The condensation plane remains y=0 with its named12m feather.

The initial *proposed* geometric bands are:

| Level | Radius / support role | Exposed protrusion and intended cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L0 | Gross180–320m heads/body | Several uneven towers/lobes and the overall base-to-top silhouette. |
| L1 | Medium60–130m caps attached to exposed L0 surfaces | Approximately30–70m relief: the main cauliflower heads/furrows, observable without fine noise. |
| L2 | Fine20–40m caps attached to exposed L1 heads/flanks | Approximately10–25m relief: subsidiary scallops and irregular silhouette at close/native inspection. |
| Below L2 | Initially absent | Do not add unresolved noise as a substitute. Later fixed material roughness may supply smaller statistical detail only after the explicit geometry passes. |

These are authored content scales, not claimed universal cloud statistics. Radius/spacing/height distributions vary by level and plume region, with correlated active heads and weaker side detail. A scale hierarchy must appear on the visible front/side/top, rather than being numerically present only inside an opaque body.

### 2. Place children on the exposed parent surface

For a spherical parent with centre c_p(t), radius R_p(t), and a child normal n_j(t), its attachment point is `a_j=c_p+R_p*n_j`. All initial parents are spheres, so this point/normal is explicit; no ray/view determines exposure. A future nonspherical parent needs a separately selected chart/distance/metric contract.

Cook deterministic variable-radius surface sites using weighted Poisson/repulsion placement in parent material coordinates. Candidate sites cover the upper head and side shoulders, with lower/base-facing sites suppressed. Test outward exposure against the other gross/coarser bodies at cook time: a site well inside another body does not count toward the required visible hierarchy. If future growth hides it naturally, union geometry handles that; do not camera-reseed a replacement. Enforce pairwise spacing in the parent tangent metric, not independent XYZ jitter.

For a child of radius r_j, define its centre during growth as:

```
g_j = smoothstep(birth, birth+growth_duration, t)
r_j(t) = r_final * g_j
c_j(t) = a_j(t) - [(1-beta_j)*r_j(t) + d_attach*(1-g_j)] * n_j(t)
```

Use beta approximately .45–.65. At full growth, the child centre remains inside its parent by `(1-beta)*r`, while its outer cap protrudes by beta*r. At zero growth it begins d_attach inside the parent's surface. Choose d_attach at least the parent's physical density-ramp width, and verify the birth location is in a filled surviving parent. Before admission, check that the child remains attached while developing; if a curved/moving parent invalidates the simple attachment formula, cook the continuous centre/normal track and its overlap bound. Do not repair it after spawn by snapping the child.

Radius-zero and sub-micrometre support use the existing declared numerical birth policy: no inverse, inactive support, finite float32 upload and bounded omitted optical depth. The common current pose remains float32-defined for CPU/frame agreement. None of this authorizes a whole-formation fade.

### 3. Guarantee actual cap/valley relief

For equal neighbouring caps over a locally planar parent, let centre spacing be s and common child radius r. Peak height is beta*r. Their midpoint height is

`max(0, -(1-beta)*r + sqrt(r²-(s/2)²))`, for s<2r,

where0 is the parent's backing surface. Thus the peak-to-furrow difference is

`min(beta*r, r-sqrt(r²-(s/2)²))`.

At s=1.6r and beta=.55, relief is .4r while the midpoint remains .15r above the backing body. This produces a visible valley between connected caps. At s near1.8r, the backing parent fills the valley rather than exposing an empty gap. Too-small spacing buries both caps into one broad bulge; too-large spacing exposes isolated buttons. Therefore admit a bounded spacing/protrusion window (initially roughly1.45–1.8r locally), varying radii/sector organisation to avoid a regular foam lattice.

The same rule recurses at L2, on the **exposed cap** of its L1 parent. Admit r_final no larger than .45 of the parent's local curvature radius, and do not begin a child until the current parent can sustain that scale/attachment. For example, parent curvature radius>=2r bounds the planar midpoint sag at s=1.6r to about.167r; a nominal.4r furrow is not erased by that curvature alone. The .45 rule is stricter. The cook must check actual relief/overlap on the curved parent; the planar equation is a discriminating design calculation, not a universal guarantee for arbitrarily curved heads. It gives a reason for smaller convex heads and concave furrows at each scale. It does not assert that every mathematically legal setting looks like a cloud; close front/side/top source-only images must reject beads, broccoli, regular foam or visible ball construction.

### 4. One same-condensate field, in metres

For a spherical cohort define positive-inside distance `d_j(q,t)=r_j(t)-|q-c_j(t)|`. Introduce local physical erosion E_j in metres, then publish

```
d(q,t) = max_j [d_j(q,t)-E_j(q,t)]
sigma(q,t) = sigma0 * smoothstep(0,w_edge,d(q,t)) * base_mask(y)
```

Use one sigma0 optical calibration per content profile for this correction (retain .019m^-1 initially), with w_edge=12m for the first close256-grid witness. An8m alternative needs finer source sampling or a smaller domain; other values require the same resolution/optical-depth reasoning. The field is filled, not a shell; each child replaces part of the boundary of the same condensate. Max-union prevents summing overlap as extra extinction. It creates no support outside an actual cohort. A density ramp applied **after** the geometric union avoids a hidden change of optical thickness per hierarchy level.

Anisotropic primitives are excluded from this first correction; changing this choice would need a separately reviewed distance/cost contract. A union cusp can remain a geometric furrow: the renderer integrates coefficients, not an invented glossy normal shader. If those seams look artificial in density/support views, correct the surface blending locally with a bounded metre-scale rule, not a global smooth-union that fills unrelated gaps. The first correction should use ordinary union and reveal that risk rather than adding another unqualified blending mechanism.

### 5. Selected local erosion field and resolved width (SOURCE-R1)

A bounded delay alone was incomplete. Select the following material-plane field, using the current float32-defined sphere centre c_j(t), an orthonormal cooked material frame O_j(t), final radius R_j and physical material coordinate xi=O_j^T(q-c_j):

```
A_j = R_j + w_edge
phi_j = 2*pi*hash01(event_seed,node_id)
a_material = (cos(phi_j),0.35,sin(phi_j)) / sqrt(1+0.35^2)
delta_j(xi) = (span_j/2) * clamp(1 + dot(a_material,xi)/R_j,0,2)
E_j(q,t) = A_j * smoothstep(t_stop_j+delta_j,
                           t_stop_j+delta_j+T_erase_j, t)
span_j = 0.32 * T_erase_j * R_j/(R_j+w_edge)
```

The hash is the retained deterministic event/node seed, not frame noise. Its azimuth selects a local drying side; positive material-Y component makes lower/side material clear earlier than the upper opposite side. Different cohort stops/directions and existing nested geometry provide local, asynchronous erosion. No extra high-frequency noise or cut mask is introduced. A plane front may still leave clean beads/buttons; that remains a visual rejection, not a promise that the formula alone produces natural breakup.

The initial positive T_erase values are300s/150s/90s for L0/L1/L2. Cook admission requires every derived retirement `t_stop+span+T_erase`<=1800s, sufficient parent density at every later child birth, and no negative/zero R,T or w. The field is defined on all q by the clamp; within a sphere of radius r(t)<=R its unclamped argument lies in[0,2]. Thus delta is continuous, bounded in[0,span], with spatial Lipschitz bound span/(2R). Cubic smoothstep has maximum derivative1.5, giving

`||grad E|| <= (R+w)*1.5/T_erase * span/(2R) = .24`.

After CPU pose/basis packing, validate the actual rounded normal and parameters satisfy the same bound with a conservative ceiling .25; do not silently rescale a failed upload. CPU and WGSL receive/evaluate the same rounded current centre, material-plane direction, final radius/reciprocal, A, span, stop and T. The packed admission inequality is explicitly `.75*A*span*abs(inv_R_final)*length(direction)/T<=.25`. Rigid global transforms preserve this norm; no coordinate-normalization-by-current-growing-radius appears in delta. Motion correspondence uses that same material frame.

Before erosion, d is an exact sphere distance and12m is the physical ramp width along a regular boundary normal. During erosion, F=d-E is generally **not** a signed distance. Away from centres/union ties its gradient satisfies .75<=|grad F|<=1.25, so the material smoothstep transition along a regular resulting normal (before the separate base mask) is bounded between `w/1.25` and `w/.75`: **9.6–16m for w=12m**. The max union selects one regular branch almost everywhere; contacts/centres are handled by the direct sphere/field oracle rather than an invented differentiable normal. This bounds the erosion operator's own scale and does not claim all union creases have one smooth normal. The12m base feather remains a separate product factor; source/base intersections must pass the direct-field optical oracle and are not covered by a claim that the final product is a universal12m signed-distance ramp.

At the finite last departure E=A>=R+w, so F<=-w everywhere, including every centre. There is no whole-source alpha multiplier. The first final source extent must have largest grid pitch<=4.8m to retain at least two cells across the9.6m minimum drying ramp; at256 this means largest final extent<=1228.8m. Keep the **final** Cu, including child protrusions and guard, near1.2km; do not append the hierarchy outside an already1.2km gross envelope and crop it. Larger final domains need a larger admitted grid or a revised w/feature profile, followed by its own visibility check.

## Lifecycle, renewal and material maps

This is still an authored rising/rolling approximation, not “buoyancy” proved by naming it.

- **Formation/growth:** coarse moist/head episodes retain the current filled body. L1 begins only when the parent is large/alive enough to provide its attachment; L2 follows the actual L1 growth. Local radius growth creates new material support. The head can rise while a child emerges, so the front moves out of an existing body rather than appearing as a detached sphere.
- **Mature renewal:** coarse heads can persist for several hundred seconds. Their active top sectors emit asynchronous L1 cells; L1 cells feed shorter-lived L2 caps. Initial candidate growth/lifetime ranges are L1 growth60–150s/life180–420s, L2 growth20–60s/life60–180s. Pin the actual tracks and inspect normal motion; these are not measured meteorological rates. A rising head plus a bounded meridional outward-roll trajectory n_j(t) moves fresh material toward shoulders. Cook that trajectory from an explicit tangent speed; integrate angular speed U_tangent/R_parent(t), rather than multiplying the current frequency by total age. No random4D-noise evolution supplies renewal.
- **Loss/detrainment:** use the explicit bounded-gradient material-plane E_j above, with per-cohort stops/directions and finite terminal ages. Terminal fronts act locally in physical material metres; their true-distance/ramp limits are stated separately. Parent shutdown may leave accepted detached heads temporarily, but they must erode into irregular remnants; a field of clean detached beads is a rejection.
- **Finite completion:** all births/stops/terminal delays remain within a finite storyboard. A child whose parent stops feeding is not automatically deleted; its existing material follows its bounded track until its own local retirement. Only genuinely new feeding stops. Every family can still report exact empty support after its recorded last departure.

Each cohort has identity, parent lineage, fixed physical reference basis/detail metric and a cooked pose. For surviving material its local coordinate is `xi=O_j(t)^T(q-c_j(t))` in metres; radius growth reveals additional xi rather than stretching an existing texture through q/r. Its velocity is c_dot plus angular residual motion; the first rigid world wind is added exactly once by the event map. Birth, drying and union-dominance changes invalidate correspondence. Surface attachment of a new child does not claim a globally invertible map between all overlapping clouds.

The first wind domain is deliberately **rigid**: a constant shared world translation U*t and a fixed orthonormal event yaw. Apply its inverse to world queries exactly once; metre distance, the erosion-gradient bound and the density-ramp widths are preserved. The present preview does not implement a height-shear deformation. Nonrigid height wind or anisotropic source transforms are future admitted providers; they need metric/Jacobian/error qualification and cannot inherit the world12m claim from this local proof. Internal surface roll is the explicit cooked cohort pose, not another application of U.

Keep every retired parent's immutable reference geometry/trajectory available throughout the1024-node source history. Removing its density from the live field does **not** remove its pose as an attachment reference. Its geometric reference radius holds at R_final after growth through the remaining1800s history; an inactive density flag never replaces that reference radius/centre with zero. Existing children continue their cooked tracks without dereferencing a compacted/deleted parent or snapping to a new one. The first producer retains those parent reference tracks; an explicit detached continuation would be a later alternative requiring a C1 handoff. New births still require the actual eroded parent union to be filled. A child that already exists may outlive it; poor detached-bead appearance remains a rejection.

The world source, Cu→tower growth branch, cap-arrival/anvil emission and detached high-cloud lineage retain their IDs/time seams. This hierarchy supplies convective geometry only. Sc/St remain their column provider; Ci remains its filament provider. Main/light/receiver/reflection still sample the same sigma under one transport owner. No renderer/display correction is embedded into the source.

The optical consequence is also concrete. A fine cap adds height h≈14m. With sigma0=.019 and w_edge=12m, its extra normal optical depth over the backing surface is approximately `.019*(14-12/2)≈.15` for a locally planar ramp: about14% attenuation in that above-parent-plane segment before angular/lighting/filter effects. These are segment calculations, not the whole-cloud column-depth difference: the backing body and its own ramp also contribute, and a full ray oracle must include them. They quantify how a bulge can remain inside a broad fuzzy transition; they do not establish an image-contrast prediction. Changing field resolution alone cannot correct a physical density boundary tied to a300m radius.

Ray step, source filtering and image reconstruction must preserve this scale; use existing canonical-versus-field and point/transmittance oracles. A staircase band or vanished cap at matched canonical geometry is a representation/sampling defect, not a reason to add new noise. Do not shrink the physical boundary independently per consumer to make an attractive main image.

### Selected current-pose field/index path (SOURCE-R2)

Use **16×16×16 logical-voxel bricks over the256³ field**:16³=4096 bricks. This is an index over the existing dense current field, not a sparse field/cache redesign. It aligns the four-consecutive-Z packing groups with brick boundaries. Field addressing stays in the reviewed logical XYZ convention before channel packing.

For each latched source time:

1. Evaluate immutable parent/child tracks, including inactive ancestors needed by live children, then construct compact current **contributing** poses. Exclude retired or unresolved newborn density, not needed reference poses. Require finite float32 values, positive resolved radii/reciprocals, the .25 erosion-gradient admission, at most256 live contributors and the existing declared sigma limit. Stable source IDs remain distinct from compact buffer indices.
2. With Delta=extent/256 and brick coordinate b, define B as the closed voxel-cell hull `[lo+16*b*Delta,lo+16*(b+1)*Delta]`, expanded by the numeric guard below. CPU constructs each live sphere's current support AABB and its clamped integer brick range. The recipe bounds enclose the complete time-varying source with at least two largest source-cell pitches of empty collar on finite side/top faces; the known y=0 base boundary uses its declared zero/feather rule. Do not crop a drifting or protruding child to make the field fit. Since E>=0, the entire surviving support is inside that current sphere; erosion cannot enlarge it. Enumerate only that range and perform a conservative sphere–AABB intersection against each brick. Use outward0.01m numeric padding for the first local-coordinate profile (absolute local coordinates and extents<=4096m); the total conservative error from packed q/pose construction, sphere-length arithmetic and the E/time polynomial must remain below it under the first profile's finite input ranges. This is an explicit numerical admission to establish against the float32 interval/reference evaluator, not an assumption that a small epsilon proves every backend. 3. **Count pass:** zero4096 counters; count intersecting sphere IDs per brick. **Prefix sum:** exclusive offsets, reject if any raw count>64 or total list capacity is exceeded. **Fill pass:** repeat the same deterministic intersection and write compact node indices in stable source-ID order. No per-brick heap/vector is needed. Count and fill must use identical poses, bounds and generation; assert final fill counts equal the count pass. Deduplicate IDs if a future traversal can visit a pair twice.
4. Check a conservative full-material predicate (below), encode metadata and dispatch the field producer. An empty list writes zero. A proven full-material brick writes `sigma0*base_mask(y)` per voxel. Every other logical voxel iterates only its brick list (<=64), computes max(d_j-E_j), then the common ramp/base mask. No source voxel scans all1024 history nodes or all256 live nodes by default.
5. Build the complete fresh current field/mips/max products and its matching index generation before any view/light consumer reads them. Every brick is rewritten, so newly born, vanished, moved and newly eroded support cannot leave stale field values. Publish one complete generation; retire old index/node/field buffers only after their readers finish. Rebuilding the index each latched time avoids an unselected dirty-region algorithm.

**Exact full-material shortcut:** for brick B, let D_far=max_corner|corner-c_j| (the farthest AABB corner). Compute the minimum material-plane projection over B analytically by endpoint/sign selection; apply the monotone clamp to obtain delta_min(B). Because E decreases as delta increases,

`E_max(B) = A_j * smoothstep(t_stop+delta_min(B),t_stop+delta_min(B)+T_erase,t)`.

If any actual live candidate has `r_j-D_far-E_max(B) >= w_edge + 0.01m`, it alone proves d_j-E_j>=w throughout the brick, with numeric margin. The max union cannot lower it; constant sigma0 therefore makes the material factor exactly1 independent of every other candidate. Apply the base mask per voxel—this is not a claim the base feather is constant. The test includes current erosion, so it cannot accidentally certify a cleared parent. Do not infer “full” by checking only brick corners, by nominal parent radius, by several different spheres at different corners, or by ignoring E. A false-negative full test only costs work. The first profile still enforces the raw64-candidate cap even for full bricks; no hidden overflow bypass is introduced.

## Prepared-master alternative and actual evidence limits

The retained Q1 shape is an adapted WDAS density asset with its licence/provenance pinned, not a successful new live fluid simulation. `scripts/cloud_source_proof/authored.py` applies local birth-threshold and material-space erosion to that fixed detailed volume. The selected product is512m/128³ with a240s finite lifecycle and no local material velocity. Its reviewed local formation/breakup, irregular shape and coupled proof are useful evidence. It does **not** demonstrate new head roll, mature replacement, four gross variants, arbitrary scaling to2km, or a complete cloud family.
