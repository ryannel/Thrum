# Water

`./dev scene examples/water` cooks this example's grey calibration float and opens the sea under
the engine's sky. The game selects its own 60 Hz world, trade wind, longer swell and sea declaration.
The panel controls the Sun, camera height and bearing, and the sea's stage.

`./dev scene examples/water --web` serves the same scene and cooked float in Chrome with WebGPU.
The page pauses its world while hidden. If the graphics device is lost, **Recover graphics**
rebuilds its GPU resources while retaining the scene, controls and clock.
`node scripts/check_water_browser.mjs --case first-frame` compares the submitted sea fields
with the f64 model; `--case frames` also covers interpolation, repeats, visibility, a hitch
and device replacement. Run either under the orchestrate script's Chrome lock.
The `fixed` and `realtime` cases save submitted 2560×1440 images and source/time records;
their page callback intervals describe the page clock, not physical display cadence.
`THRUM_WATER_OUTPUT` selects a new scratch folder under `tmp/waters-M3/`.

`thrum-water` owns one contribution containing the ambient wave passes and the after-opaque surface.
It takes the level from the shared CPU map at each frame boundary and borrows all lighting and exposure
from the scene. It holds no example presets or cooked content. This first surface supports a sea;
other bodies, shore masks, interaction waves and full optics follow in their planned milestones.

The source for the calibration float and white texel is `make_float.py`; running it regenerates the
committed assets. The water example's original cellular foam rank texture is generated by
`python3 examples/water/make_foam_rank.py` and cooked as `textures/foam-rank`.

For submitted Metal evidence, build the proof driver before taking the GPU lock:

```sh
cargo test --release --locked --offline -p water-scene --test proof_gpu --no-run
```

Under the orchestrate script's `lock gpu`, run `python3 scripts/capture_water.py --capture-only --mode fixed --output
tmp/waters-M2/fixed` or its `--mode realtime` counterpart. Set `THRUM_ASSET_STORE` to this example's
cooked store to include the float. Fixed mode submits six declared views at an explicit synthetic
time. Then run the same command with `--package-only` outside the lock. Realtime mode pans the camera
using actual elapsed time and retains every admitted submitted image in a hashed, lossless spool,
with time records and an encoded review movie. Its bounded three-second capture has a 3 GiB pixel
reserve and a 6 GiB raw-spool allowance; these are observer resources, absent from ordinary frames.
Neither route
captures the desktop or proves physical display cadence.

The proof reads the scene variables below through the same parse as the window and the page, the
`settings` module, so it captures the scene `./dev scene` opens with the same variables. Its own
variables say where it writes and what it records: `THRUM_WATER_PROOF`, `_OUTPUT`, `_COMMIT`,
`_ROUTE` and `_RECIPE`. A realtime island capture chooses its camera index with
`THRUM_WATER_ISLAND_CAMERA` (0 by default).

For a portable Metal comparison, set `THRUM_RECIPE=portable THRUM_WATER_RECIPE=portable`
on the fixed capture.

`python3 scripts/water_cost.py record --scene first-frame`, also under the GPU lock, records the
paired capture-off run. `check` takes a fresh measurement and validates the committed record;
timings themselves are investigation tripwires rather than pass thresholds. The cost tool cooks
the calibration float and keeps it in both arms at identical world times, so only water differs.

## Optics

Run `THRUM_WATER_OPTICS=calm ./dev scene examples/water` for the sea and the submerged
calibration floats. Choose `trade` or `gale` to inspect how the glitter widens.
**Camera pitch** changes the view independently of the Sun elevation. Set
`THRUM_WATER_SHADE=1` to enable the stand-in cloud optical-depth field across the grey float.
Chrome accepts `?optics=calm&shade=1` for the same scene. Views 7–10 on the island show the lake
from 10 m and the three shores looking down. In a trade wind, **Lake wave gain** takes the lake
from flat to sheltered waves without replacing its wave field.

Water opts into a type's extinction, downwelling attenuation and reflection budget with
`thrum_water::Profile::optics`. The geometry producer can submit conservative world boxes with
`Water::optics_bodies()` before water records its upload for that view. The surface reads
radiance and distance only where the gather copied them. Registered boxes admit floating
geometry and the bed controls shore transmission. Still water can reflect visible scenery
within reach. When that source is unavailable, the reflected path uses the lent sky.

## Clouds and fog

Clouds are off by default. Select fair or broken cloud over the open sea, a sea
fog bank coming ashore, or dawn mist over the island lake.

```sh
THRUM_WATER_CLOUDS=broken THRUM_WATER_CLOUD_SUN=low ./dev scene examples/water
```

In Chrome, use `?clouds=broken&cloud-sun=low` on the scene page. Both hosts use
the same startup settings.

| Native variable | Chrome query | Values |
|---|---|---|
| `THRUM_WATER_CLOUDS` | `clouds` | `off`, `fair`, `broken`, `sea-fog`, `dawn-mist` |
| `THRUM_WATER_CLOUD_SUN` | `cloud-sun` | `noon`, `low` (10°) |
| `THRUM_WATER_CLOUD_HEIGHT` | `cloud-height` | `2`, `40` metres in the open-sea and inside-the-bank views |
| `THRUM_WATER_CLOUD_VIEW` | `cloud-view` | `craft` follows the moving craft on the open sea |
| `THRUM_WATER_WEATHER_SPEED` | `weather-speed` | `1`, `8` times ordinary weather time |
| `THRUM_WATER_BANK_VIEW` | `bank-view` | `offshore`, `front`, `inside`, `shore` |
| `THRUM_WATER_MIST_TIME` | `mist-time` | `dawn`, `after` (one hour after sunrise) |
| `THRUM_WATER_CLOUD_CONTROL=1` | `cloud-control=off` | Keep the preset camera and Sun for a weather-absent comparison |

An active cloud preset selects its own view and takes precedence over the
labelled stand-in shade. The accelerated weather clock leaves the swell and the
craft on the scene's ordinary clock.

## Island

Run the island on Metal with `THRUM_WATER_ISLAND=trade ./dev scene examples/water`.
Use `calm` for inspecting still edges or `gale` for the larger swash. In Chrome
run `./dev scene examples/water --web` and append `?island=trade` (or the other
wind name) to the printed scene URL. The same example without a wind selection
opens the retained open-sea scene.

The camera control selects the beach, lake, pond, 40 m overlook, top-down map,
or lake sill, in that order. Move **Lake stage** from 0 to 1 m and back in the
sill view to flood and drain the second hollow. The island bed is rasterized
from the same triangles the cooker draws. Regenerate those triangles from
`python3 examples/water/make_island.py`.

## Rain yard

Run `THRUM_WATER_RAIN=calm ./dev scene examples/water` on Metal or add
`?rain=calm` to the web scene URL. Raise **Rain time scale** to fill the
paving puddles, then set **Rain (mm/h)** to zero to hold their levels and
let the rain rings fade. **Yard view** 8 shows both puddles from eye height.
Use **Walk through the puddles** to send a marker across the paving, or
drop a stone into either hollow. Return a control to zero to use it again.

## Floating bodies

Run `THRUM_WATER_BODIES=gale ./dev scene examples/water` for the craft and eight buoys. Use
`calm`, `trade` or `gale` and optionally set `THRUM_WATER_SPEED=5` or `15`,
`THRUM_WATER_HEADING=beam`, `THRUM_WATER_VIEW=chase`, or `THRUM_WATER_LOCATION=lake`.
In Chrome the corresponding URL options are `?bodies=gale&speed=5&heading=beam&view=chase`.
At zero speed the launch is held horizontally so the waterline shows pitch and roll in place.
The panel controls speed, camera and Sun and shows refused requests, late points, unavailable
uses and blocking reads.

The game publishes six columns per body at each fixed tick. The water contribution uses the
world map, analytic long waves and the existing cascade mips, then copies a single dispatch's
results into a nonblocking staging ring. The game drains the mailbox at the start of its tick,
uses the long-wave CPU model as a fallback and retains the hull forces and rigid-body state.
Run `node scripts/check_water_browser.mjs --case bodies` under the Chrome lock for the hardware
WebGPU check and reply-age counters.
The wake route uses the same floating bodies and adds their water reactions to the interaction step. Run the calm-sea overhead view with

```sh
THRUM_WATER_WAKE=calm THRUM_WATER_SPEED=3 THRUM_WATER_VIEW=overhead ./dev scene examples/water
```

Use `THRUM_WATER_LOCATION=lake`, `THRUM_WATER_STONE=1`, and `THRUM_WATER_VIEW=shore` for the lake impact. The craft speed and course can be changed while the scene runs. In the web scene the same options are `?wake=calm&speed=3&view=overhead` and `?wake=calm&location=lake&stone=1&view=shore`.

## Whitewater

`THRUM_WATER_FOAM=gale THRUM_WATER_VIEW=vista ./dev scene examples/water` opens the sea from
40 m. Choose `waterline` for 2 m, or `chase` and `overhead` to see the craft's trail.
`THRUM_WATER_SPEED=6` sets its initial speed in metres per second. Use **Craft present** to remove
the craft and watch the water carry its remaining trail. Choose `THRUM_WATER_LOCATION=beach`
for the island's sand, aerial and lake views. The Chrome options use the same names, for
example `?foam=gale&location=beach`. The browser proof is `node scripts/check_water_browser.mjs --case foam`.

## Underwater inspection

Use `THRUM_WATER_DIVE=calm ./dev scene examples/water` on the Mac or
`?dive=calm` in the browser for the island lake. Add
`THRUM_WATER_LOCATION=sea` on the Mac or `&location=sea` in the browser for
the offshore shelf. The dive routes expose camera depth, pitch, bearing and Sun elevation.
Choose `breeze` for a 5 m/s local wind without swell or `trade` for wind and swell.
The offshore route lifts the terrain and water levels 16 m so the sky camera
can reach 10 m underwater within its supported altitude range.
Choose `deep-sea` as the location for the 20 m offshore inspection or `pond` to look down
through the sheltered shallows.
Choose `THRUM_WATER_LOCATION=swash` or `&location=swash` to cross the
waterline on the beach. This view uses the same raised datum and the island
breaker section.
Choose `lake-high-stage` as the location to inspect the island lake from 5 m
under its surface. This route raises the lake's level by 2.75 m.

For a submitted `realtime` capture, set `THRUM_WATER_ROUTE` to
`dive-up-1m`, `dive-inclined-1m`, `dive-up-5m`, or `dive-inclined-5m` to hold
that view while the waves move. Use a deep site for the 5 m views. The overhead
presets use 89.99° within the sky camera's pitch range. At either lake stage the
overhead view widens to 154° vertically so the refraction can sample the bank.
Use `dive-hold` to keep the camera at mean water level or leave the route unset
to lower and raise it.

For WP16 lighting captures, set `THRUM_WATER_ROUTE=under-light` in fixed mode.
A realtime take can hold a depth and pitch using `THRUM_WATER_DIVE_HOLD=3,67`
or descend and return with `THRUM_WATER_DIVE_TRAVEL=20` at the deep sea site.
Use bearing 228° to face the Sun. At 60° air elevation aim near 67° pitch and
at 15° aim near 43° pitch to follow the refracted beam.

## Lake outlet

Run `THRUM_WATER_CHANNEL=trade ./dev scene examples/water` on the Mac, or open
`?channel=trade` on the hosted WebGPU scene. The camera selector steps from
the source through the narrows to the mouth and overlook. Lake stage spans
0 to +1 m and tide spans −0.5 to +0.5 m. The float starts just inside the
source join. The chart and cut terrain are hand-authored stand-ins for the
RIVERS solver. Regenerate the terrain with
`python3 examples/water/make_island.py --channel`.

### Chart writer contract

A `Chart` uses the water map's bed lattice. `Bed::lattice()` gives world
`[x, z]` of the first centre in metres, the uniform centre-to-centre spacing
in metres, and the sample counts `[nx, nz]`. Address every chart array at
`iz * nx + ix`. A solver product may store a rectangular subset whose first
centre aligns with the bed lattice. `RiverReach::chart` expands it onto
the bed using that offset and resolves named ends to body indices.

At each centre, write absolute water level in metres above the same datum
as the bed, and horizontal current `[u_x, u_z]` in metres per second.
Positive current points toward increasing world x or z. Set the footprint
for centres belonging to the chart, including its declared end segments.
The map interpolates its rows, adds the chart's stage in metres, computes
depth as water level minus bed, and mixes toward the joined body's row at
each end. The draw and queries use the same rows for surface level, wave
support, and currents. Surface detail reads upstream through the current.
A steady parallel reach with speed uniform across each section gets a
prepared travel coordinate. It carries waves at the local speed without
stretching their pattern farther as time passes. The joined current
blends that pattern into the receiving waves. Foam stock moves downstream,
and games receive the current in body answers.

In the solver's energy account, a decrease in hydraulic head downstream is a
positive head loss, measured in metres. Dividing by the along-flow distance
gives the loss gradient in metres per metre. The prepared chart stores the
resulting surface-breaking power in watts per square metre, in
`RiverReach::loss` and `FlowSample::loss`. The RIVERS design gives the
discharge and friction terms for that conversion. Write zero current and
loss at dry centres. The model interpolates loss with level and current and
mixes it through the ends. The RIVERS foam reader uses the resulting positive
power to set the birth rate of foam and submerged air. The example channel's
shore foam uses lost ambient wave power from the depth limit. The hand-authored `Chart` supplies
level, footprint, currents and ends. The complete solver product also
provides flow-band gains, wetting gauges and fall records where applicable.

A plain chart stage raises its own prepared level by the given offset and
updates the wet support. Its current, footprint, gains, bed and end segments
remain as prepared. Its joined rows still follow the separate stages of
their receiving or source bodies, including the sea's reach at the mouth.
For a sampled river, the gauge selects and mixes the prepared flow samples
and their wetting thresholds. The sample layout and gauge rules are in the
[RIVERS design](../../docs/plans/RIVERS/DESIGN.md).

### Solved rivers

Run `THRUM_WATER_RIVER=trade ./dev scene examples/water` to see the spring stream and the
lake's solved outlet. Use `calm` to see current-driven chop and chart-loss foam in the
spring stream. The river view control visits the riffle, pool, sill, narrows, joins and overlooks. The spring, lake and tide controls set their stages.
The floats start at the spring and just below the lake outlet.

The Mac prepares the two keyed river files beside the cooked store and prints a line for each cold
solve. An unchanged key reuses its file. The web build packages those keyed files into its WebAssembly
bundle. Open its printed URL with `?river=trade`.
